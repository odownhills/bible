1 John 5

What the chapter means, section by section. Read the chapter

In this last chapter "testimony" and "testify" come ten times (verses 6-11). The witnesses "testify" in 1:2; here God "has testified concerning his Son" (verse 9). The letter ends without a farewell, as it began without a greeting, but verse 20 returns to its opening words: "his Son, Jesus Christ" (1:3) and "eternal life" (1:2).

Conquering the worldverses 1-5

Earlier the letter names who "has been born" by what is done: righteousness (2:29), love (4:7). Verse 1 names them by what they believe, and joins believing to love, the two parts of the one commandment in 3:23. In 4:20 loving God was tested by loving the brother; verse 2 runs the test the other way.

The believer of verse 1 holds what the liar of 2:22 denies, "that Jesus is the Christ," and verse 5 asks in the form of 2:22. The young men "have conquered" the evil one (2:13-14), the little children "them" (4:4); here, the world.

Related Scripture

Deuteronomy 30:11-14

11For this commandment that I am commanding you today is not too hard for you, and it is not far away. 12It is not in the heavens, for you to say, "Who will go up for us to the heavens and take it for us and make us hear it, so that we may do it?" 13And it is not beyond the sea, for you to say, "Who will cross over for us to the other side of the sea and take it for us and make us hear it, so that we may do it?" 14But the word is very near you, in your mouth and in your heart, to do it.

The commandment is not too hard and not far away, but near, "to do it." Verse 3: his commandments "are not burdensome."

John 14:15

If you love me, you will keep my commandments.

Loving him goes with keeping his commandments; in verses 2-3, loving God does.

The testimony of Godverses 6-12

The letter has named blood once before, "the blood of Jesus his Son" (1:7), and water never. Verse 6 holds to both, "not in the water only," but does not say what they point to. In Greek "the one that testifies" (verse 6) is neuter, like "spirit"; "three who testify" (verse 7) is masculine, though spirit, water and blood are neuter nouns. Read "at one," the three agree; read "are one," they are united.

Verse 11 says what God's testimony is: that he "gave us eternal life." Not believing God "has made him a liar," as saying "we have not sinned" does in 1:10.

Related Scripture

Deuteronomy 19:15

A single witness must not rise against a man for any guilt or for any sin, in any sin that he sins. On the word of two witnesses, or on the word of three witnesses, a matter must stand.

No matter stands on one witness. Verses 7-8 name three who testify, and they are "at one."

John 19:34-35

34But one of the soldiers pierced his side with a spear, and immediately blood and water came out. 35And the one who has seen has testified, and his testimony is true, and that one knows that he is saying what is true, so that you also may believe.

Blood and water come from Jesus' side, and one who saw "has testified," the Greek verb and tense of verses 9-10. Verse 6 does not say whether it has this in view.

Boldness in askingverses 13-17

Verse 13 is the letter's last word about its own writing.

In 4:6 the one who knows God "hears us"; in verse 14 the one we ask "hears us," the same two Greek words. "Ask" and "requests" share one Greek root; for sin toward death the verb changes to "make a request." The verse does not name who "will give him life," and the letter does not say what sin toward death is.

Related Scripture

John 20:30-31

30So Jesus did many other signs also in the sight of his disciples, which are not written in this book. 31But these are written so that you may believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God, and so that, believing, you may have life in his name.

The signs are written so that readers "may believe" and have life; verse 13, so that believers "may know" they have it. "The Christ, the Son of God" joins what is believed in verses 1 and 5.

1 Samuel 12:19

And all the people said to Samuel, "Pray for your servants to Yahweh your God, so that we do not die, because we have added to all our sins an evil, in asking for a king for ourselves."

People who have sinned ask for prayer "so that we do not die," and Samuel calls it a sin to stop praying for them (1 Samuel 12:23). Verse 16 joins asking, sin and life.

What we knowverses 18-21

Verse 18 has the words of 3:9, "everyone who has been born from God," with "does not sin" from 3:6. Two verses earlier a brother is seen "sinning" (verse 16). The letter has said, "If we say that we have no sin, we lead ourselves astray" (1:8), and "if anyone sins, we have an advocate" (2:1).

Elsewhere the letter says "has been born"; here, once, "the one who was born," and the verse does not name him. Read "keeps him," there are two, the keeper and the kept; read "keeps himself," one. Read "in the evil one" (as in verse 18), a person; read "in evil," a condition.

"This" in verse 20 follows both "the true one" and "Jesus Christ"; the Greek allows either, and the verse does not say which.

Related Scripture

John 17:3

And this is the eternal life: that they should know you, the only true God, and the one whom you sent, Jesus Christ.

"Eternal life," "know," "true God," "Jesus Christ": verse 20 has them all.

Jeremiah 10:10

But Yahweh is the true God; he is the living God and the everlasting king. At his rage the earth quakes, and the nations cannot bear his indignation.

Against the idols of the verses before it, Yahweh is the true and living God. Verse 20 ends "the true God and eternal life"; verse 21, idols.
