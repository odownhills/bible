## 17:5 So when the promise of the first of the two comes, we will raise up against you | So when the promise of the first of the two came, we raised up against you
kind: grammar
call: ask
why: This one choice runs through verses 5 to 7: whether they are still the words of the decree, telling what is to come, or the telling of what then happened. The verse before gives the decree as words spoken to the Children of Israel about what is to come, "You will certainly cause corruption on the earth twice" (17:4), and this verse goes straight on with "So", still speaking to "you", with nothing to mark that those words have ended. Arabic has one word for "when" that looks ahead and another that looks back, and this verse has the first. Where the Quran says "So when the promise comes" elsewhere, it is of what is still to come: "So when the promise of my Lord comes, he will make it level. And the promise of my Lord is true" (18:98); and later in this surah the same words are spoken to the Children of Israel again, with "we will bring you all together" after them (17:104). So also what Noah is told beforehand: "So when our command comes" (23:27). Where the Quran tells of such a moment as past, it uses the other word: "So when our command came, we saved Salih" (11:66); "So when our command came, we made its highest part its lowest" (11:82). And the Quran says elsewhere that this was announced beforehand, with the same verb: "your Lord announced that he would certainly raise up against them, until the Day of Resurrection, those who would inflict a terrible punishment on them" (7:167).
other: The verbs of verses 5 and 6 are past in form ("we raised up", "they went", "we gave back", "we supplied", "we made"), and the "when" of this verse does sometimes tell of what has already happened: "And when they saw trade or amusement, they scattered toward it and left you standing" (62:11); "So when good came to them, they said, 'This is ours'" (7:131). The Quran tells the Children of Israel their own past in just this way, as "you": "And when we saved you from Pharaoh's people" (2:49). Verse 7 speaks of the first entry as a thing done, "as they entered it the first time", and verse 8 speaks to people who have such times behind them: "if you go back, we will go back". The part itself goes on to warn by what has happened: "how many generations we have destroyed after Noah" (17:17). Read that way, the decree's own words end with verse 4, and verses 5 to 7 tell how it came about: "we raised up against you servants of ours who were fierce in battle, and they went back and forth among the homes. And it was a promise carried out. Then we gave you back the turn against them ... So when the promise of the last one came ...".
review: open

## 17:8 a prison | a mat
kind: two senses
call: clear
why: The word comes from the verb for hemming in and holding back, and that is the only way the Quran uses this family of words: "take them and confine them" (9:5); "if you are held back" (2:196); "the poor who are held back in the way of God" (2:273). And it says of Hell what a prison does: "Hell surrounds the disbelievers" (9:49; 29:54); "It is closed over them" (104:8).
other: The same word is the everyday word for a mat, a thing woven and spread out to sit or lie on. The Quran also speaks of Hell as what is spread under those in it: "They have a resting place of Hell, and over them coverings" (7:41); "So Hell is enough for him. And miserable is the resting place" (2:206); "They have canopies of fire above them, and canopies beneath them" (39:16).

## 17:16 we command those in it who live in luxury | we multiply those in it who live in luxury
kind: readings differ
call: clear
why: The main text follows the Hafs reading, "we command", which is what this verb means everywhere in the Quran. What the town does next is the word the Quran uses for going against a command: Iblis "defied the command of his Lord" (18:50). The verse before says how the command reaches a town: "we would not punish until we had raised up a messenger" (17:15); so also "your Lord would not destroy the towns until he had raised up a messenger in their mother town, reciting our signs to them" (28:59). And those who live in luxury are the ones who answer him: "We did not send a warner to any town but those in it who lived in luxury said, 'We disbelieve in what you have been sent with'" (34:34; so 43:23). What is commanded is not named, and it cannot be the defiance itself: "God does not command indecency" (7:28).
other: Another of the old established readings lengthens the first vowel of the verb, and then it says "we make many"; the verb as Hafs reads it is recorded with that sense as well. The Quran does speak of plenty given to a people before they are taken: "we opened to them the gates of everything, until, when they rejoiced in what they had been given, we took them suddenly" (6:44); "Then we put good in place of evil, until they grew plentiful ... so we took them suddenly" (7:95); "Do they suppose that with the wealth and sons we supply them with we are hurrying good things to them? Rather, they do not realize" (23:55-56).
review: open
