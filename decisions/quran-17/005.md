## 17:92 face to face with us | as guarantors
kind: two senses
call: close
why: The demand opens with the very words of an older one, "We will not believe you until we see God in plain sight" (2:55), and the next verse here asks for what was asked for beside it: "The People of the Book ask you to send down to them a book from the sky. They asked Moses for something greater than that: they said, 'Show us God in plain sight'" (4:153). So also "Why are the angels not sent down to us, or why do we not see our Lord?" (25:21), and, with the verb of this verse, "Why do you not bring us the angels, if you are telling the truth?" (15:7). In each of them the thing asked for is to see. Everything else on this list is a thing to be seen: a spring, a garden, the sky falling, a house of gold, a climb into the sky. The Arabic word is from the root for facing, and a closely related form is used of what comes before the eyes: "or the punishment comes to them face to face" (18:55; so 6:111). The same word is also the Quran's word for a tribe (7:27). Taken that way, they ask for God and the angels to be brought in a body, as in "your Lord comes, and the angels, rank upon rank" (89:22), which is still a demand to see them come.
other: The word can also mean one who stands surety for another. Read that way, they ask for God and the angels to be brought to vouch for what he says. The Quran tells of that demand too: "Why has an angel not been sent down to him, to be a warner with him?" (25:7); "or an angel come with him" (11:12); "or the angels come with him in company" (43:53). And the answer that follows a few verses on speaks to it: "God is enough as a witness between me and you" (17:96), the same answer that is given to "You are not one sent" (13:43). So also "God bears witness to what he has sent down to you ... and the angels bear witness. And God is enough as a witness" (4:166).
review: open

## 17:102 You know well | I know well
kind: readings differ
call: clear
why: The main text follows the Hafs reading, in which Moses says this of Pharaoh. The Quran says the same of Pharaoh and his people: "So when our signs came to them, giving sight, they said, 'This is clear magic.' And they denied them, though their souls were certain of them" (27:13-14); "we showed him our signs, all of them, but he called them lies and refused" (20:56). And it turns Pharaoh's words back on him: he has just said "I do think, Moses, that you are bewitched" (17:101) to a man whose signs he knows the source of.
other: Another of the old established readings has "I" here. Then Moses speaks of his own knowledge, in answer to the charge that he is bewitched. The Quran has him speak to Pharaoh in that way too: "I am a messenger from the Lord of the worlds, bound to say nothing about God but the truth. I have come to you with a clear proof from your Lord" (7:104-105); "My Lord knows best who has come with guidance from him" (28:37).
review: open

## 17:104 the promise of the hereafter | the promise of the last one
kind: two senses
call: ask
why: The Arabic word is the one this surah uses for the hereafter everywhere else (17:10, 19, 21, 45, 72). At 17:7 it means "the last one" because "the first of the two" has just been named (17:5); here nothing has been counted. What comes with it is the Quran's wording for the gathering on that Day: "we will bring you", as in "when we bring a witness from every community" (4:41) and "Wherever you are, God will bring you all together" (2:148). The verses just before are about that Day: "we will gather them on the Day of Resurrection" (17:97); "a term in which there is no doubt" (17:99). And where the Quran tells the same story elsewhere, it ends in the same place: Pharaoh is drowned, the Children of Israel are settled, and then "Your Lord will decide between them on the Day of Resurrection" (10:90-93; so 45:16-17).
other: These are word for word the words of 17:7, "So when the promise of the last one comes", which are also said to the Children of Israel, about the second of the two times decreed to them "in the Book" (17:4), the Book given to Moses (17:2). This verse tells what was said to them in the time of Moses. The end of the surah takes up its beginning in other ways as well: "a promise that will be carried out" (17:5) comes back as "The promise of our Lord is indeed carried out" (17:108). Read that way, they are told to live in the land until the last of the two times comes, and for that time they are brought together as a mixed crowd; the Quran says elsewhere that they were scattered, "we cut them up on the earth into communities" (7:168).
review: open

## 17:106 divided | made distinct
kind: two senses
call: clear
why: The verse says what it was done for, "so that you may read it to the people slowly", and it ends, "and we have sent it down in stages". The Quran answers the same point elsewhere: "Those who disbelieve say, 'Why was the Quran not sent down to him all at once?' That is how it is, so that we may make your heart firm with it" (25:32). The verb is the one for parting a thing: "when we parted the sea for you" (2:50).
other: The same verb is used of setting a thing apart so that it is clear and settled: "In it every wise matter is made distinct" (44:4); "so separate us from the defiant people" (5:25). And the Quran is called by a word of this family, "the Criterion" (25:1; so 2:185; 3:4). Read that way, the verse says that the Quran was made distinct and clear so that it could be read to the people.
review: open
