## 2:191 And do not fight them at the Sacred Place of Worship until they fight you there. But if they fight you, then kill them | And do not kill them at the Sacred Place of Worship until they kill some of you there. But if they kill some of you, then kill them
kind: readings differ
call: clear
why: The main text always follows the Hafs reading, and it has "fight" in these three places. That is the passage's own word on both sides of this verse: "fight in the way of God those who fight you" (2:190), "And fight them until there is no persecution" (2:193). When the Quran comes back to what may be done in a sacred time and at this place, the question it answers is about fighting: "They ask you about the sacred month, about fighting in it" (2:217). And elsewhere fighting is what answers fighting: "fight those who give God partners all together, as they fight you all together" (9:36); "Permission is given to those who are fought, because they were wronged" (22:39).
other: Another established reading of the same letters has "kill" in all three places. Then every verb in the verse is the same one, since it opens "And kill them wherever you come upon them" and this sentence ends "then kill them". That is a life for a life, which the passage goes on to state: "for sacred things there is retaliation. So whoever oversteps against you, overstep against him in the same way he overstepped against you" (2:194), as it said of those who are killed (2:178). So also "take them and kill them wherever you come upon them" (4:91). In this reading "you" is the whole people, some of whom are killed, as in "killing your own" (2:85).

## 2:197 and no dispute in the pilgrimage | and there is no dispute about the pilgrimage
kind: grammar
call: clear
why: The question is whether "in the pilgrimage" closes all three things or goes only with the last. The three stand in a row in the same form, each with its own "no", and the sentence is about a person: "whoever undertakes the pilgrimage in them". What follows is still about what he does: "whatever good you do, God knows it". The same two Arabic words mean "during the pilgrimage" in the verse before: "a fast of three days in the pilgrimage" (2:196). And after the word for disputing, the small word can mark when it is done: "you have disputed on their behalf in the life of this world" (4:109).
other: Almost everywhere else that the Quran sets this small word after the word for disputing, it names the thing disputed about: "Do you dispute with me about names that you have named?" (7:71), "disputing with us about the people of Lot" (11:74), "They dispute with you about the truth" (8:6), "who disputes about God without knowledge" (22:3, 8; 31:20; so also 13:13), "dispute about the signs of God" (40:4, 35, 56, 69; so also 42:35; 58:1). Read that way, the first two things are what the pilgrim keeps from, and the third says that the pilgrimage itself is settled. The verse opens with when it is, "The pilgrimage is in known months"; the new moons are "appointed times for the people and for the pilgrimage" (2:189); of rites it is said, "let them not contend with you about the matter" (22:67), with "And if they dispute with you" straight after it (22:68); and there were people who made a sacred month permitted one year and forbidden another (9:37).
review: open
