## 2:222 It is a discomfort | It is something unclean
kind: two senses
call: clear
why: Everywhere else the Quran uses this word, it is something that hurts or troubles a person, and never a name for what is unclean. It is said of the body twice more, both times beside sickness: "whoever among you is sick or has an ailment of the head" (2:196), and fighters may lay down their weapons "if you have discomfort from rain or are sick" (4:102). It is the small hurt that falls short of real harm: "They will not harm you, except for some hurt" (3:111). And it is the hurt done to someone by words or deeds: "those who hurt God and his messenger" (33:57; so also 33:58), and the hurt that must not follow charity (2:262-264). Where the Quran calls a thing unclean it uses other words: the flesh of pigs "is filth" (6:145; so also 5:90), and "those who give God partners are unclean" (9:28).
other: The Arabic word was also used for a thing that is dirty or offensive. And the verse goes straight on in words of cleanness: "do not go near them until they are pure. Then when they have purified themselves, come to them", and it ends, "he loves those who purify themselves." Read that way, the first words name what they are to be pure of. The Quran speaks of other states of the body in the same words: before prayer the command is "purify yourselves", and one who has touched women and finds no water uses clean earth, because God "wants to purify you" (5:6).
review: open

## 2:223 however you wish | whenever you wish
kind: two senses
call: clear
why: Everywhere else in the Quran this Arabic word is about the way a thing comes about or where it comes from, never about its time. It is "how" twice more in this surah, "How can he have the kingdom over us?" (2:247) and "How will God give life to this after its death?" (2:259), and when Zechariah and Mary ask how they can have a child (3:40, 47; 19:8, 20; so also 6:101). It is "from where" when Zechariah finds food with Mary, "Mary, where did you get this from?" (3:37; so also 3:165). The verse before has just spoken of the same thing in that way: "come to them from where God has commanded you" (2:222). And the picture here is a field, which a man comes to by whatever way he likes.
other: The same word was also used for "when". The verse before is about a time: "do not go near them until they are pure. Then when they have purified themselves, come to them" (2:222). So this verse can be saying that outside that time, any time is open. The Quran has just dealt with times for the same thing: "It is permitted to you, on the night of the fast, to be intimate with your wives", and "So now lie with them" (2:187).
review: open

## 2:224 And do not make God, by your oaths, an obstacle to being virtuous, being mindful of him and setting things right between people | And do not make God a target for your oaths, so that you may be virtuous, be mindful of him and set things right between people
kind: two senses
call: close
why: The Arabic word, which comes only here in the Quran, can mean a thing set across the way. The verses that follow are about oaths that stand in the way of what is right: God does not take people to task for idle talk in their oaths (2:225), and a man who swears off his wife is given four months, and "if they come back, God is forgiving, merciful" (2:226). "Setting things right" is this passage's own word for a husband and wife coming back together (2:228). The Quran has another sentence of the same shape, an oath and then the good thing it would stop, with a verb of the same family as "swear off": "Let not those of you who have bounty and plenty swear off giving to relatives, the destitute and those who have emigrated in the way of God" (24:22). And when the prophet forbade himself what God had permitted to him, the answer was, "God has laid down for you the release of your oaths" (66:1-2; so also 5:89, which sets out how an oath is made up for).
other: The same word can mean a thing set up in front of people and always in use, like a mark that is shot at. Read that way, the verse tells them not to keep swearing by God, and being virtuous, being mindful of him and setting things right are what will come of holding back. The Quran says "watch over your oaths" (5:89) and "do not obey any contemptible swearer of oaths" (68:10). It warns against breaking oaths "when you have made God a surety over you" (16:91), and against taking oaths "as a means of deceit among yourselves" (16:94) or "as a cover" (58:16).
review: open

## 2:228 three periods | three clean times between periods
kind: two senses
call: close
why: The Arabic word comes only here in the Quran. It names a time that comes round, and it was used both for a woman's monthly bleeding and for the clean time between two bleedings. The verse goes straight on to the womb, "it is not permitted to them to conceal what God has created in their wombs", and the bleeding is what shows whether there is a child. Where the Quran sets the waiting for women who have no bleeding to count, it puts months in its place: "those of your women who have despaired of menstruation, if you are in doubt, their waiting time is three months, and those who have not menstruated" (65:4). So what is counted is the bleeding.
other: The Quran's own word for the bleeding is another word, used six verses earlier, "they ask you about menstruation" (2:222), and again where the waiting is set by it (65:4). Here it uses a different word, which may be there to name a different thing. And a man is told, "when you divorce women, divorce them for their waiting time, and count the waiting time" (65:1): the count begins with the divorce, and a clean time she is already in can be counted from that day, while a bleeding has yet to come.
review: open
