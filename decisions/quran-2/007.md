## 2:165 And if only those who do wrong could see, when they see the punishment | And if only you could see those who do wrong when they see the punishment
kind: readings differ
call: clear
why: The main text always follows the Hafs reading, in which the ones who see are those who do wrong. The Quran speaks of such people in the same shape elsewhere, an "if only" with nothing after it to complete it: "If only those who disbelieve knew the time when they will not keep the Fire from their faces or from their backs" (21:39). And the passage goes on with what they are made to see: "they see the punishment" (2:166), "That is how God will show them their deeds, as regrets for them" (2:167).
other: Another established reading of the same letters makes it "you see", said to the one who is listening. That is the Quran's usual way of setting such a scene before him, each time with nothing after it to complete it: "If only you could see when they are made to stand before the Fire" (6:27; so also 6:30, 93; 8:50; 32:12; 34:31, 51).
review: open

## 2:173 neither transgressing nor overstepping | neither desiring it nor overstepping
kind: two senses
call: clear
why: The Arabic word can mean seeking a thing or going beyond what is right. Where the Quran uses its verb for seeking, it names the thing sought: "Do they seek other than the religion of God?" (3:83), "Should I seek a lord other than God?" (6:164). Where the verb stands with nothing after it, as here, it is going too far: "they would transgress on the earth" (42:27), and the two seas "do not transgress" (55:20). It stands beside the word for overstepping once more, where Pharaoh and his forces follow the Children of Israel "in transgression and aggression" (10:90), and beside sin among the things God has forbidden (7:33; so also 16:90). And where these same words come again about these same foods, the very next verse uses this word for a wrong done: "that is how we repaid them for their transgression" (6:145-146).
other: Seeking is the commoner sense of the verb in the Quran (3:83; 6:164; 12:65; 18:64), and the verse is about food: a person who is forced to eat it is the opposite of one who wants it. The same allowance is worded elsewhere as "whoever is forced by hunger, not leaning toward sin" (5:3), which speaks of what a person is drawn to.
review: open

## 2:175 So how patient they are with the Fire! | So what has made them patient with the Fire?
kind: grammar
call: clear
why: The Arabic words have the shape of a cry of wonder, and a cry of wonder closes what has just been said of them, that they bought punishment in exchange for forgiveness, as "Miserable is what they sold themselves for" follows the same kind of trade at 2:90. Nobody is spoken to here. Where the Quran asks people what brought them to the Fire, it asks them to their faces and they answer: "What brought you into Saqar?" They said, "We were not among those who prayed" (74:42-43). The Quran speaks of patience in the Fire in the same bitter way elsewhere: "Burn in it; be patient or do not be patient, it is the same for you" (52:16); "if they are patient, the Fire is their home" (41:24). And patience is this passage's own word (2:153, 155, 177).
other: The same words can be a question, "what" followed by a verb, as earlier in this surah, "What has turned them from the direction they used to face?" (2:142), and in "Man, what has deceived you about your generous Lord?" (82:6). Read so, it asks what could have made them bold enough to face the Fire.
review: open

## 2:177 though he loves it | out of love for him
kind: grammar
call: clear
why: The question is what the small Arabic word for "it" or "him" points back to, and the nearest word is "wealth". The Quran ties virtue to this elsewhere, with the same word for virtue: "You will not reach virtue until you spend from what you love" (3:92). It says that people do love wealth, "you love wealth with a great love" (89:20; so also 100:8), and it praises those who give at a cost to themselves: "they put others before themselves, even if they are in need" (59:9).
other: The same words can be "out of love for him", for God, who is named first in this verse, and the passage has just said, "those who believe are stronger in love for God" (2:165). Where the same words are said of giving food, the givers go straight on, "We feed you only for the face of God" (76:8-9). And the Quran speaks of spending in that way: "you spend only seeking the face of God" (2:272; so also 2:265; 92:20).
review: open
