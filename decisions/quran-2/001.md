## 2:2 There is no doubt in it; it is guidance | There is no doubt. In it is guidance
kind: grammar
call: clear
why: The question is where the pause falls, after "in it" or before it. Everywhere else the Quran says "no doubt" it goes straight on to "in it", thirteen times, and twice of the Book in these very words: "the sending down of the Book, there is no doubt in it, from the Lord of the worlds" (32:2; 10:37); so also of the Day and the Hour (3:9, 25; 4:87; 6:12; 17:99; 18:21; 22:7; 40:59; 42:7; 45:26, 32). And the Book is itself called guidance, with no "in it". Surah 31 opens with the same three letters and goes on, "Those are the signs of the wise Book, guidance and mercy for those who do good" (31:1-3), and its next two verses are almost word for word 2:3-5. So also "guidance and good news for the believers" (2:97; 27:2), "guidance for the people" (2:185), "guidance and a lesson for those who are mindful of God" (3:138).
other: "In it is guidance" is how the Quran speaks of the Torah and the Gospel: "We sent down the Torah; in it is guidance and light" (5:44); "we gave him the Gospel; in it is guidance and light" (5:46). Read this way "no doubt" stands alone and says that this is the Book, beyond doubt.
review: open

## 2:3 believe in the unseen | believe while unseen
kind: grammar
call: clear
why: "Believe in" followed by what is believed is how the passage itself goes on with the same verb: "those who believe in what has been sent down to you" (2:4), "We believe in God and in the Last Day" (2:8). So verses 3 and 4 list what is believed: the unseen, what was sent down, the hereafter. And the unseen is a thing in the Quran, known to God: "I know the unseen of the heavens and the earth" (2:33); "with him are the keys of the unseen" (6:59).
other: In nine other places these same two words do not name a thing but tell how something is done, unseen: "those who fear their Lord unseen" (21:49; 35:18; 67:12; so also 36:11; 50:33; 5:94), "who helps him and his messengers unseen" (57:25), "I did not betray him unseen" (12:52), "promised his servants unseen" (19:61). And this passage goes straight on to people who say "We believe" when they meet the believers and something else when they are alone (2:8, 14).
review: open

## 2:10 because they used to lie | because they used to call it a lie
kind: readings differ
call: clear
why: The main text always follows the Hafs reading, and it reads "they lie". That is what the passage has just shown: they say "We believe in God and in the Last Day" but they are not believers (2:8), and they say one thing to the believers and another to their devils (2:14). The same closing words are said elsewhere of hypocrisy put in hearts "because they used to lie" (9:77), and "God bears witness that the hypocrites are liars" (63:1).
other: Another established reading of the same letters doubles the middle letter of the verb, which makes it "they call it a lie". That is the Quran's word for refusing what God sends: "those who disbelieve and call our signs lies, those are the companions of the Fire" (2:39), and at the punishment itself, "This is what you used to call a lie" (83:17; so also 32:20; 52:14).
review: open

## 2:23 a surah like it | a surah from someone like him
kind: grammar
call: clear
why: The question is what "the like of it" or "the like of him" points back to: what was sent down, or the servant. The doubt in this verse is "about what we have sent down", so the thing in doubt is the thing to be matched. And the other challenges say so with no "from": "bring a surah like it" (10:38), "bring ten surahs like it" (11:13), "let them bring a discourse like it" (52:34), "they would not bring the like of it" (17:88).
other: Only here does the challenge say "from" before "the like of", and the nearest word it can point back to is "our servant": a surah from a man like him. The Quran makes that point about him elsewhere: "You did not recite any book before it, nor write one with your right hand" (29:48); "I have stayed among you a lifetime before it" (10:16).
review: open
