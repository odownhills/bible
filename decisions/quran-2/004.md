## 2:102 recited against the kingdom of Solomon | recited in the reign of Solomon
kind: two senses
call: close
why: The small Arabic word before "the kingdom" most often means "on" or "against", and after a word of speaking it marks the one a false thing is said about: "do you say about God what you do not know?" (2:80; so also 2:169; 7:28; 10:68), "one who invents a lie against God" (6:21; 61:7), "if he had made up any sayings against us" (69:44). The very next words answer a charge against Solomon: "Solomon did not disbelieve, but the devils disbelieved." And the Quran says where his kingdom came from: he prayed, "My Lord, forgive me and grant me a kingdom," and God made the wind and the devils serve him (38:35-38; so also 21:81-82; 34:12).
other: The same small word can also mark a time: Moses "entered the city at a time when its people were unaware" (28:15), and "our messenger has come to you ... at a break in the messengers" (5:19). The Quran places the devils inside Solomon's kingdom, diving and building for him (21:82; 38:37), so the words can be telling when and where they recited it, and "Solomon did not disbelieve" then clears him of what went on in his reign.
review: open

## 2:102 and what was sent down to the two angels in Babylon, Harut and Marut | and it was not sent down to the two angels in Babylon, Harut and Marut
kind: grammar
call: ask
why: One short Arabic word here can mean "what" or "not". The sentence goes straight on as if something was sent down and is taught: "the two do not teach anyone until they say, 'We are only a trial, so do not disbelieve,'" and "they learn from the two". The only two just named are the two angels, and their warning suits ones sent by God with something: a trial is what God sends ("we test you with evil and with good as a trial", 21:35), and the number of the angels set over the Fire is itself "only a trial for those who disbelieve" (74:31). The same two Arabic words mean "what was sent down" a few verses earlier (2:91) and at 2:4 and 2:136. And the verse goes on to say the harm is done only "by God's permission".
other: The same short word is "not" a few words earlier in this sentence: "Solomon did not disbelieve." So this can be a second denial set beside the first: Solomon did not disbelieve, and no such thing was sent down to the two angels. The words make a denial at 3:65: "the Torah and the Gospel were not sent down until after him." And it fits what the Quran says elsewhere: angels "do not disobey God in what he commands them" (66:6) and "act by his command" (21:27), and of magic it says "the magician does not succeed, wherever he goes" (20:69) and "God will bring it to nothing" (10:81).
review: open
