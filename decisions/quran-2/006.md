## 2:125 And take from the place where Abraham stood a place of prayer | and they took from the place where Abraham stood a place of prayer
kind: readings differ
call: clear
why: The main text always follows the Hafs reading, which makes this a command. The verse goes straight on to another command about the same House, "Purify my House", and commands are set inside the telling of the past in the same way earlier in this surah: "Eat from the good things we have provided for you" (2:57), "Take with strength what we have given you" (2:63). What is to be done at the House is commanded elsewhere too: "proclaim the pilgrimage to the people" (22:27), "let them go around the ancient House" (22:29).
other: Another established reading of the same letters makes it a statement, "they took". Then the verse keeps telling what happened: God made the House a place of return for the people, and the people made a place of prayer where Abraham stood. Abraham himself says that he settled his descendants by the House for prayer: "Our Lord, I have settled some of my descendants in a valley with no crops, by your sacred House, our Lord, so that they may keep up the prayer" (14:37).
review: open

## 2:143 a middle community | a just community
kind: two senses
call: ask
why: The Arabic word is the ordinary one for the middle of something, and that is how the Quran uses it and its family: "the middle prayer" (2:238), "the middle of what you feed your families" (5:89), "plunging into the middle of a gathering" (100:5). The passage has just set those spoken to apart from two sides, "Be Jews or Christians" (2:135), and has answered the question about their direction with "To God belong the east and the west" (2:142).
other: In Arabic the middle of a thing is also its best and fairest part. Among the owners of the garden, the one called by this same word, "the middle one of them", is the one who had been right: "Did I not say to you, Why do you not glorify?" (68:28). The verse says at once what this community is for, "so that you may be witnesses over the people", and the Quran wants witnesses to be just: "two just men from among you" (5:106; 65:2), "witnesses in justice" (5:8). So also "You are the best community brought out for the people" (3:110).
review: open

## 2:146 recognize it | recognize him
kind: grammar
call: clear
why: The question is what the small Arabic word for "it" or "him" points back to. The messenger is spoken to as "you" in the verses on both sides (2:145, 147), so he is not the one spoken about here. And what these people know has just been named: "those who were given the Book know well that it is the truth from their Lord" (2:144). This verse goes on, "a group of them conceal the truth while they know", and the next begins, "The truth is from your Lord". The same is said elsewhere: "those to whom we gave the Book know that it is sent down from your Lord with the truth, so do not be among the doubters" (6:114). What they recognize is a thing in "when what they recognized came to them, they disbelieved in it" (2:89) and in "because of what they recognize of the truth" (5:83).
other: The likeness is to knowing persons, "as they recognize their sons". The same sentence stands at 6:20, straight after the messenger has spoken of himself: "this Quran has been revealed to me so that I may warn you with it" (6:19). And the Quran does speak of recognizing a messenger, and of finding him in their Book: "Or did they not recognize their messenger, so they deny him?" (23:69); "the messenger ... whom they find written down with them in the Torah and the Gospel" (7:157).
review: open

## 2:148 a direction he turns toward | a direction he is turned toward
kind: readings differ
call: clear
why: The main text always follows the Hafs reading, in which each one does the turning. The same shape of sentence says so of rites: "For every community we have appointed a rite that they perform" (22:67). And the passage has just said that each keeps to its own: "they would not follow your direction, nor do you follow their direction, nor do they follow one another's direction" (2:145).
other: Another established reading of the same letters, with one vowel changed, makes it "he is turned toward". In this passage the one who turns people is God: "we will certainly turn you to a direction you will be pleased with" (2:144), and the fools ask, "What has turned them from the direction they used to face?" (2:142). So also "we turn him to what he has turned to" (4:115), and "For each of you we have made a law and a way", which goes on in these same words, "so race to what is good" (5:48). Even in the main reading "he" can be taken as God, who turns each one toward it.
review: open
