## 2:30 a successor | a deputy
kind: two senses
call: clear
why: The word names one who comes after another and has his place. Everywhere else the Quran uses it and its family, it is of those who come after others and have the earth in their turn: "he made you successors after the people of Noah" (7:69), "successors after Ad" (7:74), "then we made you successors on the earth after them, to see how you would act" (10:14; so also 6:165; 10:73; 27:62; 35:39), and as a verb, "he will make them successors on the earth as he made those before them successors" (24:55; so also 6:133; 7:129; 11:57). The one other time the word is singular it is said to David in these same words, "we have made you a successor on the earth" (38:26), and David came to the kingdom after another king (2:247, 251). And the angels answer about what this one will do on the earth, not about anyone he stands for.
other: The same root is used once of filling the place of someone who is away: Moses says to Aaron, "Take my place among my people" (7:142). David is made what this word names and is told at once, "so judge between people by the truth" (38:26), which is an office and not only a turn. And the one placed on the earth here is taught all the names, and the angels are told to bow down to him (2:31-34), which suits one who acts on the earth for God.
review: open

## 2:53 the Criterion | the deliverance
kind: two senses
call: clear
why: The word is from "to separate": what tells one thing from another. Its verb is used that way: "we make no distinction between any of them" (2:136, 285), and Moses himself prays, "so separate us from the defiant people" (5:25). Elsewhere it is something sent down as the Book is, and it goes with guidance: "clear proofs of the guidance and the criterion" (2:185); "he sent down the Torah and the Gospel before, as guidance for the people, and he sent down the Criterion" (3:3-4); "who sent down the Criterion on his servant" (25:1). Here too it is given with the Book "so that you might be guided", and again to Moses and Aaron with "a light and a reminder" (21:48).
other: The parting of the sea three verses earlier is the same word, "we parted the sea for you, and saved you" (2:50), so what Moses was given may be that rescue. The day named after this word is a day of battle, "the day the two forces met" (8:41). And it is promised to those who are mindful of God, "he will make for you" this (8:29), in the words that elsewhere promise them "a way out" (65:2).
review: open

## 2:54 kill yourselves | kill one another
kind: two senses
call: ask
why: The Arabic says "kill your selves", and a few words earlier in the same verse "your selves" can only mean themselves: "you have wronged yourselves". The same command is quoted once more beside a thing each person does himself: "If we had prescribed for them, 'Kill yourselves or leave your homes,' they would not have done it, except a few of them" (4:66).
other: Said to a whole people, "your selves" is also the Quran's way of saying "one another", and it is said so of this same people with this same verb: "you shall not shed your blood or drive yourselves out of your homes ... then here you are, killing yourselves and driving a group of you out of their homes" (2:84-85). So also "greet yourselves" for greeting one another (24:61) and "do not find fault with yourselves" (49:11).
review: open

## 2:61 its garlic | its grain
kind: two senses
call: close
why: The word comes only here in the Quran, so nothing else in it shows which is meant. It stands in a list of garden plants, after greens and cucumbers and before lentils and onions, the things eaten beside the main food, and garlic belongs in that company.
other: The word was also used for wheat, the grain for bread. What the people will not put up with is "one food" (2:61), and when the Quran speaks of what the earth is made to grow for food, grain is named first: "we brought grain out of it, and they eat of it" (36:33); "let man look at his food ... we made grain grow in it" (80:24-27).
review: open

## 2:61 Go down to a city | Go down to Egypt
kind: two senses
call: clear
why: Here the word has the ending that makes it "a city", any one. In the four places where the Quran names Egypt the word never has that ending: "in Egypt" (10:87), "from Egypt" (12:21), "Enter Egypt" (12:99), "the kingdom of Egypt" (43:51). And these people were not sent back to Egypt: they were told, "enter the holy land" (5:21), and when they refused, "it is forbidden to them for forty years; they will wander in the land" (5:26).
other: It is the same word as the name of Egypt, and a name can take that ending, as Noah's does (3:33). Egypt is where they had come from (2:49-50), and of the "gardens and springs" that Pharaoh's people were brought out of it is said, "we made the Children of Israel inherit them" (26:57-59).
review: open
