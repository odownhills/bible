## 2:104 Wait for us | Look at us
kind: two senses
call: clear
why: The Arabic verb is the common word for looking, but when it takes its object straight after it, as it does here, the Quran uses it for waiting. The same words are said once more, by those left in the dark to the believers walking ahead of them with their light: "Wait for us, so that we may take some of your light" (57:13). The same form tells what people are waiting for nine more times, once in this surah: "Are they waiting for anything but that God should come to them?" (2:210; so also 7:53; 16:33; 35:43; 36:49; 38:15; 43:66; 47:18, and 6:158, which ends, "Wait; we are waiting"), and guests are told to come "not waiting for its time" (33:53). Where someone looks at a person, the Quran writes the small word "at" every time: Moses says, "Show me, so that I may look at you" (7:143), and of the messenger himself, "among them are some who look at you" (10:43; so also 7:198; 33:19; 47:20).
other: Looking is what the word most often means, and it does take its object straight after it when the thing looked at is a whole matter: "the day a man will look at what his hands have sent ahead" (78:40), "let a soul look at what it has sent ahead for tomorrow" (59:18). The request it replaces asks for the messenger's attention. And where the same two requests are named again, this one stands next to "Hear" (4:46), so it can be asking him to turn his eyes to them as well as his ears.
review: open

## 2:106 or cause to be forgotten | or postpone
kind: readings differ
call: clear
why: The main text always follows the Hafs reading, and it reads "cause it to be forgotten". The Quran speaks of this elsewhere: "We will have you recite, so you will not forget, except what God wills" (87:6-7), and "if we willed, we could take away what we have revealed to you" (17:86). God is the one who makes people forget in "they forgot God, so he made them forget themselves" (59:19). And the verse goes on to something brought in place of the sign, which fits a sign that is gone: "when we put a sign in place of a sign" (16:101).
other: Another established reading of the same letters makes the verb "postpone". The Quran uses that word once more, for the putting off of a sacred month (9:37). And it does speak of what is sent down as coming in its time and not all at once: "a recitation that we have divided, so that you may recite it to the people slowly" (17:106; so also 25:32); "it is not for a messenger to bring a sign except by God's permission; for every term there is a writing" (13:38); and those who ask why no sign is sent down are told, "wait; I am waiting with you" (10:20).
review: open

## 2:119 and you will not be asked about the companions of the Blaze | and do not ask about the companions of the Blaze
kind: readings differ
call: clear
why: The main text always follows the Hafs reading, which makes this a statement. The same statement is made twice a little later in this surah, "you will not be asked about what they used to do" (2:134, 141), and again in "You will not be asked about what we are guilty of, nor will we be asked about what you do" (34:25). It fits what has just been said, that he is sent "as a bringer of good news and a warner": "on you is only the delivering, and on us is the reckoning" (13:40); "you are only one who reminds; you are not in control of them" (88:21-22).
other: Another established reading of the same letters makes it a command, "do not ask". Noah is told so when he pleads for his son: "do not ask me about what you have no knowledge of" (11:46; so also 11:37, "do not speak to me about those who did wrong"). And these same people are named where asking on their behalf is ruled out: "It is not for the prophet and those who believe to ask forgiveness for those who give God partners, even if they are relatives, after it has become clear to them that they are the companions of the Blaze" (9:113).
review: open
