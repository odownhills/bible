## 2:76 before your Lord? Will you not use your reason?" | before your Lord?" Will you not use your reason?
kind: grammar
call: close
why: The question is where their speech ends: is "Will you not use your reason?" still what they say to one another when alone, or is it God's question once they have spoken? Nothing in the Arabic marks a change of speaker, and the "you" runs straight on: "what God has opened to you", "argue against you", "your Lord", "will you not use your reason". What follows speaks about them, not to anyone: "Do they not know that God knows what they keep secret and what they make public?" (2:77). And wrongdoers do scold one another like this in their private talk: "those who do wrong keep their talk secret: Is this anything but a human being like you? Will you go to magic while you see?" (21:3).
other: These same words come twelve more times in the Quran, and every time they are said by God or by a messenger to people in the wrong, never by wrongdoers to one another: to the Children of Israel earlier in this surah (2:44), and 3:65; 6:32; 7:169; 10:16; 11:51; 12:109; 21:10; 21:67; 23:80; 28:60; 37:138. Read that way their speech ends at "before your Lord?", and the last question is God's own, closing the question that opened the passage: "So do you hope that they will believe you?" (2:75).
review: open

## 2:78 who do not know the Book, only wishful thinking | who know the Book only as recitation
kind: two senses
call: clear
why: The same word comes again in this surah about the same people. They say, "No one will enter the Garden unless he is a Jew or a Christian," and the answer is, "That is their wishful thinking" (2:111). So also "It is not by your wishful thinking, nor the wishful thinking of the People of the Book: whoever does evil will be repaid for it" (4:123), and "wishful thinking deluded you" (57:14). Its verb is "wish": "then wish for death" (2:94), "Or will man have whatever he wishes?" (53:24). And the passage goes straight on to such a wish, said without knowledge: "The Fire will not touch us except for a limited number of days", answered with "do you say about God what you do not know?" (2:80), which fits "they are only guessing" here.
other: The word was also used for reciting. These are unlettered people, so what they can have of the Book is what they hear recited, and the next verse turns to those who write it (2:79). The one place where the Quran itself may use the word this way is 22:52, where a messenger "wished" or "recited", and Satan cast something into his "wish" or "recitation", and then God "makes his signs firm".
review: open
