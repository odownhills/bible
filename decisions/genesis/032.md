## 32:28 with God | with divine beings
kind: two senses
call: clear
why: Jacob himself says at once whom he has met: he names the place Peniel, "for I have seen God face to face, and my life has been rescued" (32:30), and to fear for one's life is what Scripture says of seeing God himself: "We will certainly die, for we have seen God" (Judges 13:22); "a human being cannot see me and live" (Exodus 33:20). When the name is given a second time it is God who gives it: "And God said to him, 'Your name is Jacob' ... and he named him Israel" (35:10), and Jacob is sent to Bethel to build an altar "to the God who appeared to you when you fled from Esau your brother" (35:1). Hosea says of this night, "in his vigor he struggled with God" (Hosea 12:3).
other: The Hebrew word is the same for "God", for "gods" and for beings of the heavenly kind, and here it stands in a pair with "men", as in Jotham's story of the trees: "my new wine, which cheers gods and men" (Judges 9:13). The one who wrestled with Jacob is called "a man" (32:24), and Hosea goes on, "He struggled with an angel and prevailed" (Hosea 12:4). Read this way the sentence says that Jacob has held his own against those of heaven and those of earth alike.
