## 29:17 tender | weak
kind: two senses
call: ask
why: Everywhere else the word means soft, tender or delicate, and it is never said of sight: "a calf, tender and good" (18:7); "the children are tender" (33:13); "the tender and delicate woman among you" (Deuteronomy 28:56); "a soft answer turns away wrath" (Proverbs 15:1). When Scripture means eyes that are failing it uses other words: "his eyes had grown too dim to see" (27:1); "the eyes of Israel were heavy from old age" (48:10); Moses' "eye had not grown dim" (Deuteronomy 34:7). Eyes are counted in a person's beauty: David had "beautiful eyes" (1 Samuel 16:12). So the sentence can be giving Leah her one beauty and Rachel beauty in everything. "Tender" is also the word's own plain English, and it leaves room for a weakness, as "the children are tender" does.
other: The sentence sets Leah's eyes against Rachel's beauty ("but Rachel was beautiful in form and beautiful in appearance"), and the next words are "And Jacob loved Rachel" (29:18): a fault in Leah explains that most simply, and she is then "hated" (29:31). The same word is weakness in "I am weak today, though anointed king" (2 Samuel 3:39) and in "afraid and weak of heart" (Deuteronomy 20:8), and Jacob's "the children are tender" (33:13) means that they are frail. Read so, her eyes were weak or dull. It is marked "ask" because one reading names something lovely in Leah and the other a fault.
review: open
