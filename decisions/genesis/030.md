## 30:8 wrestlings of God | mighty wrestlings
kind: two senses
call: clear
why: The Hebrew words are "wrestlings of God", and in this chapter the giving of children is God's doing each time it is spoken of: "Am I in the place of God, who has kept back from you the fruit of the womb?" (30:2); "God has judged in my favor, and he has also listened to my voice and given me a son" (30:6, Rachel's words over this child's brother); "God listened to Leah" (30:17); "God remembered Rachel" (30:22). So the contest with her sister is one in which God is on the scene. The same book later sets the same word "prevailed" beside God: "you have striven with God and with men, and you have prevailed" (32:28).
other: Hebrew can add "of God" to a thing to say that it is very great: a great panic "became a trembling of God" (1 Samuel 14:15); "Nineveh was a great city to God, a journey of three days" (Jonah 3:3); "Your righteousness is like the mountains of God" (Psalm 36:6). Read this way Rachel says only how hard the wrestling with her sister was. At 23:6 the same kind of phrase stands as "a chieftain of God [alt: a mighty chieftain]".
review: open

## 30:20 will make his home with me | will honor me
kind: two senses
call: close
why: The verb stands only here in Scripture. Its noun is the place where someone lives wherever it stands: Solomon uses it of the house he has built and at once calls that house "a place for you to live in forever" (1 Kings 8:13); "Look down from the heavens and see, from your holy and glorious dwelling" (Isaiah 63:15); "sun and moon stood still in their dwelling" (Habakkuk 3:11). And it is what Leah said when her first sons were born: "my husband will now love me" (29:32); "Now, this time, my husband will be joined to me, because I have borne him three sons" (29:34), the same sentence with the same reason. She has just had to hire him for a night (30:16) and has said to Rachel, "Is your taking my husband a small thing?" (30:15).
other: In 1 Kings 8:13 and Isaiah 63:15 the noun can also be heard as a height, a lofty place: Isaiah sets it beside "the heavens", "holy" and "glorious". The verb would then be "lift up", and Leah would say that her husband will now honor her. Six sons are a reason for that too: "her sons rise up and call her happy; her husband also, and he praises her" (Proverbs 31:28).
review: open
