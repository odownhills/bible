## 38:5 And he was at Chezib when she gave birth to him | And she was at Chezib when she gave birth to them
kind: copies differ
call: clear
why: The Hebrew text has "he was" and "him", and in the Old Testament the main text is always that text. The man in these verses is Judah, who "named him Er" (38:3), so the words say where Judah was when Shelah was born. Scripture ties Shelah's line to a place of this name: among "the sons of Shelah the son of Judah" are "the men of Cozeba" (1 Chronicles 4:21-22), a name from the same root, and Achzib, a name from that root too, is a town in the lowland of Judah (Joshua 15:44; Micah 1:14), listed in the same region as Adullam (Joshua 15:35).
other: The old Greek has "she was at Chezib when she gave birth to them", which makes Chezib the place where all three sons were born. She is the one who acts all through 38:3-5 (she conceived, she gave birth, she named), and the verb form the Hebrew text has for "he was" is not the one a story normally uses; in Hebrew writing it differs from "and she" by one letter.

## 38:12 Hirah his friend the Adullamite | Hirah his shepherd the Adullamite
kind: copies differ
call: clear
why: With the vowels of the Hebrew text the word is "his friend", and in the Old Testament the main text is always that text. Hirah comes into the story as a man in his own right, the one Judah went to when he left his brothers: he "turned aside to an Adullamite man, and his name was Hirah" (38:1). Judah trusts him with a private errand (38:20) and counts him with himself: "so that we are not held in contempt" (38:23). It is the word used of "Job's three friends" (Job 2:11), and a related form is used of the man who stands beside a ruler: "Ahuzzath his friend" (26:26).
other: Written without vowels, the same letters are also "his shepherd", and the old Greek and Latin versions have that here and at 38:20. The two men are going up to the shearing of Judah's flock (38:12-13), and it is this man who is sent with the kid "from the flock" (38:17, 20). Tending the flock is what the men of this family do: "his brothers went to graze their father's flock" (37:12).
review: open

## 38:26 She is more righteous than I | She is in the right; it is by me
kind: grammar
call: clear
why: Saul says nearly the same words to David, and there, as here, the reason follows at once: "You are more righteous than I, for you have repaid me good and I have repaid you evil" (1 Samuel 24:17); here, "since I did not give her to Shelah my son". The same verb with the same small word after it is a comparison elsewhere: "they are more righteous than you" (Ezekiel 16:52); "Faithless Israel has shown herself more righteous than treacherous Judah" (Jeremiah 3:11). And where this chapter says by whom Tamar is pregnant it uses a different small word, not this one: "she conceived by him" (38:18); "By the man to whom these belong I am pregnant" (38:25).
other: The two Hebrew words can be taken apart: "She is righteous. From me!" Tamar has just sent word, "By the man to whom these belong I am pregnant", and has asked Judah to say whose they are (38:25); read this way he answers her question in so many words, and "since I did not give her to Shelah my son" says how it came about. The small word "from" is used of the father of a child at 19:36: "the two daughters of Lot conceived by their father".
review: open
