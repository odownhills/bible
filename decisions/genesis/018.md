## 18:3 Lord | My lord
kind: two senses
call: ask
why: The chapter opens by saying who has come: "And Yahweh appeared to him" (18:1). In this verse Abraham speaks to one ("your eyes", "your servant", and "do not pass" is said to one), and only in the next verse to all three ("wash your feet", 18:4); Lot, speaking to the two who go on to Sodom, says "my lords" and speaks to both at once: "Look now, my lords, please turn aside to your servant's house" (19:2). The Hebrew text writes the word here exactly as it writes it where Abraham says "the Lord" to Yahweh later in the chapter (18:27, 30, 31, 32) and "Lord Yahweh" (15:2, 8).
other: The same letters are also "my lord", what a person says to a man he honors: "Hear us, my lord" (23:6), "Drink, my lord" (24:18), and Sarah of Abraham in this chapter, "my lord is old" (18:12). What Abraham lifts his eyes and sees is "three men" (18:2), and he offers what travelers need: water for their feet, rest under the tree, bread, and "afterward you may pass on" (18:4-5). Gideon and Manoah did not know at first who was speaking with them (Judges 6:22; 13:16), and Hebrews 13:2 says that some "have had angels as guests without knowing it". Read this way Abraham is speaking politely to the chief of three strangers, and learns who he is as the chapter goes on (18:13-14).
review: open

## 18:21 whether they have done altogether according to its outcry that has come to me | whether they have done according to its outcry that has come to me; if so, it is the end
kind: grammar
call: clear
why: The Hebrew word stands right after "they have done", and the accent marks of the Hebrew text join the two; "they" are the people of the cities, who are not the ones who would make an end. The same word is "altogether" at Exodus 11:1: "when he sends you out, he will certainly drive you out from here altogether". Yahweh says here only that he is going down to see, as he "came down to see the city and the tower" (11:5), and the sentence ends "and if not, I will know"; the ruin of the place is first announced at 19:13.
other: Everywhere else the same two words, "do" or "make" with this noun, mean to make a complete end, and it is God who makes it: "I will not make a complete end" (Jeremiah 4:27; 5:18), "are you making a complete end of the remnant of Israel?" (Ezekiel 11:13), "you did not make an end of them" (Nehemiah 9:31). Taken as a short sentence by itself ("an end!"), the word gives Yahweh's sentence two balanced halves, "if so" and "and if not", and it would explain why Abraham at once speaks of the place being swept away (18:23-24).
review: open
