## 13:4 and there Abram called on the name of Yahweh | where Abram had called on the name of Yahweh
kind: grammar
call: clear
why: The Hebrew verb is the plain form of the story, the same form as "And he went" (13:3), and the sentence names Abram afresh, though he is already the "he" of "that he had made there"; words that only went on describing the altar would not need to name him again. So the story takes a step here: Abram comes back to his altar and calls on Yahweh at it. That is what he does wherever he stops. He "built an altar there to Yahweh and called on the name of Yahweh" (12:8); at the end of this chapter "he built an altar there to Yahweh" (13:18); at Beersheba, in nearly the same Hebrew words as here, "and there he called on the name of Yahweh" (21:33); and Isaac after him "built an altar there and called on the name of Yahweh" (26:25).
other: Everything else in these two verses looks back: "where his tent had been at the beginning", "that he had made there at the first". And 12:8 has already said that at this altar Abram "called on the name of Yahweh". In Hebrew a verb in the story form can carry on a clause that looks back, even with the one who acts named: "the wells of water that they had dug in the days of Abraham his father, and that the Philistines had stopped up after the death of Abraham" (26:18). Read this way, the words finish the description of the place (the altar he had made, and where he had called), and the verse does not say that Abram called on Yahweh again when he came back.
review: open
