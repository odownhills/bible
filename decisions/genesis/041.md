## 41:34 and let him take a fifth from the land of Egypt | and let him make the land of Egypt ready
kind: two senses
call: close
why: The verb stands only here and is made from the word "five". When the famine comes, a fifth is what Joseph sets as Pharaoh's share of every harvest: "you will give a fifth to Pharaoh" (47:24), and it becomes "a statute to this day over the ground of Egypt, that the fifth is Pharaoh's" (47:26).
other: A word made of the same letters is said of men drawn up in order: the sons of Israel went up from the land of Egypt "in battle order" (Exodus 13:18; also Joshua 1:14; 4:12; Judges 7:11), which gives "make ready". And what is gathered is not called a fifth: "let them gather all the food of these good years" (41:35); "he gathered all the food of the seven years" (41:48).
review: open

## 41:38 the spirit of God | a spirit of gods
kind: two senses
call: clear
why: Joseph has told Pharaoh again and again that God is the one doing this (41:16, 25, 28, 32), and Pharaoh answers with Joseph's word and a verb for one: "Since God has made all this known to you" (41:39). The phrase is the one of 1:2, "the spirit of God", and it is said of a man at Exodus 31:3.
other: The word for "God" is also the word for "gods", and the speaker is the king of Egypt, a land with gods of its own ("on all the gods of Egypt I will carry out judgments", Exodus 12:12). The king of Babylon says the same of Daniel: "a spirit of the holy gods is in him" (Daniel 4:8, 18; 5:11).
review: open

## 41:40 will submit to your word | will arm themselves at your word
kind: two senses
call: close
why: The verb is "kiss" everywhere else in Genesis (27:26-27; 29:11, 13; 33:4; 45:15; 48:10; 50:1), and a kiss is how people yield to a king or a god: Samuel kisses Saul when he anoints him (1 Samuel 10:1); "every mouth that has not kissed him" (1 Kings 19:18); people "kiss calves" (Hosea 13:2). The Hebrew is "on your mouth all my people will kiss", and a person's "mouth" is his command (45:21, "at the command of Pharaoh"); the main text gives what the kiss means, as Pharaoh does next: "without you no man will raise his hand or his foot" (41:44).
other: The same letters are a verb said of men who carry weapons: "armed with bows" (1 Chronicles 12:2; 2 Chronicles 17:17; Psalm 78:9), and the noun made from it is "weapons" (2 Kings 10:2). The verb here has no one named who is kissed, as "kiss" has everywhere else in Genesis.
review: open

## 41:43 Kneel! | an Egyptian word whose meaning is not given
kind: two senses
call: close
why: The word stands only here. Read as Hebrew it is a form of the verb "kneel": "he made the camels kneel" (24:11); "let us kneel before Yahweh our maker" (Psalm 95:6); and the noun from it is "knee" ("to me every knee will bow", Isaiah 45:23). A verb form that begins with the same letter in place of the usual one stands at Jeremiah 25:3 ("rising early"). The cry fits the moment: Joseph rides out as the man set over all the land.
other: The men who call out are Egyptians, who speak another language (42:23, "the interpreter was between them"), and in this same passage Pharaoh gives Joseph a name in that language (41:45). Scripture gives no meaning for the word and does not use it again.
review: open
