## 25:21 in front of his wife | on behalf of his wife
kind: two senses
call: close
why: Everywhere else in Scripture the Hebrew word means "facing" or "in front of". This book has it once more, of the rods that Jacob set "in front of the flocks" (30:38). Elsewhere: "the lampstand opposite the table" (Exodus 26:35), "Let your eyes look straight ahead" (Proverbs 4:25), "the ways of a man are before the eyes of Yahweh" (Proverbs 5:21), and, of prayer, "pour out your heart like water before the face of the Lord" (Lamentations 2:19). Where one person prays for another, Scripture has another word: "he will pray for you" (20:7), "Plead for me" (Exodus 8:28).
other: The sentence gives its reason at once, "because she was barren", and the answer is that "Rebekah his wife conceived": the plea was about her, and a word that means "with her before him" may here mean "for her". No other verse uses this word of the person a prayer is for.
review: open

## 25:22 why am I alive? | why is this happening to me?
kind: grammar
call: ask
why: The Hebrew is three words: "why", a small word that sharpens the question, and "I". Taken so, she asks what she is for. The small word works that way in this book, "Why is it that Sarah laughed" (18:13), and a few verses on Esau asks in the same shape, "so what is a birthright to me?" (25:32). Rebekah herself speaks this way again: "why do I have life?" (27:46). And "If it is like this" leads up to a question about something other than the struggle itself.
other: The small word is also the word "this", so the same three words can be put together as "why is this so with me?", a question about what is going on inside her. That is the question Yahweh answers: she "went to inquire of Yahweh", and he tells her what is in her womb (25:23), not why she lives.
review: open

## 25:23 the older will serve the younger | the greater will serve the lesser
kind: two senses
call: close
why: The second word is the younger of two sisters or brothers everywhere else in this book: "the firstborn said to the younger" (19:31), "to give the younger before the firstborn" (29:26), "he was the younger" (48:14). The account goes straight on to which twin came out first (25:25-26) and to the right of the firstborn (25:31-34), and when the saying comes true it is said to Esau, the older son (27:1): "you will serve your brother" (27:40).
other: The first word is the ordinary word for "great" or "many", as in "the great deep" (7:11) and "their possessions were many" (13:6), and no other verse of this book uses it of age. The second word can also mean the least in size: "the least will become a powerful nation" (Isaiah 60:22). The line before is about two peoples and which is the stronger. Paul quotes the saying in Greek words that mean "the greater" and "the lesser" (Romans 9:12).
review: open
