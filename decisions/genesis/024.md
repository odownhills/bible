## 24:21 was gazing at her | was astonished at her
kind: two senses
call: clear
why: The sentence itself says what the man was doing: he kept silent "to know whether Yahweh had made his way succeed or not". He had asked for a sign in what the young woman would say and do (24:14), and she was still doing it, so he watched her for his answer. The verb sounds almost the same as the one in "And Yahweh looked at Abel and at his offering" (4:4), and Isaiah has this same form of it beside "see": "so that we may look on and see it together" (Isaiah 41:23), and "Do not look about you, for I am your God" (Isaiah 41:10).
other: As the Hebrew text spells it here, the word belongs to a verb that is said of land laid waste: "until cities lie waste, with no one living in them" (Isaiah 6:11). Hebrew uses another word both for a land laid waste and for a person stunned: "The upright are appalled at this" (Job 17:8), "Turn to me and be appalled" (Job 21:5). And she had given him cause to be astonished: she offered what he had prayed for and drew water for ten camels (24:19-20). This form of the verb stands only here.

## 24:55 a few days, or ten | a year, or ten months
kind: two senses
call: clear
why: The Hebrew is "days, or ten". In this book "days" with no number is a time that is not counted: "And after some time" (4:3), where the Hebrew is "at the end of days", and "they were in custody for some time" (40:4). The word for "ten" in this form is elsewhere a count of days, the tenth day: "on the tenth of this month" (Exodus 12:3), "on the tenth of the month" (Leviticus 16:29). And the servant, who had asked only "Send me on my way to my master" (24:54), answers "Do not delay me" (24:56), the answer of a man asked to wait some days.
other: "Days" by itself can mean a year: "its right of redemption lasts a year" (Leviticus 25:29), "ten pieces of silver a year" (Judges 17:10), and "a year and four months" (1 Samuel 27:7), where "days" is followed by a number of months as it would be here. Read this way they ask to keep her a year, or at least ten months, with "months" left unsaid; no other verse uses this word for "ten" of months.
review: open

## 24:63 to meditate | to walk about
kind: two senses
call: clear
why: The word stands only here. It differs by one letter from a verb for turning a thing over in thought or pouring it out, often before God, and reads as another spelling of it: "I will meditate on your precepts" (Psalm 119:15), "on your deeds I will meditate" (Psalm 77:12), "Evening and morning and at noon I complain and moan" (Psalm 55:17), and "I will complain in the bitterness of my soul" (Job 7:11). Its noun is the "meditation before God" of Job 15:4. The chapter ends by saying what Isaac was carrying: "And Isaac was comforted after his mother" (24:67).
other: What Rebekah saw was a man "walking in the field to meet us" (24:65). The word is not spelled quite as the verb of the Psalms is, and no other verse of Scripture has it, so nothing outside this passage fixes its meaning.
review: open
