## 44:5 and with it he certainly learns by divination | and he would certainly learn about it by divination
kind: grammar
call: clear
why: The same small word, with the same "it", stands in the words just before, where the goblet is the thing used: "what my master drinks from" is word for word "what my master drinks with it". Other verbs of divining take that word for the means: "Divine for me, please, by a spirit of the dead" (1 Samuel 28:8); the king of Babylon "inquires by the teraphim" (Ezekiel 21:21).
other: The same small word can be "about": "you are to speak about them" (Deuteronomy 6:7); "I will speak about you to my father" (1 Samuel 19:3). Joseph does not name the goblet when he says it himself: "Did you not know that a man like me certainly learns by divination?" (44:15), which is how he says they were found out, and the goblet was then in Benjamin's bag, not in Joseph's hand.
review: open
