## 47:21 he moved them to the cities | he made them slaves
kind: copies differ
call: clear
why: The Hebrew text reads "he moved them to the cities", and in the Old Testament the main text is always that text. It can stand with what Genesis has told: in the good years Joseph "put food in the cities; the food of the fields around each city he put inside it" (41:48), so the people are brought to where the bread is kept, "from one end of the border of Egypt to its other end".
other: The Samaritan copy and the old Greek have "he made them slaves"; in Hebrew the two readings are written with nearly the same letters. It is what the people asked for and what both sides say was done: "Buy us and our ground for bread, and we and our ground will be slaves to Pharaoh" (47:19); "I have bought you today, and your ground, for Pharaoh" (47:23); "we will be slaves to Pharaoh" (47:25). The verse before tells what became of the ground (47:20), and this one would tell what became of the people, who are then told to sow the ground (47:23).

## 47:31 at the head of the bed | on the top of his staff
kind: copies differ
call: clear
why: The Hebrew text reads "bed", and in the Old Testament the main text is always that text. The story goes on with the same word: when Joseph comes to his sick father, "Israel gathered his strength and sat up on the bed" (48:2), and at the end Jacob "drew his feet up into the bed" (49:33). David, old and in bed, does the same when his son is made king: "the king bowed down on the bed" (1 Kings 1:47, with another word for a bed).
other: "Bed" and "staff" are written with the same Hebrew letters and differ only in their vowels, which the oldest copies did not write. The old Greek read the word as "staff", and Hebrews 11:21 gives the verse that way: "By faith Jacob, when he was dying, blessed each of the sons of Joseph, and he worshiped on the top of his staff." Read as "staff", it is the word for Judah's staff at 38:18.
review: open
