## 27:39 away from the richness of the earth will be the place where you live, and away from the dew of the sky above | of the richness of the earth will be the place where you live, and of the dew of the sky above
kind: two senses
call: ask
why: Isaac has just told Esau that nothing is left to give: "with grain and new wine I have sustained him. And for you, then, what can I do, my son?" (27:37), and grain and new wine are what the dew and the rich earth give (27:28). The next line gives Esau another way to live than the field: "by your sword you will live, and you will serve your brother" (27:40). And Esau does not go away as a man who has been given the same good land: he "bore a grudge against Jacob over the blessing with which his father had blessed him" (27:41). The small Hebrew word that stands before "the richness" and "the dew" also means "away from" or "without": "you will lift your face without blemish" (Job 11:15); "Their houses are at peace, without dread" (Job 21:9). Scripture later speaks of Esau's land as a high and rocky one: "you who live in the clefts of the rock" (Obadiah 3); "I made his mountains a desolation" (Malachi 1:3).
other: These are the words of Jacob's blessing over again, in the other order, and there the same small word is "of": "may God give you of the dew of the sky and of the richness of the earth" (27:28). A hearer who had just heard them would take them the same way. Scripture counts what Isaac said to Esau as a blessing: "By faith Isaac blessed Jacob and Esau, also about things to come" (Hebrews 11:20). And Esau did well in his land: Yahweh says, "I have given the hill country of Seir to Esau as a possession" (Deuteronomy 2:5), and of Esau and Jacob it is said that "their possessions were too many for them to live together" (36:7). The first word can also be read as one noun with no small word before it, "the rich places", as in "the richest parts of the province" (Daniel 11:24); that gives "the rich places of the earth will be the place where you live". It is marked "ask" because the two readings say opposite things about what Esau was given.
review: open

## 27:40 when you grow restless | when you roam free
kind: two senses
call: close
why: The verb is rare, and in this form it stands in only one other place, of a man who cannot be still under his trouble: "I am restless in my complaint, and I moan" (Psalm 55:2). Read so, Esau serves until the serving chafes, and then he breaks the yoke. That is how it went: "In his days Edom rebelled from under the hand of Judah, and they set a king over themselves" (2 Kings 8:20).
other: The plain form of the same verb is said of a people that goes where it likes and owns no master: "Why do my people say, 'We roam free; we will come to you no more'?" (Jeremiah 2:31). Read so, the line names the time when Esau gets loose rather than what he feels before it. The old Greek has another verb here, "when you pull down".
review: open
