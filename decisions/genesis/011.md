## 11:1 the same words | few words
kind: two senses
call: clear
why: The Hebrew sets the word "one" in the plural after "words", and the story is about being one. Yahweh sums the people up in that way: "they are one people, and they all have one speech" (11:6). What he then does is the opposite of this verse: he confuses their speech "so that they will not understand one another's speech" (11:7). So what they had at the start was words in common, and nothing in the story turns on how many words they had. The same plural form means a single thing where Ezekiel joins two sticks: "and they will become one in your hand" (Ezekiel 37:17).
other: Everywhere else that this plural form stands after a noun, the noun is "days" and the sense is "a few": "stay with him a few days" (Genesis 27:44); "and they were in his eyes like a few days" (Genesis 29:20); "but within a few days he will be broken" (Daniel 11:20). Two of the three are this writer's own. Read this way, the verse says that the first people had one speech and only a few words.

## 11:2 eastward | from the east
kind: two senses
call: close
why: The Hebrew words are "from east", and in Genesis they mark the side on which a thing lies, not a place that someone leaves: "a garden in Eden, in the east" (2:8); "east of the garden of Eden he stationed the cherubim" (3:24); "the hill country east of Bethel", with "Bethel on the west and Ai on the east" (12:8). The one other place where this writer joins them to this same verb is about Lot, who was in the hills between Bethel and Ai (13:3) and chose the plain of the Jordan, which lies to the east of them: "and Lot traveled eastward" (13:11). There he cannot be coming from the east. The ark had come to rest "on the mountains of Ararat" (8:4), and Scripture sets Ararat to the north of Babylon: Jeremiah calls "the kingdoms of Ararat, Minni, and Ashkenaz" against her (Jeremiah 51:27), and her attackers come "from the land of the north" (Jeremiah 50:9). A road from there down to Shinar does not come from the east.
other: With this verb, "from" ordinarily names the place that is left: "And Abraham traveled from there to the land of the Negev" (20:1); "And they traveled from Bethel" (35:16). Where this writer means the direction, he has another form of the word for it: "northward and southward and eastward and westward" (13:14); "eastward, to the land of the east" (25:6). And the chapter before has just named the east as a place where people lived, "the hill country of the east" (10:30), as this writer later speaks of "the land of the sons of the east" (29:1). Read this way, the people came into Shinar out of the east. The evidence is near even: the plain sense of the two words is "from the east", and the writer's own use of them is "on the east side" and, once with this verb, "eastward".

## 11:32 two hundred and five years | a hundred and forty-five years
kind: copies differ
call: clear
why: The Hebrew text reads "two hundred and five", and in the Old Testament the main text is always that text. It agrees with the rest of Scripture if 11:26 is read as 5:32 has to be read. "And Noah was five hundred years old, and Noah fathered Shem, Ham, and Japheth" (5:32) names three sons at one age, yet Shem was not born in that year: he was "a hundred years old" two years after the flood (11:10), and Noah was six hundred when the flood came (7:6). In the same way "Terah lived seventy years, and he fathered Abram, Nahor, and Haran" (11:26) does not have to mean that Abram was the son born in that year. If Abram was born when Terah was a hundred and thirty, then Abram, who "was seventy-five years old when he went out from Haran" (12:4), left in the year that his father died at two hundred and five.
other: The Samaritan copy of Genesis reads "a hundred and forty-five". That is the seventy years of 11:26 and the seventy-five of 12:4 added together, so that Terah dies in the year Abram leaves Haran. Stephen says that Abram left after that death: "and from there, after his father died, God moved him to this land in which you now live" (Acts 7:4). And Genesis tells of Terah's death (11:32) before it tells of Abram going out (12:1-4). With two hundred and five years, and Abram born when Terah was seventy, Terah would have lived on in Haran for sixty years after Abram left.
review: open
