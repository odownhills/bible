## 19:14 who were to marry his daughters | who had married his daughters
kind: grammar
call: close
why: The Hebrew is "the takers of his daughters", a form that does not say when. The only daughters of Lot that the story shows are the two in his house, "who have not been intimate with a man" (19:8), and they leave Sodom with no husbands (19:30-31). The same form is used twice just here of what is about to happen: "we are going to ruin this place" (19:13), "Yahweh is going to ruin the city" (19:14). A man can be called a husband, and a woman his wife, before the wedding: the virgin promised to a man is "his neighbor's wife" (Deuteronomy 22:23-24), and Joseph is told, "do not be afraid to take Mary your wife" (Matthew 1:20).
other: The angels say "your two daughters who are here" (19:15), which can be heard as setting these two apart from daughters who are not in the house, and they have just asked about "a son-in-law, and your sons and your daughters" in the city (19:12). The form is also a plain description of a husband, the man who has taken a woman (the verb of 11:29, "Abram and Nahor took wives for themselves"). Read this way Lot had other daughters, married, who stayed with their husbands and died in Sodom.
review: open

## 19:18 Lord | my lords
kind: two senses
call: ask
why: In the next verse Lot speaks to one: "your servant", "your eyes", and "your faithful love that you have shown me" are all said to one, where at 19:2, speaking to the two, he says "your servant" in the form for more than one. One answers him, and answers as the one who overturns: "I will not overturn the city of which you have spoken" (19:21); and it is Yahweh who does it (19:24-25). The life Lot gives thanks for was kept "because Yahweh was sparing him" (19:16). The Hebrew for "my lord", said to one man, is written differently (18:12; 23:6); this word is written as it is where Abraham says "the Lord" to Yahweh (18:27, 30, 31, 32).
other: The verse itself says "Lot said to them", and at 19:2 he calls the two "my lords". At the end of a sentence, where the word stands here, the Hebrew text writes "my lords" and "Lord" in the same way. The two say "Yahweh has sent us" (19:13), and Yahweh "went away when he had finished speaking to Abraham" (18:33). Read this way Lot is still speaking politely to the two, and in the next verse he turns to the one who has just spoken ("he said", 19:17).
review: open

## 19:31 in the land | on the earth
kind: two senses
call: close
why: Lot and his daughters have just come from Zoar, a city that was spared at Lot's own asking (19:20-22) and that still had people in it, since "he was afraid to live in Zoar" (19:30). The angels spoke only of "this place" and "the city" (19:13-14), and what was overturned was "those cities and all the plain" (19:25). The word is "the land" where Genesis speaks of this region: "the land of the plain" (19:28); "the Canaanites were then in the land" (12:6).
other: The same Hebrew word is "the earth", and it stands again at the end of this sentence: "the way of all the earth". The daughters go on to speak of keeping "offspring alive" (19:32, 34), words that stand in only one other place, of the flood: "to keep offspring alive on the face of the whole earth" (7:3). Read this way the firstborn thinks the fire has left no man alive anywhere but her father, as after the flood "only Noah was left, and those with him in the ark" (7:23).
review: open
