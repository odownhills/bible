## 17:16 And I will bless her, and she will become nations; kings of peoples will be from her | And I will bless him, and he will become nations; kings of peoples will be from him
kind: copies differ
call: clear
why: The Hebrew text reads "her" and "she", and in the Old Testament the main text is always that text. These two verses are about Sarah and her new name, and they follow the pattern of what was said to Abraham with his: first the name (17:5), then "I will make you into nations, and kings will come out of you" (17:6). So Sarah, like Abraham, is given a name, nations and kings. Scripture sets the two side by side as the source of the people: "Look to Abraham your father, and to Sarah, who gave you birth" (Isaiah 51:2).
other: The old Greek and Latin versions have "him" and "from him" here, so that the second blessing is on the son who has just been named: "I also give you a son from her. And I will bless him". Read this way the sentence does not say "I will bless her" twice. A blessing with nations in it is what God says of a son a few lines later, "I have blessed him ... and I will make him into a great nation" (17:20), and of Sarah's son it is said, "God blessed Isaac his son" (25:11) and, to his wife, "Two nations are in your womb" (25:23). Either way the nations and the kings come from Sarah through her son.
review: open

## 17:19 Indeed, Sarah your wife is going to bear you a son | But Sarah your wife is going to bear you a son
kind: two senses
call: close
why: The Hebrew word that begins God's answer stands in one other place in Genesis, and there it can only affirm: "Indeed we are guilty over our brother" (42:21). It affirms again at 2 Samuel 14:5 ("Indeed I am a widow") and 2 Kings 4:14 ("Indeed she has no son, and her husband is old"). Abraham has just laughed and said in his heart that a man of a hundred and a woman of ninety cannot have a child (17:17), and God answers by saying it again and naming the son for the laugh. God does not refuse what Abraham asked for Ishmael: "And as for Ishmael, I have heard you" (17:20).
other: In the later books the same word is "but": "the men who were with me did not see the vision, but a great trembling fell on them" (Daniel 10:7); "But the people are many" (Ezra 10:13); "But the people were still sacrificing at the high places" (2 Chronicles 33:17). At 1 Kings 1:43 it begins an answer that corrects what was just said ("you bring good news", 1:42). Here it comes straight after Abraham's wish, "If only Ishmael might live before you!" (17:18), and God goes on to set Sarah's son against Ishmael: "But my covenant I will establish with Isaac" (17:21). Read this way God is turning Abraham from Ishmael back to the son Sarah will bear.
review: open
