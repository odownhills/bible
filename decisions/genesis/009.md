## 9:6 by humankind his blood will be shed | in exchange for that human being his blood will be shed
kind: grammar
call: ask
why: The small Hebrew word before "humankind" most often means "in", "with" or "by". In the sentence before, God has named the hand from which he will demand a human life: "from the hand of humankind, from the hand of each one's brother, I will demand the life of humankind" (9:5). Now the wording changes from "I will demand" to "his blood will be shed", and "by humankind" says whose hand is to shed it. That is how the Law carries this out: the dead man's next of kin "is to put the murderer to death" (Numbers 35:19), it is done on the word of witnesses (Numbers 35:30), and the elders of the killer's city are to give him into that kinsman's hand, "and he is to die" (Deuteronomy 19:12). The same small word names the one by whom a thing is done in "a people saved by Yahweh" (Deuteronomy 33:29) and "my lord was commanded by Yahweh" (Numbers 36:2).
other: The same small word also names what a price is paid for: "I will serve you seven years for Rachel" (Genesis 29:18); "life for life, eye for eye" (Deuteronomy 19:21); "that we may put him to death for the life of his brother whom he killed" (2 Samuel 14:7). Read that way, the words say what the killer's blood is shed for, the human being he killed, and they leave unsaid who sheds it. In the sentence before it is God who says three times "I will demand", and he demands it from every living thing as well as from humankind (9:5). The two halves of the saying mirror each other word for word, "sheds, blood, the human" and then "the human, his blood, shed", which is how the Law tells of an exchange: "as he has done, so it will be done to him" (Leviticus 24:19); "no atonement can be made for the land for the blood that is shed in it, except by the blood of the one who shed it" (Numbers 35:33). The reason that follows, "for in the image of God he made humankind", is about the worth of the one who was killed. It is marked "ask" because with "by" the saying puts the shedding of a killer's blood into human hands, and with "in exchange for" it does not say whose hands; the small word is used both ways, and the Law speaks both ways.
review: open

## 9:27 May God enlarge Japheth | May God persuade Japheth
kind: two senses
call: clear
why: This form of the Hebrew verb stands only here in Scripture, and the small word "for" follows it: "may God make it wide for Japheth". This writer speaks of living space in the same way, with another verb and the same small word: "For now Yahweh has made room for us, and we will be fruitful in the land" (Genesis 26:22). Moses blesses a tribe so: "Blessed is the one who enlarges Gad" (Deuteronomy 33:20). The words that follow are about where a people lives: "and may he live in the tents of Shem". Where the verb means "persuade", the one persuaded is its direct object, with no "for" or "to" before him (Judges 14:15; 1 Kings 22:20; Jeremiah 20:7), and here "for" stands before Japheth.
other: Everywhere else in Scripture this verb is about a person who is simple, or who is won over by words, for good or for harm: "I am going to allure her and lead her into the wilderness" (Hosea 2:14); "You persuaded me, Yahweh, and I was persuaded" (Jeremiah 20:7); "By patience a ruler is persuaded" (Proverbs 25:15). The sense "make wide" is seen elsewhere only in "one who opens his lips wide" (Proverbs 20:19), if that is what those words mean. Read as "persuade", Noah asks that God win Japheth over, and living in the tents of Shem is what he is won over to.
review: open
