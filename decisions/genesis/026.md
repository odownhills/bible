## 26:8 laughing with Rebekah his wife | caressing Rebekah his wife
kind: two senses
call: ask
why: Everywhere else in this book the verb is laughing or joking, never touching: "he was like one who was joking" (19:14), the son of Hagar "laughing" (21:9), and in its plain form "Abraham fell on his face and laughed" (17:17) and "everyone who hears will laugh with me" (21:6). It is the word Isaac's name is made from (17:19; 21:6), and here the name and the verb stand side by side: Isaac is doing what his name says. The small word after it is the one for "with" two verses on, "lain with your wife" (26:10), and in "Enoch walked with God" (5:22). The Hebrew picks a word that does not say what Abimelech saw him do, and the main text says no more than the Hebrew does.
other: What Abimelech saw told him at once that she was a wife and not a sister: "Surely, look, she is your wife" (26:9), and he goes straight on to a man lying with her (26:10). A brother and a sister can laugh together, so the word must here cover the way a husband plays with his wife. It is the verb Potiphar's wife uses when she accuses Joseph: "he has brought us a Hebrew man to mock us; he came to me to lie with me" (39:14, and 39:17). Of the people before the golden calf it is said that they "rose to play" (Exodus 32:6), and Paul quotes that line and goes on, "And let us not commit sexual immorality, as some of them did" (1 Corinthians 10:7-8). The small word after the verb can also mark the one a thing is done to, which would make Rebekah the object of the verb.
review: open
