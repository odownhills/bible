## 37:3 a long tunic | a tunic of many colors
kind: two senses
call: ask
why: The Hebrew word that describes this tunic stands only here (37:3, 23, 32) and at 2 Samuel 13:18-19, where Tamar wears the same garment, "for so the king's daughters who were virgins used to dress, in robes": a mark of rank, set beside the word for a long outer robe. The only other place the word itself is found is in the Aramaic of Daniel, where it is the flat of the hand: "the king saw the palm of the hand that was writing" (Daniel 5:5; also 5:24). So the tunic is one "of the palms", long enough to reach the hands. Scripture says nothing of its color, and no other verse fixes the meaning: "long" is as much as these two places support.
other: The old Greek has "a tunic of many colors" here, which is how the word was understood by its translators. Cloth of dyed colors is prized in Scripture: "plunder of dyed cloths for Sisera, plunder of dyed cloths, embroidered" (Judges 5:30), though the words there are different ones.
review: open

## 37:36 the Medanites | the Midianites
kind: copies differ
call: clear
why: The Hebrew text writes "the Medanites" here, and in the Old Testament the main text is always that text. Medan and Midian are two names in Scripture, brothers: "And she bore him Zimran and Jokshan and Medan and Midian and Ishbak and Shuah" (25:2).
other: The old Greek and other ancient versions have "the Midianites" here, the name of the merchants who passed by at 37:28 ("Midianite men, merchants"). In Hebrew writing the two names differ by one small letter. Where the story is taken up again it names a third people: "Potiphar ... bought him from the hand of the Ishmaelites who had brought him down there" (39:1).
review: open

## 37:36 a court official of Pharaoh | a eunuch of Pharaoh
kind: two senses
call: clear
why: Potiphar has a wife: "his master's wife lifted her eyes to Joseph" (39:7). The same word is used of men who hold an office under a king with nothing said of their bodies: Pharaoh's chief cupbearer and chief baker (40:2, 7), an officer "who was in charge of the men of war" (2 Kings 25:19), and the king's men in Samuel's warning, "he will take a tenth of your seed and of your vineyards and give it to his court officials and to his servants" (1 Samuel 8:15).
other: The word is also the word for a castrated man: "let not the eunuch say, 'Look, I am a dry tree'" (Isaiah 56:3). Such men served in kings' courts and kept the women's quarters (Esther 2:3, 14), and of Hezekiah's sons it is said, "they will be eunuchs in the palace of the king of Babylon" (2 Kings 20:18). The old Greek has "eunuch" here. A man could be both.
review: open
