## 20:13 God made me wander | the gods made me wander
kind: grammar
call: clear
why: Abraham has just told this king what he feared was missing in Gerar, "fear of God" (20:11), and the one who sent him out was Yahweh: "Go from your land and from your relatives and from your father's house" (12:1). All through the chapter the word is the one God, who came to Abimelech (20:3, 6) and to whom Abraham prays (20:17). The Hebrew word for "God" is plural in form, and in a few places its verb is plural too where only the one God can be meant: "there God had revealed himself to him" (35:7, of Bethel, where it was Yahweh who stood over Jacob, 28:13); "whom God went to redeem as a people for himself" (2 Samuel 7:23). The Samaritan copy of the Hebrew has the verb in the singular here.
other: The verb is plural, "they made me wander", and that is the ordinary way of saying "gods": "These are your gods, Israel, who brought you up from the land of Egypt" (Exodus 32:4); "these are the gods who struck Egypt" (1 Samuel 4:8, in the mouths of Philistines). Abraham is speaking to a foreign king, and his father's house "served other gods" (Joshua 24:2). Read this way he puts his leaving home in the words his hearer would use.
review: open

## 20:16 it is | he is
kind: grammar
call: clear
why: Abimelech says "Look" twice: first he names the gift, "I have given a thousand pieces of silver to your brother", and then he says what it is for her. In Scripture it is a gift or a payment that covers a person's face or eyes: "I will cover his face with the gift that goes ahead of me" (32:20); "From whose hand have I taken a payment, to hide my eyes with it?" (1 Samuel 12:3); "a bribe blinds those who see clearly" (Exodus 23:8). The word "covering" is everywhere else a thing, never a person: "it is his only covering" (Exodus 22:27); "your covering, with which you cover yourself" (Deuteronomy 22:12); Job 24:7; 31:19.
other: The Hebrew word is "he" as much as "it", and the one named just before it is "your brother". A husband is spoken of as covering his wife: "spread your wing over your servant" (Ruth 3:9); "I spread my wing over you and covered your nakedness" (Ezekiel 16:8). Read this way Abimelech tells Sarah that Abraham himself, now paid and known as her husband, is what shields her from the eyes of everyone with her.
review: open

## 20:16 you are cleared | you are rebuked
kind: two senses
call: ask
why: Everything Abimelech does here is to put the matter right: God has told him that he acted "in the blamelessness of your heart" (20:6), he gives Abraham gifts and the choice of his land (20:14-15), and the silver is "for you", a covering of the eyes for all who are with her. A payment made for her sake goes with clearing her, not with blaming her. In this form the verb is used of a case being settled between two sides: "Come now, and let us settle it, says Yahweh" (Isaiah 1:18); "There an upright man could argue his case with him" (Job 23:7). In its other form it is deciding who is in the right: "let them decide between the two of us" (31:37).
other: The same verb is "rebuke" in the next chapter, between these same two men: "And Abraham rebuked Abimelech over the well of water" (21:25); and "God saw my affliction and the toil of my hands, and he rebuked you last night" (31:42). Abimelech calls Abraham "your brother", which gives Sarah back her own words, "He is my brother" (20:5), and he has just rebuked Abraham (20:9-10). Read this way the silver closes the matter, and his last word to Sarah is that she too stands corrected.
review: open
