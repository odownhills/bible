## 22:13 a ram behind him | one ram, as other Hebrew copies, the Samaritan copy and the old Greek have it
kind: copies differ
call: clear
why: The Hebrew text has the word "behind", and in the Old Testament the main text is always that text. It can be read as it stands: the same word tells where someone is at Psalm 68:25, "The singers went in front, the players behind", and this writer says of Sarah at the entrance of the tent, "it was behind him" (18:10). And when someone lifts his eyes and sees, this writer goes on to say where the thing seen is: "And he lifted his eyes and saw, and look, three men were standing near him" (18:2).
other: The Hebrew words for "behind" and "one" differ by a single letter, and the two letters look much alike. Other Hebrew copies, the Samaritan copy and the old Greek have "one", which is how Hebrew says "a ram", and Daniel has the same sentence with that word: "And I lifted my eyes and saw, and look, one ram standing before the canal" (Daniel 8:3). Standing alone, with no "him" after it, the word in the Hebrew text is ordinarily "afterward" (10:18; 18:5), and "him" has to be supplied to read it as "behind him".
review: open

## 22:14 it will be seen to | he will be seen
kind: grammar
call: ask
why: The saying is given to explain the name, and the name answers 22:8, where Isaac asked, "where is the sheep for a burnt offering?" and Abraham said, "God will see to the sheep for a burnt offering for himself". Then "Abraham lifted his eyes and saw, and look, a ram" (22:13). What is seen on the mountain in this chapter is the ram that God saw to; no one sees Yahweh, whose angel only "called ... from the heavens" (22:11, 15). The Hebrew is "On the mountain of Yahweh ... will be seen" and names no one and nothing as the one seen, like "it is said" just before it, so the saying turns the name around: what Yahweh sees to is seen there. "See" is used the same way of choosing and providing at 1 Samuel 16:1, "for I have seen among his sons a king for myself".
other: Everywhere else that this writer uses this form of "see" with Yahweh, it is Yahweh who is seen: "And Yahweh appeared to Abram" (12:7), "Yahweh appeared to Abram" (17:1), "And Yahweh appeared to him among the oaks of Mamre" (18:1); and the form nowhere else means "be provided". "Yahweh" is the last one named before the verb. The only other verse that names Moriah has the same form of the same verb: "And Solomon began to build the house of Yahweh in Jerusalem on Mount Moriah, where he had appeared to David his father" (2 Chronicles 3:1). At the altar Yahweh is the one seen: "for today Yahweh appears to you" (Leviticus 9:4). The old Greek has "On the mountain the Lord was seen".
review: open

## 22:17 their enemies | his enemies
kind: grammar
call: clear
why: The same sentence has just said that this offspring will be multiplied "like the stars of the sky and like the sand that is on the shore of the sea", so it is many. The Hebrew word "offspring" is singular in form and takes "his" also when it is a whole people: "Our sister, may you become thousands of ten thousands, and may your offspring take possession of the gate of those who hate them" (24:60, where the Hebrew is "him"). Earlier promises speak of the offspring as many: "your offspring will be resident foreigners in a land that is not theirs" (15:13); "I will greatly multiply your offspring, and they will be too many to count" (16:10).
other: The Hebrew is "his enemies", and "offspring" is also said of one son: "another offspring in place of Abel" (4:25); "he is your offspring" (21:13); "I will raise up your offspring after you, who will come out of your body, and I will establish his kingdom" (2 Samuel 7:12). Paul reads the promises to Abraham that way: "It does not say, 'and to offsprings', as of many, but as of one, 'and to your offspring', who is Christ" (Galatians 3:16). The first promise about an offspring says "He will strike your head" (3:15).
review: open

## 22:18 will be blessed in your offspring | will bless themselves by your offspring
kind: two senses
call: close
why: Peter quotes these words, "in your offspring", and takes them as a blessing received: "And in your offspring all the families of the earth will be blessed" (Acts 3:25); and Paul reads the promise the same way, "'All the nations will be blessed in you.' So those who are of faith are blessed together with Abraham, who believed" (Galatians 3:8-9). It is one promise, said five times, and three of the five have the other form of the verb, which was put as "will be blessed in" (12:3; 18:18; 28:14). Here too Yahweh is the one who blesses: "I will certainly bless you" (22:17).
other: The form of the verb here (and at 26:4) ordinarily means doing a thing to oneself: a man "blesses himself in his heart" (Deuteronomy 29:19); "whoever blesses himself in the land will bless himself by the God of truth" (Isaiah 65:16); "nations will bless themselves by him, and in him they will boast" (Jeremiah 4:2). Of the king it stands beside what the nations say: "may all nations bless themselves by him; may they call him happy" (Psalm 72:17). To bless "by" someone is to name him in the blessing: "By you Israel will bless, saying, 'May God make you like Ephraim and like Manasseh'" (48:20). Read this way, the nations will wish for themselves what Abraham's offspring has, who have just been promised "the gate of their enemies".
review: open
