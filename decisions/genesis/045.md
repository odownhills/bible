## 45:7 as a great company of survivors | by a great deliverance
kind: two senses
call: clear
why: The word is the one Jacob used of his camp, with the same small word before it: "then the camp that is left will escape" (32:8), word for word "will be for an escape", that is, will be the part that gets away. Here it stands beside "a remnant" in the same sentence, and the two are paired elsewhere of people who are left: "the survivors of the house of Judah who are left will again take root downward ... for out of Jerusalem a remnant will go out, and survivors from Mount Zion" (2 Kings 19:30-31; also Isaiah 10:20; Ezra 9:14). Joseph says the same thing again later with the people as what is kept alive: "to keep a numerous people alive" (50:20).
other: The same word can be the escape itself: "or there will be no escape for us from Absalom" (2 Samuel 15:14); "I will give them some deliverance in a little while" (2 Chronicles 12:7). "Great" would then say how great the rescue is, not how many are rescued.
review: open

## 45:24 Do not get angry on the way | Do not be afraid on the way
kind: two senses
call: ask
why: The verb means to be shaken or stirred up, and it is used of anger: "If a wise man goes to law with a foolish man, there is raging and laughing, and no quiet" (Proverbs 29:9); "your raging against me" (2 Kings 19:27-28). The brothers have turned on one another before over Joseph: "Did I not say to you, saying, 'Do not sin against the child'? But you did not listen" (42:22). Joseph has just told them, "let there be no anger in your eyes that you sold me here" (45:5), he has just given Benjamin far more than the rest (45:22), and they are on their way to tell their father what they did.
other: In the only other places where the books of Moses have this verb or a word made from it, it is shaking with fear: "Peoples have heard; they tremble" (Exodus 15:14); "they will tremble and writhe because of you" (Deuteronomy 2:25); "a trembling heart" (Deuteronomy 28:65). The brothers were afraid on the way before: "they turned to one another trembling" (42:28, another verb), and the last time they left the city they were overtaken on the way (44:4-6); they were "dismayed before him" only now (45:3).
review: open
