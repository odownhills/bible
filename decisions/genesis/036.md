## 36:2 the daughter of Zibeon the Hivite | the son of Zibeon the Hivite
kind: copies differ
call: clear
why: The Hebrew text reads "daughter" here and again at 36:14, and in the Old Testament the main text is always that text. The word does not have to make Anah a woman: it can be a second description of Oholibamah, set beside the first, since "son" and "daughter" are also said of a grandchild. This chapter counts Adah's grandsons as "the sons of Adah" (36:12, 16) and Basemath's as "the sons of Basemath" (36:13, 17); Laban is "the son of Nahor" (29:5), though his father was "Bethuel the son of Nahor" (24:47); and Athaliah is called "the daughter of Omri" (2 Kings 8:26), though her father was Ahab, Omri's son (2 Kings 8:18).
other: The Samaritan copy and the old Greek have "son" here and at 36:14, which says outright what the chapter says further on: Anah is one of "the sons of Zibeon", the one who was grazing the donkeys "of Zibeon his father" (36:24). A reader of the Hebrew text alone could take Anah for a woman.

## 36:24 the hot springs | the mules, or the Emim
kind: two senses
call: ask
why: The Hebrew word stands only here in Scripture, so no other verse fixes its meaning. In form it is close to the Hebrew words for "sea" and "water", and water is what a herdsman in dry country is remembered for finding: "And Isaac's servants dug in the valley and found there a well of living water" (26:19); "We have found water" (26:32); "they went three days in the wilderness and found no water" (Exodus 15:22). The word is not the usual one for a spring (16:7) or for a well (26:19). Scripture cannot confirm that these springs were hot: that part of the meaning comes from how the word was understood in ancient times, not from another verse. If the main text should say only what Scripture can support, it would be "the springs".
other: "The mules" rests on the sentence itself, which sets the find beside the donkeys he was grazing; but Scripture has another word wherever it speaks of a mule (2 Samuel 13:29; 1 Kings 1:33), and a mule is bred, not found. The Samaritan copy has "the Emim", a people Scripture names beside the Horites of Seir (14:5-6; Deuteronomy 2:10-12); the name is written almost the same way, and "found" is also said of coming upon an enemy: "they found Adoni-Bezek in Bezek and fought against him" (Judges 1:5).
review: open

## 36:26 the sons of Dishan | the sons of Dishon
kind: copies differ
call: clear
why: The Hebrew text of Genesis reads "Dishan" here, and in the Old Testament the main text is always that text.
other: Seir's sons are "Lotan and Shobal and Zibeon and Anah and Dishon and Ezer and Dishan" (36:20-21), and the verses that follow take them in that order: Lotan (36:22), Shobal (36:23), Zibeon (36:24), Anah (36:25), then this verse, then Ezer (36:27) and Dishan (36:28). So this verse stands in Dishon's place; as it is written, Dishan's sons are given twice, with different names, and Dishon's not at all. Where the list is repeated the Hebrew text itself reads "And the sons of Dishon were Hamran and Eshban and Ithran and Cheran" (1 Chronicles 1:41), and other old copies have "Dishon" here. In Hebrew writing the two names are almost the same.

## 36:40 chief Timna | the chief of Timna
kind: grammar
call: clear
why: The list is introduced as "the names of the chiefs of Esau ... by their names", so what follows are the chiefs' own names. In the earlier lists the same word stands before the names of men who have just been listed as sons: "the sons of Eliphaz were Teman, Omar, Zepho and Gatam and Kenaz" (36:11), then "chief Teman, chief Omar, chief Zepho, chief Kenaz" (36:15), and the same with the sons of Seir (36:20-21, 29-30). Two of those men, Kenaz and Teman, stand in this list as well (36:42).
other: In Hebrew "chief Timna" and "the chief of Timna" are written the same way. This list is given "according to their families, according to their places" and ends "according to the places where they lived in the land of their holding" (36:43), which fits the names of clans and their places. Timna and Oholibamah are women in this chapter (36:12, 22; 36:2, 25), and Oholibamah's name already stands for a group: "These are the chiefs of Oholibamah" (36:18). Teman is a place elsewhere: "the land of the Temanites" (36:34); "the inhabitants of Teman" (Jeremiah 49:20).
review: open
