## 33:15 Why is that? Let me find favor in the eyes of my lord | Why is it that I should find favor in the eyes of my lord?
kind: grammar
call: close
why: The Hebrew can stop after "Why is that?" and then go on with a wish. The words that follow are the set wish of one who speaks to a greater man, and they stand by themselves elsewhere: Shechem says in the next chapter, "Let me find favor in your eyes, and whatever you say to me I will give" (34:11); the Egyptians say to Joseph, "May we find favor in the eyes of my lord" (47:25); Ruth says to Boaz, "May I find favor in your eyes, my lord" (Ruth 2:13). Jacob has already said that this is all he is after: "To find favor in the eyes of my lord" (33:8; 32:5). Where he speaks of favor already found, the verb has another form: "If I have found favor in your eyes" (33:10). And "Why is that?" is what turns the offer down, which is how Esau takes it: he returns that day (33:16).
other: Elsewhere in Genesis the two Hebrew words "why" and "this" open one question that runs straight on into its verb: "Why is it that you ask my name?" (32:29); "Why is it that Sarah laughed" (18:13). Read that way here, Jacob asks why his lord should be so kind to him, as Ruth asks Boaz, "Why have I found favor in your eyes?" (Ruth 2:10).
review: open

## 33:18 came safely to the city of Shechem | came to Salem, a city of Shechem
kind: two senses
call: clear
why: The Hebrew word is "whole", "safe", "at peace"; the same letters are also the place name Salem. Jacob vowed at Bethel, "If God will be with me and watch over me on this way that I am going ... and I return in peace to my father's house, then Yahweh will be God to me" (28:20-21), and God told him, "Return to the land of your fathers and to your relatives, and I will be with you" (31:3). This verse says that it has happened: he is back in the land of Canaan, unharmed by Laban and by Esau, and he names his altar "God, the God of Israel" (33:20). The same word is said of Jacob and his sons in the next chapter by Hamor and Shechem, at the gate of this very city: "These men are at peace with us" (34:21). And the city is Shechem itself, not a town near it: Abram came "as far as the place of Shechem" (12:6), and Joseph's bones were buried "at Shechem, in the plot of the field that Jacob had bought from the sons of Hamor, the father of Shechem, for a hundred kesitahs" (Joshua 24:32). The Samaritan copy has "in peace" here.
other: Salem is the name of a city: "Melchizedek king of Salem" (14:18); "His shelter is in Salem" (Psalm 76:2). After "came", a place name is what a reader expects, and read as "safely" the Hebrew has no word for "to" before "the city of Shechem". Shechem is also a man in this passage, "Hamor, the father of Shechem" (33:19), so "a city of Shechem" can be a town that belonged to him. The old Greek and Latin versions read it so.
review: open
