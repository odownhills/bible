## 23:6 a chieftain of God | a mighty chieftain
kind: two senses
call: clear
why: The Hebrew words are "a chieftain of God", and in this book the people of the land speak of Abraham and his house by naming their God. Abimelech said to him, "God is with you in all that you do" (21:22), and Melchizedek said, "Blessed is Abram by God Most High" (14:19). Later Abimelech says to Isaac, "We have seen plainly that Yahweh is with you" and "You are now the blessed of Yahweh" (26:28-29), which is a title of the same shape on a foreigner's lips. The same word "chieftain" is used of the twelve that God promised to Ishmael (17:20), and God himself has called Abraham "a prophet" before a foreign king (20:7).
other: Hebrew can add "of God" to a thing to say that it is very great: "Nineveh was a great city to God, a journey of three days" (Jonah 3:3); a great panic "became a trembling of God" (1 Samuel 14:15); "Your righteousness is like the mountains of God" (Psalm 36:6); and Rachel says, "With wrestlings of God I have wrestled with my sister" (30:8). Read this way the sons of Heth say nothing about God. They only answer Abraham's "I am a resident foreigner and a settler among you" (23:4) by calling him a great chieftain.
