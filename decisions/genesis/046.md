## 46:13 Iob | Jashub
kind: copies differ
call: clear
why: The Hebrew text of Genesis reads "Iob" here, and in the Old Testament the main text is always that text. (It is not the name of Job of the land of Uz, which is written with another letter in Hebrew.) Genesis names this son of Issachar nowhere else, so nothing in the book can confirm the name or correct it.
other: Where the families of Issachar are counted in the wilderness, the Hebrew text itself has "Jashub" in the same place in the list, between Puvah and Shimron: "of Jashub, the family of the Jashubites" (Numbers 26:24), and 1 Chronicles 7:1 has nearly the same name there. The Samaritan copy and the old Greek have "Jashub" here too. In Hebrew the two names differ by one letter, so one came from the other in copying.
review: open

## 46:27 seventy | seventy-five
kind: copies differ
call: clear
why: The Hebrew text reads "seventy", and in the Old Testament the main text is always that text. The chapter's own counts come to it both ways: thirty-three (46:15), sixteen (46:18), fourteen (46:22) and seven (46:25) are seventy, and the sixty-six of 46:26 with Jacob, Joseph and Joseph's two sons are seventy. Moses says the same: "As seventy people your fathers went down to Egypt" (Deuteronomy 10:22), and the Hebrew text of Exodus 1:5 has "seventy people".
other: The old Greek has "seventy-five" here and at Exodus 1:5, and in this verse it says that the sons of Joseph born to him in Egypt were nine people, not two: at 46:20 it has five more names, a son and a grandson of Manasseh and two sons and a grandson of Ephraim (the men of Numbers 26:29 and 26:35-36). Stephen gives that number: "And Joseph sent and called for Jacob his father and all his relatives, seventy-five people in all" (Acts 7:14).
review: open

## 46:28 to show the way ahead of him to Goshen | to appear before him in Goshen
kind: copies differ
call: clear
why: The Hebrew text has the verb for pointing a thing out and for teaching: "And Yahweh showed him a tree" (Exodus 15:25); "Teach me, and I myself will be silent" (Job 6:24). In the Old Testament the main text is always that text, and it fits what is happening: Joseph had named the place, "you will live in the land of Goshen" (45:10), Jacob is coming with wagons, little ones and herds into a land he has never seen, and the verse ends, "and they came to the land of Goshen."
other: The Samaritan copy has a word written with almost the same letters, "to appear before him", and the old Greek has "to meet him". That is what the next verse tells, with the same verb: Joseph "went up to meet Israel his father, to Goshen, and he appeared to him" (46:29).
review: open
