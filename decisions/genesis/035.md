## 35:7 there God had revealed himself to him | there the divine beings had revealed themselves to him
kind: grammar
call: clear
why: The chapter opens with God sending Jacob to this place to make an altar "to the God who appeared to you when you fled from Esau your brother" (35:1), and this verse says the same thing again in its own words, down to "when he fled from his brother"; Jacob calls him "the God who answered me on the day of my distress" (35:3). At Bethel it was Yahweh who stood beside Jacob and spoke to him (28:13), and the one who later said to him "I am the God of Bethel" (31:13) is one, as is the name Jacob gives the place here, "God of Bethel". The Hebrew word for "God" is plural in form, and in a few places its verb is plural too where only the one God can be meant: "when God made me wander from my father's house" (20:13); "whom God went to redeem as a people for himself" (2 Samuel 7:23).
other: The verb is plural, "they revealed themselves", which is the ordinary way of speaking of more than one. What Jacob saw at this place, before Yahweh spoke, was a stairway, "and look, angels of God were going up and coming down on it" (28:12), and on his way back "angels of God met him" (32:1). The same Hebrew word names beings of the heavenly kind as well as God: "in the midst of the gods he judges" (Psalm 82:1). Read this way the verse says that this is where those of heaven showed themselves to him.
