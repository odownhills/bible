## 15:1 your reward will be very great | I am a shield to you, your very great reward
kind: grammar
call: close
why: Abram answers at once as a man who has been promised something that is still to be given: "Lord Yahweh, what will you give me, since I go childless?" (15:2), and what Yahweh then gives him is an heir and offspring like the stars (15:4-5). Everywhere else in Scripture this word "reward" is a thing that is given or paid, never God himself: "God has given my reward" (30:18); "sons are an inheritance from Yahweh, the fruit of the womb is a reward" (Psalm 127:3), which is the very thing Abram asks about; "there is a reward for your work" (Jeremiah 31:16). The Samaritan copy of Genesis writes "I will multiply your reward greatly" here.
other: The Hebrew has no "is" or "will be", and its words can be heard as one sentence with two names for Yahweh: "I am a shield to you, your very great reward". Abram has just refused the possessions of Sodom and sworn by "Yahweh, God Most High, owner of heaven and earth" that he will take nothing, "so that you will not say, 'I am the one who made Abram rich'" (14:22-23), and "After these things" (15:1) ties this word to that refusal: Yahweh himself in the place of the riches. Scripture speaks of Yahweh this way elsewhere, though with other words than "reward": "I am your portion and your inheritance" (Numbers 18:20); "Yahweh is his inheritance" (Deuteronomy 10:9); "Yahweh is my allotted portion and my cup" (Psalm 16:5).
review: open

## 15:2 the one who will get my house | the one in charge of my house
kind: two senses
call: close
why: The Hebrew word stands only here in Scripture, so the sentences around it have to say what it means. Abram says the same thing again in his next words: "to me you have given no offspring, and look, one of my household is to be my heir" (15:3), and Yahweh answers about one man, "This one will not be your heir" (15:4): the man just named is the man who would get the house. A word made from the same letters stands once more in Scripture, of land taken over by weeds: "a possession of nettles" (Zephaniah 2:9).
other: Since the word stands only here, it may name what Eliezer already is and not what he will get. Abraham's house did have such a man: "his servant, the elder of his house, who governed all that he had" (24:2), and so did Potiphar's, who took Joseph and "put him in charge of his house" (39:4). Read this way, Abram says two things, one after the other: the man who runs his house is a man from Damascus (15:2), and so one of his household, not a son, will be his heir (15:3). The old Latin version has "the son of the steward of my house" here.
review: open
