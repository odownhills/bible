## 31:43 what can I do for these today | what can I do to these today
kind: two senses
call: close
why: The Hebrew small word after "do" is "for" or "to". The sentence is built like Isaac's to Esau, with the person set first: "And for you, then, what can I do, my son?" (27:37), and Jacob has said to Laban, "when will I too do something for my household?" (30:30). Laban goes straight on to the one thing he can still do for his daughters: "And now, come, let us make a covenant" (31:44), and its terms guard them: "If you afflict my daughters, and if you take wives in addition to my daughters" (31:50).
other: The same verb with the same small word is harm done to someone in this chapter and before it: "I have seen all that Laban is doing to you" (31:12); "What is this you have done to me?" (29:25). Laban has just said, "It is in the power of my hand to do you harm" (31:29), and Jacob has said he was afraid: "You might seize your daughters from me" (31:31). Read this way Laban says that he would do nothing against daughters and their sons, who are his own.
review: open

## 31:53 the god of Nahor, the gods of their father | the God of Nahor, the God of their father
kind: grammar
call: ask
why: The Hebrew word is the same for "God" and "gods", and here its verb, "judge", is plural, as it is when gods are meant: "These are your gods, Israel, who brought you up from the land of Egypt" (Exodus 32:4). Everywhere else in this chapter the verb with God is singular: "God came to Laban" (31:24), "God has seen" (31:42), "May Yahweh keep watch" (31:49). Scripture says that the father of these two served other gods: "Your fathers lived beyond the River long ago, Terah the father of Abraham and the father of Nahor, and they served other gods" (Joshua 24:2). Laban is of Nahor's house ("Laban the son of Nahor", 29:5) and has gods of his own: "Why did you steal my gods?" (31:30). And Jacob does not swear by Laban's words: he "swore by the Dread of his father Isaac" (31:53), the name he has just set beside "the God of Abraham" for the God of his own father (31:42).
other: A plural verb stands a few times where only the one God is meant: "when God made me wander" (20:13); "there God had revealed himself to him" (35:7). Laban knows Yahweh by name and speaks of him in this same speech: "May Yahweh keep watch between me and you" (31:49); "God is witness between me and you" (31:50); "From Yahweh the matter has come" (24:50). Read this way he calls on the one God whom both families' fathers knew. The old Greek has the verb in the singular, as the Samaritan copy of the Hebrew does, and it does not have the words "the God of their father".
review: open
