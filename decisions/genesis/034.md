## 34:7 came in from the field when they heard, and the men were pained | came in from the field. When they heard, the men were pained
kind: grammar
call: close
why: In the Hebrew the words "when they heard" stand between "came in from the field" and "the men were pained", and can go with either. The Hebrew text as it has been handed down marks its pauses so that they go with "came in from the field", and the main text follows that text, as the rules say it always does. Read so, the news reached the sons out in the field and brought them home; their coming is set beside Hamor's ("Hamor the father of Shechem went out to Jacob", 34:6), and in the next verse he is speaking "with them" (34:8).
other: Elsewhere in Genesis the same form opens its sentence and the verb comes after it: "When Esau heard the words of his father, he cried out" (27:34); "when he saw the ring ... and when he heard the words of Rebekah his sister ... he came to the man" (24:30). Jacob "kept silent until they came" (34:5), which is easiest to follow if they heard it from him when they came. The old Greek and Latin versions divide the verse this way.
review: open

## 34:7 in Israel | against Israel
kind: two senses
call: clear
why: The small Hebrew word is "in" and also "against". The same charge is made in the same words later in Scripture, and there Israel is the people among whom the thing was done: "because she has done an outrage in Israel by whoring in her father's house" (Deuteronomy 22:21); "because he has done an outrage in Israel" (Joshua 7:15); "for they have done a shameful act and an outrage in Israel" (Judges 20:6). Tamar says both halves of this verse to Amnon: "such a thing is not done in Israel; do not do this outrage" (2 Samuel 13:12), where it can only be "in".
other: When this was done, Israel was one man: "Your name will no longer be said to be Jacob, but Israel" (32:28). The verse itself goes on, "by lying with the daughter of Jacob", so the wrong it speaks of was done to him, and the same small word is "against" in "his hand against everyone and everyone's hand against him" (16:12).
review: open

## 34:10 trade in it | move about in it
kind: two senses
call: clear
why: The Hebrew verb is the one that the word "merchant" comes from: "current among the merchants" (23:16); "Midianite men, merchants, passed by" (37:28). What Hamor offers and what he hopes for is property: "take holdings in it" (34:10), and to his own city, "Their herds and what they have acquired and all their livestock—will they not be ours?" (34:23). Shechem goes straight on to a price: "Put on me a very great bride price and gift" (34:12).
other: The verb's plain sense is to go around: "for both prophet and priest have gone about to a land, and they do not know" (Jeremiah 14:18). Where it comes back in Genesis it follows a charge of spying, so that it can be heard there as leave to go about freely: "then I will know that you are not spies ... and you may go about in the land" (42:34). Hamor has just said, "the land will be before you" (34:10), as Abimelech said to Abraham, "Look, my land is before you. Live where it is good in your eyes" (20:15).
review: open

## 34:25 came against the city in security | came against the city while it felt secure
kind: grammar
call: close
why: The one Hebrew word "in security" stands after "came against the city", with nothing to say whose security it is. Elsewhere it goes with the one who does the verb: "you will live on the land in security" (Leviticus 25:18); "Whoever walks in blamelessness walks in security" (Proverbs 10:9). The verse has just said why the two brothers had nothing to fear: it was the third day, "when they were in pain". Where the word is meant of the side that is attacked, the text gives it a sentence of its own: "and he struck down the camp, and the camp was in security" (Judges 8:11).
other: The men of the city had taken the brothers at their word: "These men are at peace with us" (34:21). The word is said of the one who is attacked in "to make Cush, in its security, tremble" (Ezekiel 30:9), and a word from the same root is said of Laish when the sons of Dan came against it, "against a people quiet and secure" (Judges 18:27).
review: open
