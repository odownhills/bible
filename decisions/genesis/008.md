## 8:21 for what is formed in the heart of humankind is evil from their youth | though what is formed in the heart of humankind is evil from their youth
kind: two senses
call: ask
why: The small Hebrew word that joins the two statements most often gives a reason, and wherever else it stands between two statements in the story of the flood it gives the reason for the first: "for I regret that I made them" (6:7); "for all flesh had ruined its way on the earth" (6:12); "for it is you I have seen to be righteous before me in this generation" (7:1); "for there was water on the face of the whole earth" (8:9). Scripture does give what human beings are as God's reason for holding back: "As a father has compassion on children, Yahweh has compassion on those who fear him, for he himself knows how we are formed; he remembers that we are dust" (Psalm 103:13-14, where "how we are formed" is the Hebrew word that is "what is formed" here); "he, being compassionate, atoned for guilt and did not ruin ... and he remembered that they were flesh, a wind that goes and does not return" (Psalm 78:38-39, with the verb "ruin" of Genesis 6:13); "do not come into judgment with your servant, for no one living is righteous before you" (Psalm 143:2); "For I will not contend forever, and I will not always be angry; for the spirit would grow faint before me" (Isaiah 57:16). The words also differ from those of 6:5, which told of that generation: "everything", "only" and "all day long" are not said again, and "from their youth" is new. That is how one speaks of what people are from the start, which is a reason to bear with them.
other: The same word can mean "though": "God did not lead them by the way of the land of the Philistines, though it was near" (Exodus 13:17); Israel laid his right hand on the head of Ephraim, the younger, "though Manasseh was the firstborn" (Genesis 48:14). What is said here of the human heart is what Yahweh saw before the flood, and there it was the reason for the flood: "everything formed in the thoughts of their heart was only evil all day long ... 'I will wipe humankind, whom I created, off the face of the ground'" (6:5-7). The same thing can hardly be the reason for the flood and the reason that there will be no second one. Read as "though", the words grant that the flood has not changed humankind, and Yahweh makes his promise all the same; what moves him is named just before: "Yahweh smelled the soothing aroma". The words can also be taken as spelling out "because of humankind" (he will not bring a curse on the ground again on this account, that their heart is evil), which comes to the same thing. Moses prays in this way with the same word, after Yahweh had given the people's stubbornness as his reason for not going with them: "I will not go up among you, for you are a stiff-necked people" (Exodus 33:3); "let the Lord go among us, though it is a stiff-necked people, and forgive our guilt and our sin" (Exodus 34:9). It is marked "ask" because the two readings give opposite answers to why Yahweh spares the earth, because of what humankind is or in spite of it, and Scripture speaks in both ways.
