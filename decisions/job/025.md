## 25:3 And on whom does his light not rise | And against whom does his ambush not rise up, as the old Greek version reads
kind: copies differ
call: close
why: The Hebrew text has "his light", and light is what the lines on both sides are about. Job has just spoken of "those who rebel against the light", who "do not know the light" (24:13, 16), and Bildad goes straight on to the moon that does not shine and the stars that are not pure "in his eyes" (25:5). Light is Bildad's own word: "the light of the wicked is snuffed out" (18:5). "Rise" is said of what grows bright in Zophar's "life will rise brighter than noon" (11:17). And Scripture says elsewhere that God's light reaches everyone: he "brings the shadow of death out to the light" (12:22); "even darkness is not dark to you, and night shines like the day" (Psalm 139:12); "he makes his sun rise on the evil and on the good" (Matthew 5:45).
other: The old Greek version has "ambush" where the Hebrew text has "his light"; the two words look much alike in Hebrew writing. The first half of the line is about his troops, and "rise" is what Scripture says of an ambush: "the ambush rose quickly from its place" (Joshua 8:19); "lies in ambush for him and rises against him" (Deuteronomy 19:11, where "rises against" is the same two Hebrew words as "rise on" here). Job has said this of God: "His troops come together; they build up their way against me and camp on every side of my tent" (19:12, the same word for troops). Then Bildad asks whom God's forces cannot reach, and the line says nothing of light.
review: open
