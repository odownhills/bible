## 3:5 reclaim it | defile it
kind: two senses
call: clear
why: The verb is the one for a next of kin who takes back what belongs to the family (Leviticus 25:25; Ruth 4:4-6), and Job uses it that way: "my redeemer lives" (19:25). Darkness was there before the first day (Genesis 1:2-5), so Job calls on it to take this day back as its own, and the lines that follow say the same kind of thing: a cloud is to "settle on it", and deep darkness is to "take" the night (3:6).
other: A second Hebrew verb with the same letters means "defile" (Isaiah 59:3; Lamentations 4:14; Malachi 1:7), which would give "let darkness and the shadow of death defile it". But that verb is never found elsewhere in the simple form that stands here, and Job does not use it anywhere.
review: open

## 3:5 let them terrify it like the bitter things of a day | let all that blackens the day terrify it
kind: two senses
call: ask
why: As the Masoretic Text writes the word, it is most simply read as "like" joined to "bitter things" (the ancient Latin version also read "bitterness"), and the main text keeps to the Masoretic vowels. Bitterness is a word of Job's, used later in this same speech ("those bitter in soul", 3:20; also 7:11; 10:1; 27:2); Amos 8:10 speaks of an end "like a bitter day", and Deuteronomy 32:24 has a closely related word for a "bitter" plague. The darkness of the lines around it is still there on this reading: "them" is the darkness, the shadow of death and the cloud.
other: The same letters can also be read as a single word, "blackenings": whatever turns a day black, such as an eclipse (Amos 8:9). That suits the lines around it, which are all darkness ("let it be darkness", 3:4; "darkness and the shadow of death", "a cloud", 3:5; "deep darkness", 3:6), and it gives the verb a subject of its own, as the two clauses before it have. Lamentations 5:10 may use the same root of skin turned black like an oven. No other place in Scripture has this word.
review: open

## 3:6 rejoice among the days of the year | be joined to the days of the year
kind: two senses
call: close
why: With the vowels the Masoretic Text gives it, the verb is "rejoice", the same verb as in "Jethro rejoiced" (Exodus 18:9), and the main text keeps to the Masoretic vowels. Joy is what Job goes on to refuse this night in the next verse: "let no cry of joy come into it" (3:7).
other: With other vowels the same letters are "be joined", as in "let my glory not be joined to their assembly" (Genesis 49:6). That matches the second half of the line, "let it not come into the number of the months", and the ancient Latin version read it that way ("let it not be counted among the days of the year").
review: open

## 3:14 who rebuilt ruins for themselves | who built for themselves what are now ruins
kind: two senses
call: clear
why: Everywhere else in Scripture, to "build ruins" is to build them up again: Isaiah 58:12 and 61:4 (rebuilding "the ancient ruins"), Ezekiel 36:10, 33 ("the ruins will be rebuilt"), Malachi 1:4 ("we will rebuild the ruins"). It also matches the next line, which names what the princes did while they lived: they "filled their houses with silver" (3:15).
other: The Hebrew says only "who built ruins for themselves", so it can be heard as bitter: everything these kings built for themselves is ruins now, and they lie dead. The lines that follow are about death bringing all alike to the same place: "Small and great are there" (3:19).
review: open

## 3:24 comes before my bread | comes like my bread
kind: two senses
call: close
why: "Before" is what this Hebrew word means nearly everywhere, in Job too: "before any other plant it withers" (8:12), "brought forth before the hills" (15:7). So Job's sighing comes first, before he can eat.
other: In a few places the word shades into "as" or "like": "do not take your servant for a worthless woman" (1 Samuel 1:16), and perhaps "crushed like a moth" (Job 4:19). The second half of the line says "like water", and grief is called food and drink elsewhere: "my tears have been my bread day and night" (Psalm 42:3); "I eat ashes like bread and mix my drink with weeping" (Psalm 102:9).
review: open
