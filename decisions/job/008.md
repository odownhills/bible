## 8:19 from the dust others sprout | from other dust they sprout
kind: grammar
call: ask
why: The line before has him swallowed up from his place, and the place says, "I have not seen you" (8:18); Job has just said of the dead, "his place no longer recognizes him" (7:10). So what follows is others growing where he stood, as God "shatters the mighty ... and sets others in their place" (34:24). The friends use a rooted plant as a picture of the wicked who do not last: "I myself have seen a fool taking root, and suddenly I put a curse on his home" (5:3), and Bildad again, "his roots dry up below ... his memory perishes from the earth" (18:16-17). Psalm 37:35-36 tells the same story: a wicked man spreading like a green tree, "and he passed away, and look, he was no more." The gladness of such a man is short: "the joy of the godless is for a moment" (20:5). The speech ends, "the tent of the wicked will be no more" (8:22).
other: In the Hebrew "dust" and "other" stand side by side, and those two words together are "other plaster" in Leviticus 14:42. "Other" is singular and "sprout" is plural; when another person takes a man's place the verb is singular ("let me sow and another eat", 31:8), and "others" has its plural form (34:24). So "they" can be the plant's own roots and shoots (8:16-17): torn from its place, it comes up again in other soil. Job draws that picture of a tree, with the same word for shoots: "if it is cut down, it will sprout again, and its shoots will not cease" (14:7). Then 8:16-19 is a second plant, "full of sap before the sun", set against the papyrus that withers (8:11-15), like the tree planted by water that "does not fear when heat comes" (Jeremiah 17:8). On this reading "the gladness of his way" is meant in earnest, as Bildad promises Job laughter (8:21), and 8:20 names the two plants: "a blameless man" and "evildoers".
