## 23:2 my complaint is defiance | my complaint is bitter
kind: copies differ
call: close
why: The Hebrew text has the word for standing against someone's word, as in "For I myself know your defiance and your stiff neck" (Deuteronomy 31:27) and "defiance is like the sin of divination" (1 Samuel 15:23). That is what the friends have made of Job's complaint: "you turn your spirit against God and bring out words from your mouth" (15:13); "If you return to the Almighty, you will be built up" (22:23). "Even today" fits it: after all three have spoken, he still does not give way, and he goes straight on to say that he would "set out my case before him, and fill my mouth with arguments" (23:4).
other: The old Syriac and Latin versions have "bitter", a word of almost the same letters, and Job has said that of his complaint twice: "I will complain in the bitterness of my soul" (7:11); "I will abandon myself to my complaint; I will speak in the bitterness of my soul" (10:1). The second half of the line, with its sighing, goes well with it.
review: open

## 23:2 my hand is heavy on my sighing | his hand is heavy on my sighing
kind: copies differ
call: close
why: The Hebrew text has "my hand", and the main text always follows it. It can be understood: a hand is what a person lays on his own mouth to keep it still, "put your hand on your mouth" (21:5; again at 40:4), and Job has said that keeping still does not help him: "If I say, 'I will forget my complaint'" (9:27); "if I cease, what of it goes from me?" (16:6). So his hand weighs on his sighing, and the complaint breaks out all the same.
other: The old Greek and Syriac versions have "his hand", one letter different, and a hand that is heavy on a person is God's hand everywhere else in Scripture: "day and night your hand was heavy on me" (Psalm 32:4); "the hand of Yahweh was heavy on the people of Ashdod" (1 Samuel 5:6). Job says it of himself: "the hand of God has touched me" (19:21); "Put your palm far away from me" (13:21). And the chapter ends with what God has done to him (23:16).
review: open

## 23:12 more than what is allotted to me | within me, as the old Greek and Latin versions read
kind: copies differ
call: close
why: The Hebrew text has "more than" with the word that returns two lines later, "what is allotted to me" (23:14). Scripture uses this word for a person's regular share of food: "feed me with the bread allotted to me" (Proverbs 30:8); "the priests had an allowance from Pharaoh, and they ate their allowance" (Genesis 47:22). And Scripture sets the words of God's mouth above food: "a human being does not live on bread alone, but on everything that comes out of the mouth of Yahweh" (Deuteronomy 8:3); "How sweet your word is to my palate, more than honey to my mouth" (Psalm 119:103).
other: The old Greek and Latin versions have "in my bosom", one letter different. That is how Scripture speaks of keeping God's words, with this same verb: "In my heart I have hidden away your word" (Psalm 119:11); "hide away my commandments with you" (Proverbs 2:1). And it would answer Eliphaz word for word: "put his words in your heart" (22:22).
review: open

## 23:13 he is one | he is of one mind
kind: grammar
call: close
why: The Hebrew is "he is in one". The small word "in" can mark what someone is: "I appeared to Abraham, to Isaac and to Jacob as God Almighty" (Exodus 6:3); "the God of my father was my help" (Exodus 18:4). So the words say that he is one, as in "Yahweh our God, Yahweh is one" (Deuteronomy 6:4). That there is no other is why no one can turn him back: "there is no one who rescues from my hand; I act, and who can turn it back?" (Isaiah 43:13). Job has said the same: "For he is not a man like me ... There is no arbiter between us" (9:32-33); "who can turn him back? Who will say to him, 'What are you doing?'" (9:12).
other: "In one" can also be heard as "in one purpose", and the lines that follow are about his will, which nothing changes: "What his soul desires, he does. For he will carry out what is allotted to me" (23:13-14). Scripture says this of him: "God is not a man, that he should lie, or a human being, that he should change his mind" (Numbers 23:19); "The counsel of Yahweh stands forever" (Psalm 33:11).
review: open

## 23:17 For I was not brought to an end before the darkness, and he did not cover the deep darkness from my face | For it is not because of the darkness that I am brought to an end, nor because deep darkness covers my face
kind: grammar
call: ask
why: Job's first speech has a sentence of the same shape, one "not" for two verbs, with the same thought: "For it did not shut the doors of my mother's womb and hide misery from my eyes" (3:10). He has wished more than once that he had died before he saw trouble: "Why did I not die at birth" (3:11); "I would have breathed my last, and no eye would have seen me" (10:18). The Hebrew for "before" is "from the face of", which is said of one who dies before trouble comes: "the righteous one is taken away from the face of disaster" (Isaiah 57:1). And "cover from" is to keep a thing from someone's sight: "Am I to hide from Abraham what I am going to do" (Genesis 18:17, the same verb). So the line gives the reason for his dread (23:15-16): God let him live to see this darkness.
other: "From the face of" most often gives a cause, and these lines turn on it: "dismayed before him" (23:15) is the same Hebrew. Eliphaz had said that it was dread that "dismays you, or darkness, so that you cannot see, and an abundance of water covers you" (22:10-11). Then Job is answering him with his own words: it is God who has dismayed me (23:16), not the darkness. The second half is harder to put together this way; the Hebrew is "and from my face deep darkness has covered."
review: open
