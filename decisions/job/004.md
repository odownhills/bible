## 4:15 a spirit passed before my face | a wind passed over my face
kind: two senses
call: close
why: What passed then stands still and has an "appearance" and a "form" that Eliphaz cannot make out (4:16), which is said of a being, not of moving air. The same Hebrew word is a spirit that "came out and stood before Yahweh" and spoke in 1 Kings 22:21, and passing "before his face" is how Yahweh passes by Moses (Exodus 34:6; compare 33:19).
other: The Hebrew word is also the ordinary one for wind, and that is its meaning in Job up to here: "a great wind" (1:19), "the wind of his anger" (4:9). A wind goes ahead of God's voice elsewhere: the great wind before the voice Elijah heard, where the rare word translated "hush" in 4:16 comes again (1 Kings 19:11-13), and the storm from which Yahweh answers Job (38:1). On this reading he feels a wind on his face, and what stands before him in 4:16 is left unnamed.
review: open

## 4:17 righteous before God, clean before his maker | more righteous than God, cleaner than his maker
kind: grammar
call: ask
why: The proof that follows is that God finds fault even with his angels, "how much more" with those who live in houses of clay (4:18-19); that shows that no one is in the right in God's eyes, not merely that no one outdoes God. Eliphaz repeats the saying at 15:14-16 ("What is a mortal, that he should be pure? ... the heavens are not pure in his eyes"), Job takes it up as "how can a mortal be righteous with God?" (9:2), and Bildad says the same (25:4-5). The small Hebrew word is the one in "you will be innocent before Yahweh and before Israel" (Numbers 32:22).
other: After "be righteous" this small word normally means "more than": "She is more righteous than I" (Genesis 38:26; so also 1 Samuel 24:17; Ezekiel 16:52). Later in the book this is the very charge against Job: he "justified himself rather than God" (32:2), and "you say, 'My righteousness is more than God's'" (35:2; compare 40:8). Job has just asked why God gives light to one in misery (3:20-23), and the voice may be answering that.
review: open

## 4:19 crushed sooner than a moth | crushed like a moth
kind: two senses
call: close
why: The Hebrew word is "before", and of time it means "sooner than": "before any other plant it withers" (8:12), "brought forth before the hills" (15:7). The next line is about how short their time is: "From morning to evening they are shattered" (4:20). So they are crushed even more quickly than a moth is.
other: In a few places the word may shade into "like" (1 Samuel 1:16; see the entry at Job 3:24), and the ancient Greek version reads "like a moth" here. A moth is a picture of what does not last in "he builds his house like a moth" (27:18). The words can also be heard as "crushed before a moth", as an army falls before its enemy, since elsewhere the moth is the one that destroys (13:28; Isaiah 50:9; Hosea 5:12).
review: open

## 4:21 their tent cord pulled up within them | the abundance that is in them taken away
kind: two senses
call: clear
why: The verb is the one for pulling up tent pegs and breaking camp, and the only other place it stands in this form is about dying: "my dwelling is pulled up and removed from me like a shepherd's tent" (Isaiah 38:12; compare Isaiah 33:20, a tent whose pegs "will never be pulled up"). The lines before have people living in "houses of clay, whose foundation is in the dust" (4:19), and the noun is "cord" again at 30:11 and in Judges 16:7-9.
other: The same letters are also a word for what is over and above, abundance or excellence, and in exactly this form Eliphaz himself uses it that way: "what they had left, fire has devoured" (22:20; also Psalm 17:14; compare Genesis 49:3). That a man's wealth and glory do not go with him when he dies is said in Psalm 49:17.
review: open
