## 15:5 your guilt teaches your mouth | your mouth makes your guilt known
kind: grammar
call: close
why: The Hebrew has "teaches", "your guilt" and "your mouth", with nothing to mark which of the two is the teacher. In the other places where this verb stands in Job, what follows it is the one who is taught: "I will teach you wisdom" (33:33); "who teaches us more than the beasts of the earth" (35:11). So the mouth is the pupil and guilt the teacher; and "your guilt" stands straight after the verb, where a Hebrew sentence usually puts its subject. The second half of the line says the same thing from the other side, a man picking his words: "you choose the tongue of the shrewd". That what is inside a person schools his mouth is said in Proverbs 16:23: "The heart of the wise makes his mouth prudent." And the line opens with "For": it gives the reason why Job does away with fear of God (15:4).
other: The verb can also be followed by the thing that is taught ("wisdom", 33:33), and then the mouth is the teacher: what Job says shows his guilt. That leads straight into the next line, "Your mouth condemns you, and not I, and your lips testify against you" (15:6), where Eliphaz takes up Job's own words, "my mouth would condemn me" (9:20). The mouth is what gives a person away elsewhere in Scripture: "your mouth has testified against you" (2 Samuel 1:16); "A fool's mouth is his ruin" (Proverbs 18:7); "by your words you will be condemned" (Matthew 12:37).
review: open

## 15:23 He wanders about for bread: 'Where is it?' | He wanders about as food for the vulture
kind: copies differ
call: ask
why: With the vowels the Masoretic Text gives it, the last word of the line is "Where?", and the main text keeps to the Masoretic vowels. Eliphaz is telling what the wicked man hears and expects while he is still at peace: "Sounds of dread are in his ears" (15:21); "He does not believe that he will return from darkness" (15:22); "He knows that a day of darkness is ready at his hand" (15:23). Among these the fat and wealthy man (15:27, 29) sees himself hunting for bread. Wandering in search of food is what Scripture says of a wicked man's house: "Let his sons wander about and beg" (Psalm 109:10); "They wander about for food" (Psalm 59:15); and the opposite is said of the righteous: "I have not seen a righteous man abandoned, or his offspring seeking bread" (Psalm 37:25). In his first speech Eliphaz promised the man God corrects the reverse of both lines: "In famine he will ransom you from death ... you will not be afraid of destruction when it comes" (5:20-21). "Where?" is the cry over what is gone, as in "and where is he?" (14:10) and "Where then is my hope?" (17:15).
other: With other vowels the same letters are the name of a bird of prey (Leviticus 11:14; Job 28:7), and the line says that he wanders about as bread for the vulture. The old Greek version reads it that way: "he has been appointed as food for vultures". "Bread" is said of what animals eat (Psalm 147:9; Proverbs 6:8) and of people who are to be devoured (Numbers 14:9). It carries on from the line before, "he is watched for by the sword" (15:22): those who fall by the sword are left as food for the birds (Deuteronomy 28:26; 1 Samuel 17:46; Jeremiah 7:33). And the Hebrew has no word such as "saying" before "Where?"; read this way none is needed.
review: open

## 15:29 their gain will not stretch out over the land | he will not stretch out a shadow over the land
kind: copies differ
call: close
why: The Hebrew word stands only here in Scripture, and in the Old Testament the main text is always the Masoretic Text. The first two parts of the line are about riches, "He will not be rich, and his wealth will not last", so the word is read as what he has gained; it looks like a noun from a verb that stands once, in Isaiah 33:1, for bringing something to its end. Wealth that spreads over the land is how the blessing on Job was described: "his herds have spread out in the land" (1:10). The verb "stretch out" is the one used four lines earlier of this man's hand: "he has stretched out his hand against God" (15:25). "Their" is plural in the Hebrew, as in Eliphaz's first speech: "the snare pants for their wealth" (5:5).
other: The old Greek version has "he will not cast a shadow on the earth". The verb of this line is the one Scripture uses of a shadow growing long: "My days are like a shadow stretched out" (Psalm 102:11; also Psalm 109:23; 2 Kings 20:10). The lines that follow picture the wicked man as a plant, with his shoots (15:30), his frond (15:32) and the vine and the olive tree (15:33), as Bildad pictured him (8:16-17), and a great tree is known by its shade (Ezekiel 31:6; Hosea 14:7). A human life is itself called a shadow on the earth: "our days on earth are a shadow" (8:9).
review: open
