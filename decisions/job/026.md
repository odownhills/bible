## 26:4 To whom have you told words | With whose help have you told words
kind: grammar
call: close
why: In Hebrew the verb "tell" takes the person who is told as its plain object, as in "the number of my steps I would tell him" (31:37) and "tell the house of Israel about the house" (Ezekiel 43:10). The two lines before are about the one Bildad has been speaking to: "the one without strength", "the one without wisdom" (26:2-3). Job has said the same to the friends before: "who does not have such things as these?" (12:3); "What you know, I myself also know" (13:2). And he goes straight on to say more about God's power than Bildad did (26:5-14), which shows that the words were told to someone who had them already.
other: The small word before "whom" also means "with". It is the word in "I have acquired a man with Yahweh" (Genesis 4:1), and Job's "who does not have such things as these?" (12:3) is in Hebrew "with whom are there not such things as these?" The second half of the line asks where Bildad's words came from: "whose breath has come out of you?" Read as "with whose help", the first half asks the same thing. Eliphaz had claimed a source of that kind: "to me a word was brought by stealth" (4:12).
review: open

## 26:5 The dead | The Rephaim, the giant people of old
kind: two senses
call: clear
why: The Hebrew word is the name Rephaim, and Scripture uses it for those who are in the world of the dead: "Is it for the dead that you do wonders? Do the Rephaim rise and praise you?" (Psalm 88:10); "The dead will not live; the Rephaim will not rise" (Isaiah 26:14); "he does not know that the Rephaim are there, that her guests are in the depths of Sheol" (Proverbs 9:18); "Sheol below is in turmoil over you, to meet you when you come; it rouses the Rephaim for you" (Isaiah 14:9). The next line here is "Sheol is naked before him" (26:6).
other: The same name belongs to a people of great size who lived in the land long before: "they struck down the Rephaim in Ashteroth-Karnaim" (Genesis 14:5); the Emim were "a people great and many, and tall like the Anakim; they too are counted as Rephaim, like the Anakim" (Deuteronomy 2:10-11); "only Og king of Bashan was left of the remnant of the Rephaim; look, his bed was a bed of iron ... nine cubits its length" (Deuteronomy 3:11). Read so, Job says that even those mighty ones of old now writhe under the waters. The old Greek version has "giants" here.
review: open

## 26:9 the face of the throne | the face of the full moon
kind: two senses
call: close
why: With the vowels of the Hebrew text the word is "throne", written with another last letter than usual, as it is in "The throne had six steps" (1 Kings 10:19). God's throne is in the heavens, and cloud hides it: "Yahweh—his throne is in the heavens" (Psalm 11:4); "Cloud and thick darkness are around him; righteousness and justice are the foundation of his throne" (Psalm 97:2); "Clouds are a hiding place for him" (Job 22:14). Job has said that he cannot get there: "If only I knew where to find him, that I might come right to his seat!" (23:3). The old Greek version has "throne" too.
other: The same letters with other vowels are the word for the full moon: "Blow the horn at the new moon, at the full moon, for the day of our feast" (Psalm 81:3); "at the day of the full moon he will come home" (Proverbs 7:20). The lines around this one go through what is seen above and below: the north, the earth, the clouds, the waters, light and darkness (26:7-10). And Bildad has just said, "even the moon does not shine" (25:5); a cloud spread over the moon's face is that very sight. No old copy is known to read the word this way.
review: open

## 26:12 he stirred up the sea | he stilled the sea
kind: two senses
call: ask
why: The same two Hebrew words stand in Isaiah 51:15 and Jeremiah 31:35: "who stirs up the sea, and its waves roar; Yahweh of hosts is his name." The roaring waves show which sense is meant there, and that chapter of Isaiah also speaks of Rahab and of piercing the sea monster (Isaiah 51:9), as Job does here. Job has used the verb of his own skin, which "breaks out and runs" (7:5). Scripture says elsewhere that God raises the storm: "He spoke and raised a storm wind, and it lifted its waves" (Psalm 107:25). Read so, the two verses go from storm to calm: he stirred up the sea, and by his wind the heavens are fair (26:13).
other: Another form of this verb means to give rest: "so that he may give rest to the land" (Jeremiah 50:34); "among those nations you will find no rest" (Deuteronomy 28:65). The other half of the line is about a defeat, "he wounded Rahab", and Scripture sets the stilling of the sea beside that defeat: "You rule the swelling of the sea; when its waves rise, you still them. You crushed Rahab like one who is slain" (Psalm 89:9-10); "who stills the roaring of the seas" (Psalm 65:7). Job has spoken of the sea as something God holds down: "Am I the sea, or a sea monster, that you set a guard over me?" (7:12). The old Greek version has "he stilled the sea".
review: open

## 26:13 By his wind | By his spirit
kind: two senses
call: close
why: The line says what makes the heavens fair, and in this book it is wind that clears the sky: "a wind has passed and made them clean" (37:21, of the skies). Where the friends use this word with "his", it is God's wind: "by the wind of his anger they come to an end" (4:9); "he will depart by the wind of his mouth" (15:30). The line before is about the sea, and there Scripture has God's wind at work: "You blew with your wind; the sea covered them" (Exodus 15:10).
other: The word is also "spirit", and it stands here in a row with "his strength", "his understanding" and "his hand" (26:12-13). Scripture says that God's spirit made the heavens and what lives under them: "By the word of Yahweh the heavens were made, and all their host by the spirit of his mouth" (Psalm 33:6, the same word); "The spirit of God made me" (Job 33:4); "the spirit of God was hovering over the face of the waters" (Genesis 1:2). This chapter has other words of that verse: "the waste" (26:7; "desolate" there), "the face of the waters" and "darkness" (26:10).
review: open

## 26:13 his hand pierced | his hand brought forth
kind: two senses
call: close
why: The line before says "he wounded Rahab", and Isaiah has the same pair with this verb: "Was it not you who cut Rahab in pieces, who pierced the sea monster?" (Isaiah 51:9). The snake is one that God strikes: "On that day Yahweh will punish with his hard and great and strong sword Leviathan the fleeing snake, and Leviathan the twisting snake, and he will kill the sea monster that is in the sea" (Isaiah 27:1, the only other place that has "the fleeing snake"); "You crushed the heads of Leviathan" (Psalm 74:14). The old Greek version has "he put to death" here.
other: The same form also belongs to the verb for bringing to birth. Eliphaz used it: "were you brought to birth before the hills?" (15:7); and it is said of God making the world: "Before the mountains were born, and you brought forth the earth and the world" (Psalm 90:2). In Job the hand of God is the hand that makes: "Your hands shaped me and made me" (10:8); "the hand of Yahweh has done this" (12:9). The lines before are about what God has made (26:7-10), and Scripture says that he made the creatures of the sea: "God created the great sea monsters" (Genesis 1:21); "Leviathan, which you formed to play in it" (Psalm 104:26). Another form of that verb is "writhe" in this chapter (26:5).
review: open
