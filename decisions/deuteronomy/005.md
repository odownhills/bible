## 5:7 before my face | besides me
kind: two senses
call: close
why: The Hebrew is "upon my face", and of a person that is said of what is done in front of him, where he sees it: "he will certainly curse you to your face" (Job 1:11); "a people who provoke me to my face continually" (Isaiah 65:3); "Haran died before the face of Terah his father" (Genesis 11:28). The chapter has just said how Yahweh spoke with them, "Face to face" (5:4), and the reason given with this command is "I, Yahweh your God, am a jealous God" (5:9), the jealousy that Scripture speaks of in a husband over his wife (Numbers 5:14): the other gods would be set up in his sight.
other: The same phrase can say that one thing is put on top of another, or over it: "He breaches me, breach upon breach" (Job 16:14); a man may not make the son of the wife he loves his firstborn "over the son of the hated one" (Deuteronomy 21:16). Then the command is that no other god is to be added to Yahweh or set above him, and Scripture says the command again in that way: "I am Yahweh your God from the land of Egypt, and you know no God but me, and there is no savior besides me" (Hosea 13:4); "Yahweh, he is God; there is no other besides him" (Deuteronomy 4:35).
review: open

## 5:10 his commandments | my commandments
kind: copies differ
call: ask
why: The rules follow the written Hebrew text wherever it can be read, and here it can: what is written is "his commandments". Moses says the same thing in the same words two chapters later, speaking of Yahweh as "he": "who keeps the covenant and the faithful love for those who love him and keep his commandments, to a thousand generations" (7:9). And inside the ten words Yahweh is spoken of by name and as "he" in the very next lines: "Yahweh will not hold innocent the one who takes up his name in vain" (5:11); "as Yahweh your God has commanded you" (5:12, 16).
other: The traditional way of reading the verse aloud has "my commandments", and that is what is written where the ten words stand in Exodus: "of those who love me and keep my commandments" (Exodus 20:6). Yahweh says "me" all through this sentence, "those who hate me" (5:9) and "those who love me", and later in the chapter he says "all my commandments" (5:29). It is marked "ask" because the main text here follows the rule for the written text and not the weight of the sentence, and this is the wording that is heard when the ten words are read aloud.
review: open

## 5:11 in vain | for a falsehood
kind: two senses
call: ask
why: The word is "emptiness": "months of emptiness" (Job 7:3); "Unless Yahweh builds the house, its builders labor on it in vain" (Psalm 127:1). With the same small word before it as here, it is "in vain" each time in Jeremiah: "In vain I have struck your sons" (Jeremiah 2:30; also 4:30; 6:29; 46:11). For swearing to a lie the Law has a plainer word: "You must not swear by my name falsely" (Leviticus 19:12). So the wider word here forbids every empty use of the name, the false oath among them.
other: In the Law this word is also used of what is untrue: "a false witness" in this chapter (5:20) is "a lying witness" where the ten words stand in Exodus (20:16), and "You must not take up a false report" (Exodus 23:1) has the same verb as this command. Where Scripture names the ten words again it speaks of swearing falsely: "Will you steal, murder and commit adultery, and swear falsely ...?" (Jeremiah 7:9); "the house of the thief and the house of the one who swears by my name falsely" (Zechariah 5:4). And a psalm sets "who has not lifted up ... to what is false" beside "and has not sworn deceitfully" (Psalm 24:4). Read that way the command is against using the name of Yahweh to back a lie.
review: open

## 5:17 murder | kill
kind: two senses
call: clear
why: This is not the common Hebrew word for killing. Scripture uses it of a life taken unlawfully: "Have you murdered and also taken possession?" (1 Kings 21:19); "they murder on the way to Shechem" (Hosea 6:9); "the murderer must certainly be put to death" (Numbers 35:16). And the same Law commands killing in judgment and in war, with other words: "you must certainly kill him" (Deuteronomy 13:9); "you must strike down all its males with the edge of the sword" (Deuteronomy 20:13). So the command cannot be against every killing.
other: The word is wider than the English "murder". It is used of the one who "kills his neighbor without knowing it, when he did not hate him before" (Deuteronomy 4:42), and the Law uses it once of the avenger of blood, who "has no bloodguilt" (Numbers 35:27), and once of putting a murderer to death on the word of witnesses (Numbers 35:30). Read that way the command forbids taking a human life, and the Law itself then sets out when a life is taken by its judgment.
review: open
