Genesis 2

TV mode Listen only

The seventh day

1And the heavens and the earth were finished, and all their array [alt: all their army]. 2And on the seventh day God finished his work that he had done, and he ceased [alt: rested] on the seventh day from all his work that he had done. [In Hebrew "ceased" sounds like the word "Sabbath."] 3And God blessed the seventh day and made it holy, because on it he ceased from all his work that God had done in creating [alt: that God had created to do].

The human and the garden

4These are the generations [alt: This is the account] of the heavens and the earth when they were created, on the day that Yahweh God made earth and heavens. [alt: when they were created. On the day that Yahweh God made earth and heavens, with the sentence running on into verses 5-7]

5Now no shrub of the field was yet on the earth, and no plant of the field had yet sprouted, because Yahweh God had not made it rain on the earth, and there was no human to work the ground, 6but a spring [alt: a mist] would rise from the earth and water the whole face of the ground. 7Then Yahweh God formed the human out of dust from the ground, and blew into his nostrils the breath of life, and the human became a living creature. [In Hebrew "human" sounds like the word for "ground."]

8And Yahweh God planted a garden in Eden, in the east, and there he put the human he had formed. [In Hebrew "Eden" sounds like the word for "delight."] 9And Yahweh God made every tree that is desirable to look at and good for food sprout from the ground, and the tree of life in the middle of the garden, and the tree of the knowledge of good and bad [alt: good and evil].

The four rivers

10Now a river flows out of Eden to water the garden, and from there it divides and becomes four headwaters. 11The name of the first is Pishon. It is the one that goes around the whole land of Havilah, where there is gold. 12And the gold of that land is good. Bdellium resin and onyx stone [alt: carnelian, or lapis lazuli] are there. 13And the name of the second river is Gihon. It is the one that goes around the whole land of Cush. 14And the name of the third river is Tigris. It is the one that flows east of Asshur [alt: east of Assyria]. And the fourth river is the Euphrates.

The command about the tree

15And Yahweh God took the human and settled him in the garden of Eden to work it and watch over it. 16And Yahweh God commanded the human, saying, "From every tree of the garden you may certainly eat, 17but from the tree of the knowledge of good and bad—you must not eat from it, because on the day you eat from it you will certainly die."

A counterpart for the human

18And Yahweh God said, "It is not good for the human to be alone. I will make for him a helper who is his counterpart." 19And Yahweh God formed from the ground every wild animal of the field and every bird of the sky, and he brought them to the human to see what he would call each one. And whatever the human called each living creature, that was its name. 20And the human gave names to all the livestock, to the birds of the sky, and to every wild animal of the field. But for Adam [alt: for the human] no helper was found who was his counterpart. [In Hebrew the name "Adam" is the word for "human."]

21So Yahweh God made a deep sleep fall on the human, and he slept. And he took one of his ribs [alt: one of his sides] and closed up the flesh in its place. 22And Yahweh God built the rib that he had taken from the human into a woman, and he brought her to the human. 23And the human said, "This one, this time, is bone from my bones and flesh from my flesh. This one will be called 'woman,' because from a man this one was taken." [In Hebrew "woman" sounds like "man," a different word from the one translated "human."]

24That is why a man leaves his father and his mother and clings to his wife, and they become one flesh. [In Hebrew "wife" is the same word as "woman."] 25And the two of them were naked, the human and his wife, and they were not ashamed [alt: were not ashamed in front of each other]. [In Hebrew "naked" sounds like the word that describes the snake in the next verse, 3:1.]
