Job 9

Watch Listen only

Job answers Bildad

1And Job answered and said:

2"Truly I know that it is so; and how can a mortal be righteous with God? 3If he wished to contend with him, he could not answer him one time in a thousand [alt: God would not answer him one time in a thousand]. 4Wise in heart and mighty in strength—who has hardened himself against him and come out whole?

He moves mountains

5"He moves mountains, and they do not know it; he overturns them in his anger. 6He shakes the earth from its place, and its pillars shudder. 7He speaks to the sun, and it does not rise, and he seals up the stars.

8"He stretches out the heavens by himself and treads on the heights of the sea. 9He makes the Bear, Orion and the Pleiades, and the chambers of the south. 10He does great things beyond searching out, and wonders beyond number.

11"Look, he passes by me, and I do not see him; he sweeps on, and I do not discern him. 12Look, he snatches away; who can turn him back? Who will say to him, 'What are you doing?' 13God does not turn back his anger; under him the helpers of Rahab [alt: the helpers of the proud] bowed low.

How could I answer him?

14"How much less could I myself answer him, choose my words with him! 15Though I am righteous, I could not answer him; I would plead with my judge. 16If I called and he answered me, I would not believe that he was listening to my voice, 17he who strikes me with a storm [alt: for a hair] and multiplies my wounds for nothing. 18He does not let me get my breath back, but gives me my fill of bitter things.

19"If it is a matter of strength, look, he is mighty; and if of justice, who will summon me [alt: who will summon him, as the old Greek version takes it]? 20Though I am righteous, my mouth would condemn me; I am blameless, and it would declare me crooked [alt: and he would declare me crooked].

Blameless and wicked alike

21"I am blameless; I do not care about myself; I reject my life. 22It is all one; that is why I say, 'He makes an end of the blameless and the wicked.' 23If a scourge suddenly brings death, he mocks at the testing of the innocent [alt: the despair of the innocent]. 24The earth has been given into the hand of the wicked; he covers the faces of its judges. If not, then who is it?

Swifter than a runner

25"And my days are swifter than a runner; they flee; they do not see good. 26They sweep past like reed boats, like an eagle swooping on food.

27"If I say, 'I will forget my complaint; I will abandon my sad face and be cheerful,' 28I shrink from all my pains; I know that you will not hold me innocent. 29I myself am to be condemned; why then do I toil in vain?

30"If I washed myself with snow and made my palms pure with lye, 31then you would dip me in the pit, and my clothes would detest me.

No arbiter between us

32"For he is not a man like me, that I should answer him, that we should come together into court. 33There is no arbiter between us [alt: If only there were an arbiter between us, as the old Greek version reads], who might lay his hand on us both. 34Let him remove his rod from me, and let the terror of him not terrify me. 35Then I would speak and not fear him; for I am not so in myself.
