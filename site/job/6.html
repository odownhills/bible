Job 6

Job answers Eliphaz

1And Job answered and said:

2"If only my resentment were actually weighed, and my ruin lifted in the scales together with it! 3For then it would be heavier than the sand of the seas; that is why my words have been rash. 4For the arrows of the Almighty are in me; my spirit drinks their poison; the terrors of God are lined up against me.

5"Does a wild donkey bray over grass, or an ox low over its fodder? 6Is what is tasteless eaten without salt, or is there taste in the white of an egg? 7My soul refuses to touch them; they are like sickness in my bread.

If only God would crush me

8"If only what I ask for would come, and God would give what I hope for, 9and God would be willing to crush me, would let loose his hand and cut me off! 10Then I would still have my comfort, and I would leap for joy [alt: recoil, or steel myself] in anguish, though he does not spare; for I have not concealed the words of the Holy One.

11"What is my strength, that I should wait? And what is my end, that I should be patient? 12Is my strength the strength of stones, or is my flesh bronze? 13Is there no help for me in myself, and has success been driven away from me?

Brothers like a torrent

14"One who is losing heart is owed loyalty by his friend, even if he abandons the fear of the Almighty [alt: or that friend abandons the fear of the Almighty]. 15My brothers have been treacherous like a torrent, like the channel of torrents that pass away, 16that are dark with ice, where the snow hides itself. 17At the time they are scorched, they are brought to an end; when it is hot, they are snuffed out from their place. 18The paths of their way wind about; they go up into the waste and perish [alt: Caravans turn aside from their way; they go up into the waste and perish].

19"The caravans of Tema looked; the travelers of Sheba hoped for them. 20They were ashamed because they had trusted; they came to it and were humiliated. 21For you have now become nothing [alt: you have become like it, as the Hebrew is traditionally read aloud]; you see a horror and are afraid. [In Hebrew "you see" and "you are afraid" sound alike.]

22"Have I said, 'Give me something,' or 'Pay a bribe for me out of your means,' 23or 'Deliver me from the hand of a foe,' or 'Ransom me from the hand of the ruthless'?

Teach me, and I will be silent

24"Teach me, and I myself will be silent; and make me understand how I have gone astray. 25How painful are upright words! But what does correction from you correct? 26Do you plan to correct words, and count the sayings of a despairing man as wind [alt: when the sayings of a despairing man are for the wind]? 27You would even cast lots over an orphan, and bargain over your friend.

28"And now, be willing to face me; and to your faces I will certainly not lie. 29Turn back now; let there be no injustice. And turn back again; my righteousness is in this. 30Is there injustice on my tongue, or can my palate not discern what is ruinous?
