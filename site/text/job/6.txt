Job, chapter 6. And Job answered and said: "If only my resentment were actually weighed, and my ruin lifted in the scales together with it! For then it would be heavier than the sand of the seas; that is why my words have been rash. For the arrows of the Almighty are in me; my spirit drinks their poison; the terrors of God are lined up against me. "Does a wild donkey bray over grass, or an ox low over its fodder? Is what is tasteless eaten without salt, or is there taste in the white of an egg? My soul refuses to touch them; they are like sickness in my bread. "If only what I ask for would come, and God would give what I hope for, and God would be willing to crush me, would let loose his hand and cut me off! Then I would still have my comfort, and I would leap for joy in anguish, though he does not spare; for I have not concealed the words of the Holy One. "What is my strength, that I should wait? And what is my end, that I should be patient? Is my strength the strength of stones, or is my flesh bronze? Is there no help for me in myself, and has success been driven away from me? "One who is losing heart is owed loyalty by his friend, even if he abandons the fear of the Almighty. My brothers have been treacherous like a torrent, like the channel of torrents that pass away, that are dark with ice, where the snow hides itself. At the time they are scorched, they are brought to an end; when it is hot, they are snuffed out from their place. The paths of their way wind about; they go up into the waste and perish. "The caravans of Tema looked; the travelers of Sheba hoped for them. They were ashamed because they had trusted; they came to it and were humiliated. For you have now become nothing; you see a horror and are afraid. "Have I said, 'Give me something,' or 'Pay a bribe for me out of your means,' or 'Deliver me from the hand of a foe,' or 'Ransom me from the hand of the ruthless'? "Teach me, and I myself will be silent; and make me understand how I have gone astray. How painful are upright words! But what does correction from you correct? Do you plan to correct words, and count the sayings of a despairing man as wind? You would even cast lots over an orphan, and bargain over your friend. "And now, be willing to face me; and to your faces I will certainly not lie. Turn back now; let there be no injustice. And turn back again; my righteousness is in this. Is there injustice on my tongue, or can my palate not discern what is ruinous?
