Job, chapter 14. "A human being, born of a woman, is short of days and full of turmoil. Like a flower he comes out and withers; he flees like a shadow and does not stay. "Do you open your eye even on such a one, and is it me you bring into judgment with you? Who can bring what is clean out of what is unclean? Not one. If his days are determined, if the number of his months is with you, if you have set his limit and he cannot pass it, look away from him so that he may rest, until, like a hired worker, he completes his day. "For there is hope for a tree: if it is cut down, it will sprout again, and its shoots will not cease. Though its root grows old in the earth and its stump dies in the dust, at the scent of water it will bud and put out branches like a sapling. "But a man dies and is laid low; a human being breathes his last, and where is he? As water is gone from a sea, and a river is drained and dries up, so a man lies down and does not rise; until the heavens are no more, they will not awake, and they will not be roused from their sleep. "If only you would hide me away in Sheol, hide me until your anger turns back, set a limit for me and remember me! If a man dies, will he live? All the days of my soldier's service I would wait, until my relief came. You would call, and I myself would answer you; you would yearn for the work of your hands. For then you would count my steps; you would not keep watch on my sin. My rebellion would be sealed in a bag, and you would plaster over my guilt. "But as a mountain falls and crumbles away, and a rock is moved from its place, as water wears away stones and its floods wash away the dust of the earth, so you destroy the hope of a mortal. You overpower him forever, and he goes; you change his face and send him away. His sons are honored, and he does not know it; they are brought low, and he does not discern it. Only his flesh on him is in pain, and his soul within him mourns."
