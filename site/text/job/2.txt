Job, chapter 2. And one day the sons of God came to present themselves before Yahweh, and the adversary also came among them to present himself before Yahweh. And Yahweh said to the adversary, "From where do you come?" And the adversary answered Yahweh and said, "From roaming the earth and from walking about on it." And Yahweh said to the adversary, "Have you considered my servant Job? For there is no one like him on the earth, a blameless and upright man, who fears God and turns away from evil. And he is still holding fast to his blamelessness, and you incited me against him, to swallow him up for nothing." And the adversary answered Yahweh and said, "Skin for skin! And all that a man has he will give for his life. But reach out your hand now and touch his bone and his flesh; he will certainly curse you to your face." And Yahweh said to the adversary, "Look, he is in your hand; only guard his life." And the adversary went out from the presence of Yahweh, and he struck Job with bad boils from the sole of his foot to the crown of his head. And he took for himself a piece of broken pottery to scrape himself with, while he was sitting among the ashes. And his wife said to him, "Are you still holding fast to your blamelessness? Curse God and die." And he said to her, "You are speaking as one of the foolish women speaks. Shall we accept the good from God and not accept the bad as well?" In all this Job did not sin with his lips. And Job's three friends heard of all this disaster that had come upon him, and they came, each from his place: Eliphaz the Temanite and Bildad the Shuhite and Zophar the Naamathite. And they met together by arrangement to come to grieve with him and to comfort him. And they lifted their eyes from far off and did not recognize him, and they lifted their voice and wept, and each one tore his robe, and they threw dust on their heads, toward the sky. And they sat with him on the ground for seven days and seven nights, and no one spoke a word to him, for they saw that the pain was very great.
