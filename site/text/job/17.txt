Job, chapter 17. "My spirit is ruined; my days are snuffed out; graves are mine. Surely mockers are with me, and my eye dwells on their provocation. "Lay down a pledge now; be my guarantor with yourself. Who is it that will clasp my hand in pledge? For you have hidden their heart away from insight; that is why you will not raise them high. One who informs on friends for a share—the eyes of his sons will fail. "And he has set me as a byword among the peoples, and I am one whose face is spat on. And my eye has grown dim from grief, and all my limbs are like a shadow. "The upright are appalled at this, and the innocent man rouses himself against the godless man. And the righteous man holds to his way, and the one with clean hands grows firmer. "But all of you, come again now, and I will not find a wise man among you. My days have passed; my purposes are broken off, the desires of my heart. They turn night into day: 'Light is near,' in the face of darkness. "If I hope for Sheol as my house, if I have spread my bed in the darkness, if I have called to the pit, 'You are my father,' and to the maggot, 'My mother and my sister,' where then is my hope? And my hope—who will catch sight of it? It will go down to the bars of Sheol, when there is rest together on the dust."
