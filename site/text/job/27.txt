Job, chapter 27. And Job again took up his discourse and said: "As God lives, who has removed my justice, and the Almighty, who has made my soul bitter, for as long as my breath is in me and the spirit of God is in my nostrils, my lips will certainly not speak injustice, and my tongue will not utter deceit. Far be it from me to declare you in the right; until I breathe my last I will not remove my blamelessness from me. I have held fast to my righteousness and will not let go of it; my heart does not insult any of my days. "Let my enemy be like the wicked man, and the one who rises up against me like the unjust man. For what is the hope of the godless man when he is cut off, when God draws out his life? Is it his cry that God will hear when distress comes upon him? Or will he take delight in the Almighty? Will he call on God at all times? I will teach you about the hand of God; what is with the Almighty I will not conceal. Look, you yourselves, all of you, have gazed on it; why then do you breathe out mere breath? "This is the portion of a wicked human being with God, and the inheritance that the ruthless receive from the Almighty: If his sons are many, it is for the sword, and his descendants will not have their fill of bread. His survivors will be buried by the plague, and his widows will not weep. If he heaps up silver like dust and prepares clothing like clay, he prepares it, but the righteous man will wear it, and the innocent man will portion out the silver. He builds his house like a moth, and like a shelter that a watchman makes. "He lies down rich, and will not be gathered; he opens his eyes, and he is no more. Terrors overtake him like water; by night a whirlwind steals him away. The east wind lifts him up, and he is gone, and it sweeps him from his place. And it lets fly at him and does not spare; from its hand he flees headlong. It claps its hands at him and hisses at him from his place.
