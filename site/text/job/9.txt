Job, chapter 9. And Job answered and said: "Truly I know that it is so; and how can a mortal be righteous with God? If he wished to contend with him, he could not answer him one time in a thousand. Wise in heart and mighty in strength—who has hardened himself against him and come out whole? "He moves mountains, and they do not know it; he overturns them in his anger. He shakes the earth from its place, and its pillars shudder. He speaks to the sun, and it does not rise, and he seals up the stars. "He stretches out the heavens by himself and treads on the heights of the sea. He makes the Bear, Orion and the Pleiades, and the chambers of the south. He does great things beyond searching out, and wonders beyond number. "Look, he passes by me, and I do not see him; he sweeps on, and I do not discern him. Look, he snatches away; who can turn him back? Who will say to him, 'What are you doing?' God does not turn back his anger; under him the helpers of Rahab bowed low. "How much less could I myself answer him, choose my words with him! Though I am righteous, I could not answer him; I would plead with my judge. If I called and he answered me, I would not believe that he was listening to my voice, he who strikes me with a storm and multiplies my wounds for nothing. He does not let me get my breath back, but gives me my fill of bitter things. "If it is a matter of strength, look, he is mighty; and if of justice, who will summon me? Though I am righteous, my mouth would condemn me; I am blameless, and it would declare me crooked. "I am blameless; I do not care about myself; I reject my life. It is all one; that is why I say, 'He makes an end of the blameless and the wicked.' If a scourge suddenly brings death, he mocks at the testing of the innocent. The earth has been given into the hand of the wicked; he covers the faces of its judges. If not, then who is it? "And my days are swifter than a runner; they flee; they do not see good. They sweep past like reed boats, like an eagle swooping on food. "If I say, 'I will forget my complaint; I will abandon my sad face and be cheerful,' I shrink from all my pains; I know that you will not hold me innocent. I myself am to be condemned; why then do I toil in vain? "If I washed myself with snow and made my palms pure with lye, then you would dip me in the pit, and my clothes would detest me. "For he is not a man like me, that I should answer him, that we should come together into court. There is no arbiter between us, who might lay his hand on us both. Let him remove his rod from me, and let the terror of him not terrify me. Then I would speak and not fear him; for I am not so in myself.
