Job, chapter 7. "Is there not a soldier's service for a mortal on earth, and are his days not like the days of a hired worker? Like a slave who pants for the shadow, and like a hired worker who hopes for his pay, so I have been made to inherit months of emptiness, and nights of misery have been appointed for me. "When I lie down, I say, 'When will I rise?' But the evening stretches on, and I have my fill of tossing until the morning twilight. My flesh is clothed with maggots and clods of dust; my skin breaks out and runs. My days are swifter than a weaver's shuttle, and they come to an end without hope. "Remember that my life is wind; my eye will not see good again. The eye of one who sees me will not catch sight of me; your eyes will be on me, and I will be no more. "A cloud comes to an end and is gone; so one who goes down to Sheol does not come up. He no longer returns to his house, and his place no longer recognizes him. "I, for my part, will not restrain my mouth; I will speak in the distress of my spirit; I will complain in the bitterness of my soul. Am I the sea, or a sea monster, that you set a guard over me? "When I say, 'My couch will comfort me; my bed will help carry my complaint,' then you frighten me with dreams and terrify me with visions, and my soul chooses strangling, death rather than my bones. I reject my life; I will not live forever. Leave me alone, for my days are a breath. "What is a mortal, that you make so much of him, and that you consider him, and inspect him every morning, and test him every moment? How long will you not look away from me, or let go of me long enough for me to swallow my spit? "If I have sinned, what do I do to you, watcher of humankind? Why have you set me as your target, so that I have become a burden to myself? And why do you not carry away my rebellion and take away my guilt? For I will now lie down in the dust; you will search for me, and I will be no more."
