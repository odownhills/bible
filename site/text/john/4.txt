John, chapter 4. So when Jesus knew that the Pharisees had heard that Jesus was making and baptizing more disciples than John (although Jesus himself was not baptizing, but his disciples were), he left Judea and went away again into Galilee. Now he had to pass through Samaria. So he comes to a city of Samaria called Sychar, close to the plot of land that Jacob gave to Joseph his son; and Jacob's spring was there. So Jesus, having become weary from the journey, was sitting in this way at the spring. It was about the sixth hour. A woman from Samaria comes to draw water. Jesus says to her, "Give me a drink." For his disciples had gone away into the city so that they might buy provisions. So the Samaritan woman says to him, "How do you, being a Jew, ask for a drink from me, being a Samaritan woman?" For Jews do not have dealings with Samaritans. Jesus answered and said to her, "If you knew the gift of God and who it is who is saying to you, 'Give me a drink,' you yourself would have asked him, and he would have given you living water." The woman says to him, "Sir, you have nothing to draw with, and the well is deep; where then do you have the living water from? You are not greater, are you, than our father Jacob, who gave us the well, and he himself drank from it, and his sons and his livestock?" Jesus answered and said to her, "Everyone who drinks from this water will be thirsty again; but whoever drinks from the water that I myself will give him will never be thirsty, but the water that I will give him will become in him a spring of water leaping up into eternal life." The woman says to him, "Sir, give me this water, so that I may not be thirsty nor come all the way here to draw." He says to her, "Go, call your husband, and come here." The woman answered and said to him, "I do not have a husband." Jesus says to her, "You said well, 'A husband I do not have'; for you have had five husbands, and the one you have now is not your husband. This is a true thing you have said." The woman says to him, "Sir, I see that you are a prophet. Our fathers worshiped on this mountain, and you yourselves say that in Jerusalem is the place where one must worship." Jesus says to her, "Believe me, woman, that an hour is coming when you all will worship the Father neither on this mountain nor in Jerusalem. You yourselves worship what you do not know; we ourselves worship what we know, because salvation is from the Jews. But an hour is coming, and it is now, when the true worshipers will worship the Father in spirit and truth; for indeed the Father seeks such people to be those who worship him. God is spirit, and those who worship him must worship in spirit and truth." The woman says to him, "I know that Messiah is coming" (the one who is called Christ); "when that one comes, he will declare all things to us." Jesus says to her, "I am he, the one who is speaking to you." And at this his disciples came, and they were surprised that he was speaking with a woman; yet no one said, "What are you seeking?" or, "Why are you speaking with her?" So the woman left her water jar and went away into the city, and she says to the people, "Come, see a man who said to me all the things that I did. This one is not the Christ, is he?" They went out from the city and were coming to him. In the meantime the disciples were making a request of him, saying, "Rabbi, eat." But he said to them, "I myself have food to eat that you yourselves do not know." So the disciples were saying to one another, "No one has carried him anything to eat, has he?" Jesus says to them, "My food is that I should do the will of the one who sent me and make his work complete. Do you yourselves not say, 'It is still four months, and the harvest comes'? Look, I say to you, lift your eyes and gaze at the fields, that they are white for harvest. Already the one who harvests is receiving wages and gathering fruit into eternal life, so that the one who sows and the one who harvests may rejoice together. For in this the word is true: 'The one who sows is one, and the one who harvests is another.' I myself sent you to harvest what you yourselves have not labored for; others have labored, and you yourselves have entered into their labor." And from that city many of the Samaritans believed in him because of the word of the woman, who testified, "He said to me all the things that I did." So when the Samaritans came to him, they were making a request of him to stay with them; and he stayed there two days. And many more believed because of his word, and they were saying to the woman, "It is no longer because of your speaking that we believe; for we ourselves have heard, and we know that this is truly the savior of the world." And after the two days he went out from there into Galilee; for Jesus himself testified that a prophet has no honor in his own homeland. So when he came into Galilee, the Galileans welcomed him, having seen all the things that he did in Jerusalem at the festival; for they themselves also had come to the festival. So he came again to Cana of Galilee, where he had made the water into wine. And there was a certain royal official whose son was sick in Capernaum. This one, having heard that Jesus had come from Judea into Galilee, went away to him and was making a request that he should come down and heal his son, for he was about to die. So Jesus said to him, "Unless you all see signs and wonders, you will certainly not believe." The royal official says to him, "Sir, come down before my young child dies." Jesus says to him, "Go on your way; your son lives." The man believed the word that Jesus said to him, and he went on his way. And already, as he was going down, his slaves met him, saying that his boy was living. So he inquired from them the hour at which he got better. So they said to him, "Yesterday at the seventh hour the fever left him." So the father knew that it was at that hour at which Jesus had said to him, "Your son lives"; and he himself believed, and his whole household. And Jesus did this, again, as a second sign, having come from Judea into Galilee.
