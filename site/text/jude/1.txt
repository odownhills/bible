Jude, chapter 1. Jude, a slave of Jesus Christ and brother of James, to those who are called, who have been loved in God the Father and kept for Jesus Christ: May mercy and peace and love be multiplied to you. Beloved, while I was making every effort to write to you concerning our shared salvation, I had to write to you, urging you to struggle for the faith that was handed over to the holy ones once for all. For certain people have sneaked in, those who long ago were written down beforehand for this judgment, godless people, who change the grace of our God into debauchery and deny our only Master and Lord, Jesus Christ. And I wish to remind you, though you know all things once for all, that Jesus, having saved a people from the land of Egypt, the second time destroyed those who did not believe, and the angels who did not keep their own domain but abandoned their own dwelling he has kept in everlasting bonds under gloom for the judgment of the great day, as Sodom and Gomorrah and the cities around them, which, in a way like these, committed sexual immorality and went away after different flesh, are set out as an example, undergoing the penalty of eternal fire. Yet likewise these also, as they dream, defile flesh, reject lordship and slander glories. But Michael the archangel, when, disputing with the devil, he was arguing concerning the body of Moses, did not dare to bring a judgment of slander against him, but said, "May the Lord rebuke you." But these slander whatever they do not know; and whatever they understand by nature, as the unreasoning animals do—in these things they are being ruined. Woe to them, because they have gone the way of Cain, and have poured themselves out into the error of Balaam for wages, and have perished in the rebellion of Korah. These are the ones who are reefs at your love feasts, feasting together without fear, shepherding themselves; waterless clouds carried along by winds; late-autumn trees, fruitless, having died twice, uprooted; wild waves of the sea, foaming up their own shameful deeds; wandering stars, for which the gloom of darkness has been kept forever. And Enoch, the seventh from Adam, prophesied about these also, saying, "Look, the Lord came with his holy myriads to do judgment against all and to expose every soul concerning all their works of godlessness that they did godlessly, and concerning all the hard things that godless sinners spoke against him." These are grumblers, complaining about their lot, going on their way according to their own desires—and their mouth speaks inflated things—admiring faces for the sake of benefit. But you yourselves, beloved, remember the words said beforehand by the apostles of our Lord Jesus Christ, that they were saying to you, "In the last time there will be mockers, going on their way according to their own desires of godlessness." These are the ones who make divisions, natural people, not having spirit. But you yourselves, beloved, building yourselves up on your most holy faith, praying in holy spirit, keep yourselves in the love of God, looking forward to the mercy of our Lord Jesus Christ into eternal life. And have mercy on some who are doubting; and save others, seizing them from the fire; and on others have mercy in fear, hating even the tunic stained by the flesh. And to the one who can guard you from stumbling and make you stand in the presence of his glory without blemish, in exultation, to the only God, our savior, through Jesus Christ our Lord, be glory, greatness, might and authority before all time and now and for all time. Amen.
