# Notes on Genesis 37, for the translator of Genesis 38

Text followed: the Masoretic Text; the chapter has no marginal reading. The dots the Hebrew text sets over one small word at 37:12 change nothing. The Hebrew has one paragraph break, after 37:36. Numbering is the same in Hebrew and English.

Brackets: three, each in the decision log.
- 37:3 "a long tunic" [alt: a tunic of many colors, as the old Greek has it] (ask). Not repeated at 37:23, 32.
- 37:36 "the Medanites" [alt: the Midianites, as the old Greek and other ancient versions have it] (clear).
- 37:36 "a court official of Pharaoh" [alt: a eunuch of Pharaoh] (clear). Not to be repeated at 39:1 or 40:2, 7.

Places where the old copies differ, not bracketed:
- 37:10 the old Greek does not have "And he recounted it to his father and to his brothers" as the Hebrew does. 37:17 "I heard them saying": the Hebrew has no "them", and the old Greek writes it out; the sense is the same. 37:27 the old Greek has "and our flesh". 37:33 the old Greek has "It is my son's tunic".

Weighed and left unbracketed:
- 37:28 "they pulled Joseph up ... and they sold Joseph ... and they brought Joseph": the Hebrew does not say who "they" are. Read by the nearest subject, the Midianite merchants pulled him out and sold him to the Ishmaelites, which is why Reuben finds the pit empty (37:29-30) and why Joseph says "I was stolen" (40:15). Read with 37:27, the brothers pulled him out and sold him, as Joseph says, "whom you sold into Egypt" (45:4-5). The English "they" is as open as the Hebrew. Who brought him to Egypt is open in the same way (37:36 the Medanites; 39:1 the Ishmaelites).
- 37:2 "shepherding the flock with his brothers": the same words could be put together as "shepherding his brothers"; the flock is named beside them. "He was a boy with the sons of Bilhah": the word is "boy" (21:12; 22:5) and also "young man", a servant (18:7; 22:3), so it may say that he worked under them. "A bad report about them": the Hebrew is "their report, a bad one", which can also be the bad talk they themselves spread (the word is used both ways: Numbers 13:32; Jeremiah 20:10).
- 37:3 "he made": the verb form can also be "he would make". "A son of his old age" has no article in the Hebrew.
- 37:4 "speak peaceably to him" is "speak to him for peace"; the same word is "whether it is well with" at 37:14 (29:6), and the echo is not shown.
- 37:10 "your mother": Rachel has died (35:19); the text says what it says.
- 37:11 "kept the matter in mind": "in mind" carries the verb "keep".
- 37:19 "that dreamer" is "that master of dreams", the ordinary way to say a man of some kind; it can be heard as a sneer.
- 37:20, 33 "a savage animal" is "an evil animal". 37:21 "take his life" is "strike him, a life".
- 37:21 "he rescued him" is not softened to "tried to rescue".
- 37:22 the words after the dash are the narrator's.
- 37:25 "gum and balm and resin": three goods whose exact kinds Scripture does not fix (43:11 has all three again; Jeremiah 8:22 the balm of Gilead).
- 37:30 "The child is gone": the word of 5:24 ("he was no more"); Reuben means the pit is empty, and 42:13, 36 use it of Joseph again.
- 37:32 "they sent ... and brought": the Hebrew has both verbs with one "they".
- 37:35 the small word before "I will go down" only opens the speech (as at 21:30). "To my son ..., to Sheol": not softened. "His father wept for him": Jacob for Joseph; the Hebrew names neither.
- 37:36 "the commander of the guard": the word for the guard is "slaughterers", which is also "cooks" (1 Samuel 9:23); the old Greek has "chief cook". He keeps the king's prison (40:3), and the officer who burned Jerusalem has a title made with the same word (2 Kings 25:8).
- 37:29 "garments" and 37:34 "clothes" are two Hebrew words; so are "kill" (37:20, 26) and "put to death" (37:18).

For Genesis 38: "Recognize it, please" (37:32) and "recognized" (37:33) return at 38:25-26 and must be the same words; "male goat" (37:31) stands beside "goat kid" (38:17, 20, as at 27:9); "be comforted" (38:12) as at 24:67 and 37:35; "veil" (38:14, 19) as at 24:65; "prostitute" (38:15) as at 34:31; "daughter-in-law" (11:31); "midwife" (38:28) as at 35:17; "sleep with" for the idiom of 6:4 (38:2, 8, 9, 16, 18); Judah "went down" (38:1) as Joseph was taken down (37:25; 39:1); at 39:1 Potiphar is "a court official of Pharaoh, the commander of the guard", with no bracket, and the Ishmaelites are spelled as here.
