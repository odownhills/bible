# Notes on Genesis 49, for the translator of Genesis 50

Text followed: the Masoretic Text, read by its Ketiv. 49:10: the Ketiv and the Qere are two spellings of "Shiloh". 49:11: the Ketiv has the older spelling of "his" twice ("his colt", "his robe"), as at 9:21; nothing changes. The Hebrew paragraph breaks fall after 49:4, 7, 12, 13, 15, 19, 20, 21 and 26; each saying is its own paragraph, and each verse of the sayings is on one line.

Brackets: seven, each with an entry in the decision log: 49:5 "their swords" [alt: their dealings, or their kinship] (close); 49:6 "hamstrung an ox" [alt: tore down a wall] (clear); 49:10 "until Shiloh comes" [alt: until he comes to Shiloh; or until he comes to whom it belongs] (ask); 49:12 "dark from wine ... white from milk" [alt: darker than ... whiter than] (close); 49:21 "he gives beautiful words" [alt: fawns; or a tree and its branches] (close); 49:22 "a fruitful bough" [alt: a wild donkey's colt] (close); 49:26 "my parents, as far as" [alt: the enduring mountains, with the old Greek] (clear). Notes on sound: 49:8 (Judah, praise, hand), 49:16 (Dan, judge), 49:19 (Gad, raiders, raid), 49:25 (breasts, the Almighty).

Places where the old copies differ, not bracketed:
- 49:4 the old Greek has "you went up" at the end, where the Hebrew turns to "he went up"; the same act either way. The verse is divided as the Hebrew accents divide it: "then you desecrated it. He went up to my couch!"
- 49:6 the old Greek has "my liver" for "my glory" (the same letters).
- 49:10 the old Greek has "the expectation of nations" for "the obedience of peoples".
- 49:19-20 old versions take the first letter of 49:20 with the last word of 49:19: "their heel" and "Asher, his bread is rich". The Hebrew text has "a heel" and "From Asher"; the sense is the same, and "at their heels" is the English for it.
- 49:22 the Samaritan copy and the old Greek have "my youngest son" for the words about the branches.
- 49:24 the old Syriac version reads "by the name of the Shepherd" for "from there, the Shepherd" (other vowels). The old Greek has the archers' bows broken.
- 49:25 the Samaritan copy and old versions have "and God Almighty" for "and with the Almighty" (one letter).

Weighed and left unbracketed:
- 49:1 "in the latter days": the Hebrew is "in the after-part of the days"; it can be days to come or the last days, and the English is as open.
- 49:3 "my strength": or the strength to father children; "the beginning of my vigor" is said of a firstborn at Deut 21:17.
- 49:4 "Reckless as water": the word is "reckless" of men at Judg 9:4; of water it is what breaks loose. "You will not excel": the Hebrew can also be a wish, "may you not excel".
- 49:6 "a man", "an ox": singular in the Hebrew, as at 4:23; the story has many men killed (34:25). "At their pleasure": or "in their willfulness".
- 49:8 "Your hand will be on": the Hebrew has no verb.
- 49:9 "From the prey ... you have gone up": or "you have grown up on prey" (compare Ezek 19:3). The Hebrew perfects "crouches", "lies down" (49:9), "washes" (49:11) are put in the present, as pictures.
- 49:10 "the ruler's staff from between his feet": or "a lawgiver from among his descendants" (the word is a staff at Num 21:18 and a lawgiver at Isa 33:22; "between her feet" is said of birth at Deut 28:57). Either way the rule stays with Judah. "Will be his": Judah's or the one who comes; as open as the Hebrew.
- 49:11-12 "he": the one of 49:10, left as open.
- 49:14 "between the sheepfolds": the word is found again only at Judg 5:16, beside flocks; "between the saddlebags" is also said, from the donkey and the load of 49:15.
- 49:15 the verbs are past forms in the Hebrew and are kept so. "Rest": or "a resting place".
- 49:16 "will judge his people": the words of Deut 32:36, where it is judging in their favor (30:6).
- 49:18 "I hope": a Hebrew perfect; or "I have hoped".
- 49:21 "he gives": the Hebrew is "the one giving", in the masculine.
- 49:23 "bore a grudge against him": the verb of 27:41 and 50:15, kept so that 50:15 is heard with it.
- 49:24 "from there, the Shepherd, the Stone of Israel": as open as the Hebrew, which can also be a sentence, "from there is the Shepherd, the Stone of Israel". "The arms of his hands": word for word.
- 49:25 "may he help you", "may he bless you" and 49:26 "May they be": the forms can also be plain futures ("he will help you"); wishes, as at 27:28 and 48:16.
- 49:26 "delight": could be "bounds" (the verb of Num 34:10); see the decision log. "Set apart from his brothers": or "among his brothers"; the word is the one for a Nazirite, and nowhere in Scripture clearly "prince".
- 49:28 "All these are the twelve tribes of Israel": the Hebrew is "the tribes of Israel, twelve".
- 49:29 "with my fathers, in the cave": the Hebrew has "to my fathers, to the cave". 49:32 has no verb: "the purchase ... from the sons of Heth".

For Genesis 50: "bore a grudge against" (50:15; 49:23; 27:41); "the cave of the field of Machpelah", "the field that Abraham bought ... from Ephron the Hittite as a holding for a grave" and "facing Mamre" (50:13; 49:29-30; 23:17-20); "as he had commanded them" takes up "commanded" (50:12; 49:29, 33); "be gathered to his people" and "breathed his last" (49:33); "the bed" (49:33); "attend to" (50:24, 25; 21:1) and "bring up" (50:24; 46:4); "in the place of God" (50:19; 30:2); "speak to the heart of" (50:21; 34:3) and "provide for" (50:21; 45:11); "give birth on the knees of" (50:23; 30:3); "those who live in the land" (50:11; 34:30); Joseph "fell on his father's face" (50:1) is not the "fall on the neck" of 46:29; "Israel" and "Jacob" each as the Hebrew has it.
