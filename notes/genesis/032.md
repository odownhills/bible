# Notes on Genesis 32, for the translator of Genesis 33

Text followed: the Masoretic Text; the chapter has no Qere. The Hebrew has a paragraph break after 32:2. The Hebrew counts 31:55 as 32:1, so every verse here is numbered one lower than in the Hebrew file (Hebrew 32:2 is English 32:1); the `numbering:` line says so. Chapter 33 is numbered the same in both.

Places where the old copies differ, none bracketed:
- 32:28 the old Greek and Latin end the saying another way: the Greek "you have been strong with God, and with men, mighty", the Latin "if you have been strong against God, how much more will you prevail against men". The Hebrew verb is past, "and have prevailed", as at Hosea 12:4.
- 32:30, 31 the Hebrew spells the place "Peniel" and then "Penuel"; the Samaritan copy has "Penuel" both times. Each is spelled as the Hebrew has it, and the note at 32:30 says so.
- 32:32 the old Greek has "the sinew that grew numb" for the sinew's name.

Brackets: one. 32:28 "with God" [alt: with divine beings] (clear). Six notes: 32:2 "Mahanaim"; 32:3 "messengers" is the word "angels"; 32:20 the four "faces"; 32:24 "wrestled", "Jabbok", "Jacob"; 32:28 "Israel"; 32:30 "Peniel".

Weighed and left unbracketed:
- 32:4 the first "This is what" can also be heard as the opening of the message itself ("To my lord Esau: this is what your servant Jacob says"); the message is the same.
- 32:4, 18 "to my lord, to Esau", 32:6 "to your brother, to Esau": the Hebrew repeats the small word, as at 24:14.
- 32:5 "oxen and donkeys, flocks and male slaves and female slaves": each is one animal or person in the Hebrew, standing for the kind.
- 32:8 "will escape" is "will be an escape", the noun of "one who had escaped" (14:13).
- 32:10 "I am too small for": not softened to "unworthy". "Acts of faithful love" is the plural of "faithful love". "Staff" is the "stick" of 30:37.
- 32:11 "mother with children" is "mother on children" (Hosea 10:14).
- 32:12 "too many to count" (16:10) can go with the offspring or with the sand, in English as in Hebrew.
- 32:13 "present": the word of Cain's and Abel's "offering" (4:3-5), here of what is given to a man; "gift" is another word (25:6). "What had come into his hand" is what he had with him.
- 32:15 "nursing camels and their young, thirty": the Hebrew order is kept, since it does not say whether the young are in the thirty. "Male donkeys" is "colt" at Job 11:12; here it is the male beside the female, as with the goats, sheep and cattle.
- 32:16, 19 "herd" is the "flock" of 29:2; it covers camels, cattle and donkeys here.
- 32:20 "appease him" and "accept me" are idioms with "face", put by their meaning; the note gives the four faces. "Ahead of him" (32:21) is a fifth, "before his face". "For he said" is the narrator's: the words speak of Esau as "him".
- 32:23 "brought them across": the Hebrew is "made them cross" and does not say on which side Jacob then was; 32:22 says he crossed. "Torrent" as in Job 6:15.
- 32:24, 25 "wrestled" shares its English with the verb of 30:8; this verb stands only here.
- 32:25 "he saw that he could not prevail against him": the Hebrew names neither; the man is the one who saw. "Touched" can be a blow (Job 1:19 "hit"); the plain word is kept, as at 32:32.
- 32:28 "struggled": the verb stands only here and at Hosea 12:3. It can also be heard as "been a prince" (17:15 "Sarah"; Judges 9:22 "ruled"); what follows, "and have prevailed", and the wrestling are why it is "struggled". "Said to be" is not the "called" of 17:5 and 35:10.
- 32:29 "he blessed him there": the man blessed Jacob.
- 32:30 "my life has been rescued" is the verb of "rescue me" (32:11).
- 32:32 "the sinew of the hip": the second word stands only here, and the verse itself says where the sinew is. "He touched": the Hebrew names no one.

For Genesis 33: "four hundred men" (33:1) as at 32:6; "divided" (33:1) is the verb of 32:7; "female slaves" (33:1, 2, 6) and "children" (33:1, 2, 5) as at 32:22; "passed on ahead of them" (33:3) as at 32:16, 21; "camp" (33:8) and "that I met" as at 32:2, 17; "to find favor in the eyes of my lord" (33:8, 15) as at 32:5; "present" (33:10) as at 32:13, beside "blessing" (33:11); "I have seen your face as one sees the face of God" (33:10) takes up the faces of 32:20 and "Peniel" (32:30), and "accepted me" there is another verb than the "accept me" of 32:20; "my lord" and "your servant" as here; "Seir" (33:14, 16) as at 32:3; Jacob is still called "Jacob" by the narrator after 32:28.
