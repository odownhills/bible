# Notes on Genesis 35, for the translator of Genesis 36

Text followed: the Masoretic Text. The chapter has no Qere. "His tent" (35:21) is written in the older spelling, as at 9:21 and 12:8. The Hebrew has paragraph breaks after 35:8, inside 35:22 (after "and Israel heard") and after 35:29. The break inside 35:22 is set as a new paragraph inside the verse, the way Deuteronomy 5:21 is set; because of the file format no heading can stand there, so one heading covers 35:21-26. Numbering is the same in Hebrew and English.

Places where the old copies differ, not bracketed:
- 35:4 the old Greek adds "and destroyed them to this day".
- 35:7 the old Greek, Latin and Syriac have "called the place Bethel", without the first "God".
- 35:22 the old Greek adds, after "and Israel heard", "and it was evil in his sight".

Brackets: one. 35:7 "there God had revealed himself to him" [alt: there the divine beings had revealed themselves to him] (clear): the verb is plural. Two notes: 35:8 "Allon-Bacuth"; 35:18 "Ben-Oni" and "Benjamin".

Weighed and left unbracketed:
- 35:1, 3, 7 "the God who": the short word for "God" (as in "Bethel" and "God Almighty"), the same English as the long one.
- 35:2 "foreign gods" is "the gods of what is foreign". "Make yourselves clean" is the verb of "clean" (7:2). "Clothes" is the plural of "cloak" (9:23).
- 35:3 "who answered me": the Hebrew form is timeless ("who answers me"); the next verb, "has been with me", sets it in the past.
- 35:4 "hid": the verb is "hide" (Job 3:16), not "bury" (35:8, 19, 29). "Terebinth" is a third tree word, kept apart from the two "oak" words (12:6; 35:8). "In their hands" is "in their hand".
- 35:5 "the terror of God" can also be heard as "a very great terror", as "a chieftain of God" (23:6) and "wrestlings of God" (30:8) can. No bracket, since there are no standing brackets and God has just sent Jacob on this way. "Terror" is of the root of the word at 9:2. "Set out" is the verb "travel" (35:16, 21).
- 35:7 "God of Bethel" as at 31:13; the name is set in quotation marks like the one at 33:20.
- 35:8 "it was named": the Hebrew is "he named it", with no one named (11:9; 25:26).
- 35:9 "again" looks back to the first appearing at this place (28:13) or to 32:30; the English is as open as the Hebrew.
- 35:10 worded beside 17:5; "named him" is the verb of 35:10a, "be called", not the "said to be" of 32:28. The note on "Israel" (32:28) is not repeated.
- 35:11 "be fruitful and multiply" is said to one man (1:28 is plural). "Will be from you" as at 17:16; "come out of your loins" stands beside "come out of your body" (15:4), a different word.
- 35:13-15 "at the place where he had spoken with him" stands three times, and the English keeps all three.
- 35:14 "poured out a drink offering" and "poured oil" are two verbs.
- 35:16 "a stretch of land": the Hebrew word is a measure of distance whose length Scripture does not give (48:7; 2 Kings 5:19). "Ephrath": the Hebrew form has the ending that means "toward".
- 35:16, 17 "had hard labor", "was in hard labor": two forms of one verb with "in her giving birth".
- 35:17 "this one too is a son for you" answers 30:24, "May Yahweh add another son to me".
- 35:18 "her life" is the word also put as "soul" (34:3). "For she was dying": the Hebrew is "for she died"; 35:19 then tells the death in its place. "Ben-Oni": "sorrow" as at Deuteronomy 26:14 and Hosea 9:4; the same letters are "my vigor" at 49:3, and the note gives both. "Benjamin": "right hand" is also the word for "south".
- 35:20 "to this day" as at 19:37.
- 35:21 "Migdal-Eder" means "tower of the flock" (Micah 4:8); it is kept as a name.
- 35:22 "and Israel heard": the Hebrew has no object. "Concubine" as at 22:24; Bilhah is "female slave" at 35:25, as at 30:4.
- 35:26 "who were born to him in Paddan-Aram": so the Hebrew, though Benjamin was born in Canaan (35:16-18).
- 35:27 "at Mamre, at Kiriath-Arba": the Hebrew sets the two names side by side with no small word. Kiriath-Arba has the article here; the English name is as at 23:2.
- 35:29 worded beside 25:8-9; "full of days" where 25:8 has "satisfied".

For Genesis 36: "These are the generations of Esau" (36:1, 9) as at 25:12, 19; "Esau (that is, Edom)" in parentheses, as "(that is, Bethel)" here; Esau's wives are named differently at 26:34 and 28:9 (Judith, Basemath daughter of Elon, Mahalath), and nothing there is to be changed; 36:6-7 takes up 12:5 and 13:6 ("possessions", "amassed", "support", "the land where they were staying" as at 17:8, and "what he had acquired" as at 34:23); "the hill country of Seir" (36:8) as at 14:6 and 32:3; "the Horites" (36:20) as at 14:6; "chieftain" (17:20; 34:2) is another word than the one for the heads of Edom (36:15), which needs its own English; "Hadad" (36:35) as at 25:15; "the sons of Israel" (36:31) as at 32:32.
