# Notes on Genesis 17, for the translator of Genesis 18

Text followed: the Masoretic Text; the chapter has no Ketiv and Qere. The main text keeps to the Masoretic vowels: 17:14 "is not circumcised" (the same letters could be read "does not circumcise"; the sense is the same).

Places where the old copies differ:
- 17:16 the old Greek and Latin versions have "bless him ... from him" in the second half; bracketed (decision log, clear).
- 17:14 the Samaritan copy and the old Greek add "on the eighth day" after "the flesh of his foreskin"; not bracketed, as with what the versions add in earlier chapters.
- 17:19 the old Greek has "Yes" for the first word of God's answer, and "and for his offspring after him" at the end; not bracketed on that ground.

Brackets: 17:16 "I will bless her, and she will become nations; kings of peoples will be from her" [alt: him, he, from him] (clear); 17:19 "Indeed, Sarah your wife" [alt: But Sarah your wife] (close). Three notes: 17:5 ("Abraham"), 17:15 ("Sarah"), 17:19 ("Isaac").

Weighed and left unbracketed:
- 17:1 "God Almighty": the Job 5:17 line and its open review; no bracket is repeated. If Obay switches Job 5:17, this verse follows.
- 17:1 "and be blameless": a second command; it can also be heard as the result ("and you will be blameless"). 17:2 "and I will set" follows the commands and carries the sense "so that I may set". "Set" is the verb "give" (9:12); the covenant was "made" at 15:18 with another verb.
- 17:5, 16, 20: three Hebrew perfects in God's mouth, of a thing done in the saying: "I have made you", "I also give you", "I have blessed him". Each is put as it reads best (9:3, 9:13 and 15:18 did the same).
- 17:5 the note says only what the text itself gives as the reason for the name. 17:15 the text gives no reason for "Sarah"; the note gives the meaning of the word (Judges 5:29; Isaiah 49:23), which is heard with "kings of peoples" in 17:16. Obay may want it out.
- 17:8 "the land where you are staying": the word is from "stay" as an outsider (12:10; 15:13 "resident foreigners"). "Holding" is a new word here, for land that is held; see the glossary line.
- 17:10 "between me and you": this "you" is plural, and "your offspring after you" is singular; English cannot show it. "Among you" (17:10, 12) is the Hebrew "for you" or "of yours".
- 17:11 "it will serve as a sign": "it" is the circumcising. The wording is that of 9:13.
- 17:12 "who is not of your offspring": as open as the Hebrew (the one bought, or the foreigner he is bought from).
- 17:14 "cut off from his people": the Hebrew has "peoples", a person's kin (25:8 will have the same plural); the text does not say how he is cut off. "That person" is the word "soul" (12:5).
- 17:17 "laughed": the text does not say what kind of laugh; 18:12-15 and 21:6 use the same verb. "A child", "a man" and "a woman" are supplied: the Hebrew is "to one a hundred years old will there be born", "will one ninety years old give birth".
- 17:18 "live before you": as open as the Hebrew (stay alive, or live under God's eye and care).
- 17:20 "I have heard you" plays on the name "Ishmael" again; the note of 16:11 is not repeated.
- 17:21 "at this set time next year": the Hebrew is "at this set time, in the other year".
- 17:26 a singular verb with two subjects, as at 7:7 ("Abraham was circumcised, and Ishmael his son").

For Genesis 18: the names are now "Abraham" and "Sarah" (the notes are not repeated); "laugh" (18:12, 13, 15) is the verb of 17:17 and the name "Isaac"; 18:10, 14 take up 17:21 ("at this set time", "Sarah will have a son"); Abraham is ninety-nine and Sarah eighty-nine or ninety (17:17, 24); "among the oaks of Mamre" (13:18) at 18:1; "appeared to" (12:7; 17:1); "in her heart" follows "say in his heart" (17:17) only if the Hebrew is the same phrase (18:12 has another word, "within herself"); 18:18 "a great nation" and "be blessed in" as at 12:2-3; 18:19 "keep the way of Yahweh" has the verb of "keep my covenant" (17:9-10); "find favor in the eyes of" (6:8) at 18:3.
