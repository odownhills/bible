# Notes on Job 27, for the translator of Job 28

Text followed: the Masoretic Text, read by its Ketiv; the chapter has no Hebrew paragraph break. The one Qere (27:15, a fuller spelling of "his survivors") changes nothing, since the verb is plural, as at 5:18. 27:1 is a new heading, "And Job again took up his discourse and said" (29:1 repeats it word for word), so 27:2 opens a new quotation. The speech does not end with the chapter: 28:1 begins with "For" and has no heading, so Job 27 ends with no closing quotation mark (the Job 4 line). "You" is plural in 27:5 and 27:11-12 (the friends). Nothing in the Hebrew gives 27:13-23 to anyone but Job, though 27:13 repeats Zophar's last line (20:29); nothing is said about that in the chapter.

Brackets: six, each with an entry in the decision log: 27:3 "for as long as my breath is in me" [alt: for my breath is still whole in me] (close); 27:8 "when he is cut off" [alt: though he makes his profit] (ask); 27:15 "buried by the plague" [alt: buried in death] (clear); 27:19 "and will not be gathered" [alt: and will not do so again] (close); 27:22 "it lets fly at him ... from its hand" [alt: God lets fly at him ... from his hand] (close); 27:23 "It claps its hands at him" [alt: People clap their hands at him] (ask).

Places where the old copies differ, not bracketed:
- 27:15 the old Greek has "their widows" for "his widows"; the Hebrew plural is kept (the words are those of Psalm 78:64).
- 27:18 the old Greek and Syriac have a spider beside or in place of the moth (compare 8:14); either way the house does not last.
- 27:19 the reading of the old Greek and Syriac is the alt there.

Weighed and left unbracketed:
- 27:1 "discourse" is the word that is "proverb" at 13:12 (and "byword" in another form, 17:6); it is what Balaam "takes up" in Numbers 23:7.
- 27:2 "my justice" is the justice owed to me (the word is "case" at 13:18 and 23:4); Elihu quotes the line at 34:5. "Removed" is kept for the same verb in 27:5 ("I will not remove my blamelessness"), so that the echo is heard.
- 27:4 the imperfects are "will", with the oath; under the alt at 27:3 they would be "do not speak".
- 27:5 "declare you in the right": the verb is from "be righteous", and "my righteousness" follows in 27:6. "Until I breathe my last" goes with the second half, as the Hebrew accents divide the line.
- 27:6 "any of my days": the Hebrew is "from my days", which can also be "in all my days" (as at 38:12); either way his heart finds nothing in his life to insult. The verb has no "me". The Job 6 decision log quoted the line as "will not let it go"; the chapter says "let go of it" (7:19).
- 27:7 the Job 16 decision log quoted this as "like the wicked ... rises against me like the unjust"; the chapter follows the glossary ("the wicked man", "rises up against", "the unjust man").
- 27:8 "draws out his life": the verb stands only here; a change to "asks for his life", which no old copy has, is not noted. The active "he cuts off" is put in the passive (8:18), since God is named only in the second half.
- 27:9 "Is it his cry that": the object is set first in the question (13:7; 14:3). 27:10 is two more questions, not "If he took delight ... he would call".
- 27:11 "about the hand of God": the Hebrew can also be "by the hand of God" (with his help); the verb "teach" takes this word for what is taught (Psalm 32:8; Proverbs 4:11). "What is with the Almighty" is what he has in mind (10:13; 23:14); "with" is kept because 27:13 has "with God".
- 27:12 "breathe out mere breath": the Hebrew is a verb and a noun from the one word "breath" (7:16), "you breathe a breath"; "in vain" (9:29; 21:34) is the same word. "Gazed on it" has "it" supplied.
- 27:13 "with God" where 20:29 has "from God"; a colon follows, since "This" points forward here.
- 27:15 "his widows" is plural in the Hebrew.
- 27:17 "portion out" is from the word "portion" (27:13). 27:18 "watchman" is one who guards a field or vineyard (Isaiah 1:8 has such a shelter).
- 27:18-23: the perfects and imperfects that picture his end are put in the English present (18:5-21); those of 27:14-17 are "will".
- 27:19 "he opens his eyes, and he is no more": it can also be "and it is no more" (his wealth); the words are those of 7:8, 7:21 and 24:24, where they are said of the man. "He opens his eyes" can also be God opening his eyes on him (14:3); the English "he" is as open.
- 27:20 "like water": a flood; the change to "by day", which no old copy has, is not noted. The verb is singular with "terrors", as the Job 18 line said.
- 27:22 "lets fly at him": the Hebrew is "throws at him", with no object (Numbers 35:20). "Flees headlong" is the doubled verb, "fleeing he flees". "Hand" is kept, though of a thing it is "power" at 5:20, because 27:23 gives the wind hands.
- 27:23 "from his place" is the same Hebrew as in 27:21 and is read of the man's place both times; with the wind or God as the one who hisses it can also be "from its place" or "from his own place" (Micah 1:3). "Hands" is the word "palms" (the set phrase, as at 10:3).

For Job 28: open with a quotation mark and close the speech at 28:28 (29:1 is a new heading, the words of 27:1). 28:1 begins with "For" and takes up "silver" and "dust" from 27:16; "place" (28:1, 6, 12, 20, 23) is the word of 27:21 and 27:23. Keep "put an end to" and "limit" (28:3; 18:2; 11:7; 26:10), "deep darkness" and "the shadow of death" (28:3), "overturn" (28:5, 9; 9:5), "pathway", "glimpse" and "fierce lion" (28:7-8; 18:10; 20:9; 4:10), "root" (28:9), "secret" (28:11; 11:6), "bring out to the light" (28:11; 12:22), "wisdom" and "understanding" (28:12, 20, 28), "a mortal" (28:13), "the gold of Ophir" (28:16; 22:24), "exchange" (28:17; 15:31), "the birds of the sky" (28:21; 12:7), "Abaddon" and "death" (28:22; 26:6), "edges" or "ends" for the word of 26:14 (28:24), "weigh" (28:25; 6:2), "decree" or "limit" for the word of 14:5 (28:26), "recount", "prepare" and "search out" (28:27; 12:8; 15:35; 5:27), and in 28:28 "the fear of the Lord" (the Hebrew has "Lord" there, not "Yahweh"), "turn away from evil" (1:1) and "a human being" or "humankind" for the word of 5:7 and 7:20.
