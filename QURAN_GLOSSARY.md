# Quran glossary of settled renderings

Recurring Arabic terms and the default English chosen for them, so every part stays consistent.
Read this before translating. Use the listed rendering wherever it fits; where another wording fits
a sentence better, use it, with no bracket (QURAN_RULES.md, "Alternate readings and wording choices").
When a part settles a new recurring term, add one line here in the same commit, under a heading for
that surah. Close calls go in the part's decision log, not here.
(Transliteration is fine in this file. It is never allowed in the translation or decision files.)

## Decided by Obay
- Allah: "God" (not "Allah"). Decided 2026-11-22.
- 2026-11-22: the rules in QURAN_RULES.md are Obay's: from the Arabic only, plain English, nothing
  added, nothing from scholars or tradition, a bracket only for a real second reading.

## Conventions
- ilah "god" (lowercase). Pronouns for God lowercase, including the plural "we" and "us".
- Names with an established English form take it (Musa "Moses", Ibrahim "Abraham", Isa "Jesus",
  Maryam "Mary", Jibril "Gabriel", Firawn "Pharaoh"). Names with none are written in plain Latin
  letters.
- Technical words are translated by meaning: islam "submission", muslim "one who submits".
- The unnumbered opening line of a surah is a `title:` line worded exactly as 1:1.

## Settled in Surah 1
- bismi llahi r-rahmani r-rahim (1:1; 27:30): "In the name of God, the Most Merciful, the Merciful."
  This is the `title:` line of every other surah except Surah 9, word for word, period included.
- ar-rahman "the Most Merciful" (1:1, 3): the fuller form of the mercy-word, said only of God, always
  with "the", and often standing alone as his name (17:110; 19:18; 20:5; 25:60; 55:1). Capital
  letters. It is not the superlative arham ar-rahimin (7:151; 12:64, 92; 21:83), which needs its own
  wording with what follows it ("the most merciful of those who show mercy"), in lowercase.
- rahim "merciful"; ar-rahim "the Merciful" (1:1, 3): the ordinary adjective, said of people too
  (9:128; 48:29), so that it is heard with rahma "mercy" and rahima "have mercy on" (67:28, just
  before ar-rahman at 67:29). Lowercase when it is not a title ("forgiving, merciful").
- hamd "praise"; al-hamdu lillah "Praise belongs to God" (1:2; 10:10; 45:36): a statement, like lahu
  l-hamd "praise belongs to him" (64:1), with no "all" and no "be". shukr and sabbaha are other words
  and need other English.
- rabb "Lord" (1:2) of God; of a human master "lord" (12:41, 42, 50). rabb al-'alamin "Lord of the
  worlds", with "the" before "Lord" where the sentence needs it (2:131).
- 'alamin "worlds" (1:2) in the title rabb al-'alamin (42 times), which the Quran explains at 26:23-28.
  The 31 times the word stands without rabb it is used of people (2:47; 3:42; 7:80; 29:10; 81:27),
  and there "all peoples" may fit the sentence better. Bracketed once, at 1:2 (decision log,
  call: ask); no standing bracket after that.
- maalik "Master" (1:4; 3:26 maalik al-mulk "Master of the kingdom"), "owner" where that fits better
  (36:71). malik "King" (114:2) is the other reading at 1:4 (decision log, call: clear). malaka "have
  power over" (82:19; 34:42); mulk "kingdom" (40:16; 25:26).
- yawm ad-din "the Day of Judgment" (1:4; 82:17-19), with capitals as the name of the Day; "that day"
  stays lowercase. din here is the judging and repaying of what people did (24:25; 37:53; 51:6; 82:9;
  107:1). din as the way a person serves God (109:6) is another sense, left to be settled where it
  first comes. The Day's other names are other words and need their own English: yawm al-qiyama,
  yawm al-hisab, yawm al-fasl (37:20-21, where yawm ad-din and yawm al-fasl stand side by side).
- iyyaka before its verb (1:5): "It is you we ...", the object set first as in the Arabic. No
  "alone" or "only": the Arabic has no such word. So also iyyaya (2:40; 16:51; 29:56).
- 'abada "worship" (1:5; 51:56; 109:2); 'ibada "worship". 'abd stays "servant", so the kinship of the
  two words (4:172) is not shown; "serve" where "worship" does not fit the sentence (23:47).
  Not a bracket: it is one meaning that English splits in two.
- ista'ana "ask for help" (1:5; 2:45; 7:128); al-musta'an (12:18; 21:112) "the one whose help is
  asked", so that 1:5 is heard there. nasr is another word and needs other English.
- hada "guide"; huda "guidance" (1:6; 2:2). With the path as its direct object (1:6; 37:118; 4:68)
  "guide to": "to" is the word the Quran itself uses where it writes one (ila: 6:161; 38:22; 42:52).
- sirat "path"; as-sirat al-mustaqim "the straight path" (1:6); mustaqim "straight". The image stays
  a path. sabil and tariq are other words and need other English ("way", "road").
- an'ama 'ala "favor" (1:7; 4:69; 19:58), "show favor to" where that reads better; ni'ma "favor"
  (2:40). Keep "bless" for baraka, and find another word for faddala, which stands beside an'ama at
  2:47.
- ghadab "anger" (not "wrath"); al-maghdub 'alayhim "those who have incurred anger" (1:7): a passive
  that names no one as angry, so no "your" is added. ghadiba 'ala (58:14) "be angry with". ba'a
  bi-ghadab (2:61, 90; 3:112) is another phrase and should not share the wording of 1:7.
- dalla "go astray"; ad-dallin "those who have gone astray" (1:7; 26:20), dall "astray" (93:7);
  adalla "lead astray".
- ghayri ... wa la (1:7) "not ..., nor ...", set straight after "those you have favored" with no
  second "of", as open as the Arabic: the words can describe the favored or set two other groups
  beside them.
- Capitals: a title that stands for God takes a capital ("Lord", "the Most Merciful", "the Merciful",
  "Master"); the same word as a plain description stays lowercase. Pronouns for God stay lowercase.

## Settled in Surah 2
Verses 1-29:
- The opening letters (2:1): "A. L. M.", followed once in the surah by the note "[These are three
  letters of the Arabic alphabet, standing on their own.]" So also where other surahs open with letters.
- dhalika "that", ula'ika "those" (2:2, 5, 16, 27), not "this" and "these". dhalika l-kitab "That is the
  Book", a full statement like 31:2. al-kitab "the Book", with a capital, when it is the Book sent down.
- rayb "doubt" (2:2, 23); la rayba fihi "there is no doubt in it" (decision log 2:2, call: clear).
- ittaqa and its family: one Arabic word, at root "guard oneself" (the plain verb is "guard": 2:201;
  66:6). With God as its object "be mindful of God" (2:194, 206), and so too where no object is said:
  al-muttaqin "those who are mindful of God" (2:2, 177), la'allakum tattaqun "so that you may be
  mindful of him" or "of God" (2:21, 183), taqwa "mindfulness of God" (2:197). With the Fire or a day
  as its object "guard yourselves against" (2:24, 48, 281). Not "fear": the Quran sets this word
  beside the fear-words as something else (24:52; 2:40-41), and khafa, khashiya and rahiba each
  need their own English. The lost echo is noted once, at 2:24; no standing note.
- amana "believe", amana bi "believe in", alladhina amanu "those who believe", mu'min "believer".
  kafara "disbelieve", alladhina kafaru "those who disbelieve", al-kafirun "the disbelievers"; the
  same verb is "be ungrateful" where thanks are in view (2:152). The Arabic past tense in these set
  phrases is an English present.
- al-ghayb "the unseen" (2:3, 33). Bracketed once, at 2:3 (decision log, call: clear).
- aqama s-salat "keep up the prayer" (2:3, 43, 110, 177, 277); salat "prayer". hafiza 'ala (2:238) is
  another verb and needs other English.
- razaqa "provide", rizq "provision" (2:3, 22, 25, 57, 60). anfaqa "spend" (2:3, 215, 261).
- anzala and nazzala "send down" (2:4, 22, 23, 41, 90, 97, 176): one English for both forms.
- al-akhira "the hereafter" (2:4); al-yawm al-akhir "the Last Day" (2:8, 62). ayqana "be certain"
  (2:4, 118).
- 'ala hudan "on guidance" (2:5; 31:5; 34:24): the picture of standing on it stays. aflaha "succeed",
  al-muflihun "the ones who succeed" (2:5, 189).
- inna usually has no English word: it makes a statement firm, which a plain English statement
  already is. "As for ..." where it sets up the ones spoken about (2:6). ala (2:12, 13) "Truly,".
- andhara "warn" (2:6); nadhir "warner" (2:119).
- khatama 'ala "set a seal on" (2:7; 6:46; 45:23). qalb "heart"; sam' "hearing"; basar, plural absar,
  "sight" (2:7, 20), leaving "eye" for 'ayn. ghishawa "covering".
- 'adhab "punishment" (2:7, 10); 'azim "great"; alim "painful". lahum 'adhab "for them there is a
  punishment", and anna lahum jannat "that for them there are gardens" (2:25): the Arabic has no
  tense here.
- an-nas "people" or "the people" by the sentence (2:8, 13, 24). ya ayyuha n-nas "People," (2:21,
  168), with no "O".
- khada'a "deceive"; its longer form (2:9; 4:142) "try to deceive". sha'ara "realize" (2:9, 12, 154),
  keeping "know" for 'alima (2:13, 22).
- marad "sickness"; zada "increase" (2:10, 58).
- kadhaba "lie" (2:10); kadhdhaba "call a lie", of a person "call a liar" (2:39, 87), so that the two
  are heard together. They are the two readings at 2:10 (decision log, call: clear).
- afsada fi l-ard "cause corruption on the earth" (2:11, 27, 30, 205), fasad "corruption"; aslaha "set
  things right" (2:11, 160, 220). al-ard "the earth"; "the land" where one country is meant.
- safih, plural sufaha', "fool" (2:13, 142).
- ash-shaytan "Satan" (2:36); the plural shayatin "devils" (2:14, 102).
- istahza'a "mock" (2:14, 15).
- tughyan "excess" (2:15): going beyond the bounds (20:24; 69:11). madda fi "keep going in" (2:15;
  7:202). 'amiha "wander blindly".
- ishtara A bi-B "buy A in exchange for B" (2:16, 86, 175): B is what is given up. dalala "error"
  (2:16, 175), of the family of dalla "go astray". tijara "trade"; rabiha "make a profit". ihtada
  "be guided" (2:16, 53).
- mathal "example" (2:17, 26, 171, 261): mathaluhum ka-mathali "Their example is like that of",
  daraba mathalan "give an example".
- nur "light"; zulumat "darkness" (2:17, 19, 257): the Arabic word is always plural, the English is
  not. ada'a "light up" (2:17, 20). dhahaba bi "take away" (2:17, 20).
- summ, bukm, 'umy "deaf, mute, blind" (2:18, 171). raja'a "return" (2:18); turja'un "you will be
  returned" (2:28, 245).
- sayyib "rainstorm"; ra'd "thunder"; barq "lightning"; sa'iqa "thunderbolt" (2:19, 55). hadhara
  l-mawt "for fear of death" (2:19, 243). ahata bi "surround" (2:19, 81).
- law sha'a llah "if God had willed" (2:20). inna llaha 'ala kulli shay'in qadir "God has power over
  all things" (2:20, 106, 109, 148, 259, 284). bi-kulli shay'in 'alim "has knowledge of all things"
  (2:29, 231, 282).
- khalaqa "create" (2:21, 29).
- as-sama' "the sky" (2:19, 22, 29); its plural as-samawat "the heavens" (2:29, 33). firash "bed";
  bina' "building" (2:22; the Quran speaks of the sky as built: 51:47; 79:27).
- nidd, plural andad, "rival" (2:22, 165); ja'ala li-llahi andadan "set up rivals to God".
- sura "surah" (2:23; 10:38; 24:1): the English word this translation itself uses for the 114 parts
  of the Quran.
- min duni llah "other than God" (2:23); "apart from God" where that reads better.
- shahid, plural shuhada', "witness" (2:23). in kuntum sadiqin "if you are telling the truth" (2:23,
  31, 94, 111).
- an-nar "the Fire", with a capital, when it is the Fire of the hereafter (2:24, 39); nar "a fire"
  (2:17). waqud "fuel"; u'iddat "prepared".
- bashshara "give good news to" (2:25, 155); bushra "good news" (2:97).
- 'amila s-salihat "do righteous deeds" (2:25, 82, 277), of the family of aslaha "set things right".
  Keep "good" for khayr, hasana and ahsana.
- janna "garden"; jannat tajri min tahtiha l-anhar "gardens with rivers flowing beneath them" (2:25,
  266). al-janna "the Garden" (2:35, 82).
- tashabaha "look alike", "be alike" (2:25, 70, 118).
- zawj, plural azwaj, "spouse" (2:25, 35, 102); mutahhara "purified". hum fiha khalidun "they will
  remain there forever", "in it forever" (2:25, 39, 81, 82).
- istahya "shy away from" (2:26; 33:53). al-haqq "the truth" (2:26, 42, 147). arada "mean", of what
  is said (2:26; 74:31).
- fasaqa "defy", al-fasiqun "the defiant", fusuq "defiance" (2:26, 59, 99, 197, 282); 18:50 shows the
  sense, where the same verb is said of one who would not do what his Lord commanded. 'asa
  "disobey" (2:61, 93) is another word.
- 'ahd "covenant" (2:27, 40, 124), "promise" between people (2:177); mithaq "pledge" (2:63, 83, 84,
  93), and min ba'di mithaqihi "after it has been made binding" (2:27; 13:25), since the word is from
  tying fast (5:7). naqada "break".
- qata'a "cut"; wasala "join" (2:27). khasir "loser" (2:27, 64, 121), the word that answers the trade
  of 2:16.
- ahya "give life"; amata "cause to die" (2:28, 258); amwat "dead".
- istawa ila "turn to" (2:29; 41:11); sawwa "form" (2:29; 18:37).
