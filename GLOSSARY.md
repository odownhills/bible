# Glossary of settled renderings

Recurring Hebrew, Aramaic and Greek terms and the English chosen for them, so every chapter
stays consistent. Read this before translating. Use the listed rendering unless the context
truly cannot bear it, and then put the listed rendering in an `[alt: ...]`.
When a chapter settles a new recurring term, add one line here in the same commit.
(Transliteration is fine in this file. It is never allowed in the chapter files.)

## Decided by Obay
- nephesh chayyah: "living creature" everywhere (Gen 1:20, 21, 24; 2:7, 19). Where the phrase is
  not a countable creature (Gen 1:30, "in which there is nephesh chayyah"), write
  "the life of a living creature [alt: the breath of life]". Decided 2026-11-19.
- nishmat chayyim (Gen 2:7): "the breath of life". This is a different phrase from nephesh chayyah;
  keep the two distinct.

## Settled in Genesis 1-2
- Elohim "God"; YHWH "Yahweh"; YHWH Elohim "Yahweh God"; Adonai "Lord".
- bara "create"; asah "make". Never interchange them.
- mashal (the lights) "govern"; radah (humankind) "rule over".
- raqia "vault" [alt: expanse]. shamayim "heavens" in 1:1 and 2:1, 4; "sky" for the visible sky.
- hinneh "look" (never "behold").
- tohu wabohu "desolate and empty" [alt: formless and empty].
- ruach elohim "the spirit of God" [alt: the wind of God, or the breath of God], lowercase.
- adam: "humankind" for the collective (1:26-27); "the human" with the article in chapter 2;
  "Adam" only where it stands without the article as a name.
- ish / ishshah "man" / "woman".
- min "kind" ("according to their kinds").
- remes / romes "creeping things" / "creeps". chayyat ha'arets / hassadeh "wild animals of the earth / field".
- behemah "livestock".
- tov wara "good and bad" [alt: good and evil].
- shavat "cease" [alt: rest].
- toledot "generations" [alt: account].
- Sentence-opening "and" is kept where the Hebrew narrative chain has it; "Now" marks a break in the chain.
- Day formula: "And there was evening and there was morning" followed by a dash and the day.

## Settled in Genesis 3
- nachash "snake". arum (3:1) "shrewd". arur "cursed".
- Infinitive absolute with its own verb: "certainly" (2:16-17; 3:4); harbah arbeh "greatly multiply" (3:16).
- pen "so that ... not" (3:3, 22).
- ta'avah "delight"; nechmad "desirable"; teshuqah "longing" (3:16; 4:7). Keep the three distinct.
- haskil (3:6) "gain insight" [alt: contemplate].
- qol "sound" (3:8, 10) or "voice" (3:17), by context.
- eivah "hostility".
- zera: "seed" for plants (chapter 1); "offspring" [alt: seed] for descendants (3:15).
- shuf (3:15) "strike" [alt: crush, or bruise], the same English both times.
- itsavon "painful toil" (3:16, 17; 5:29); etsev "pain".
- mashal stays "govern" in 3:16 (the husband) and 4:7, so it remains distinct from radah "rule over".
- ish / ishshah with a possessive: "husband" / "wife".
- adam without the article (2:20; 3:17, 21): "Adam [alt: the human]".
- Chavvah "Eve". keruvim "cherubim". kuttonet "tunic".
- shalach (piel) "send out"; garash "drive out".
- shamar "watch over" (2:15) or "guard" (3:24), by context.
- hen "look", the same as hinneh.

## Settled in Genesis 4
- yada (sexual: 4:1, 17, 25) "knew" [alt: was intimate with], kept distinct from "lie with" and "go in to".
- harah "conceive". yalad: of a mother "give birth to" (the same verb as "bear children" in 3:16); of a father (4:18) "fathered"; passive "was born". yeled (4:23) "boy" [alt: young man].
- qanah (4:1) "acquire" [alt: bring into being].
- ro'eh "shepherd"; tson "flock(s)"; miqneh "herds", kept distinct from behemah "livestock".
- minchah "offering". sha'ah el (4:4-5) "look at" [alt: look with favor on].
- charah le- "be angry". hetiv (4:7) "do good". se'et (4:7) "a lifting up" [alt: be accepted, or forgiveness].
- chatta't "sin". ravats (4:7) "crouch". petach "entrance" ("door" is kept for delet).
- harag "kill". hikkah (4:15) "strike down", kept distinct from shuf "strike". naqam "avenge" / "take vengeance"; shiv'atayim "seven times over".
- shomer (4:9) "guardian". tsa'aq "cry out".
- avon "punishment" or "guilt", by context (never "iniquity").
- na wanad (4:12, 14) "a drifter and a wanderer".
- panim "face" (4:5-6, 14); millifnei YHWH "from the presence of Yahweh" (4:16), as in 3:8.
- ot "sign" (1:14; 4:15). yashav "live" (4:16, 20). lakhen "therefore".
- kinnor "lyre"; ugav "pipe"; nechoshet "bronze"; barzel "iron".
- shit (4:25) "set", so that the echo with the name "Seth" survives in English.
- qara beshem YHWH "call on the name of Yahweh". qara shem "name" (verb), as in 3:20.
- wayehi before a time clause: "And after ..." / "And when ...", with no separate "it came to pass".
- adam with no article and no preposition (4:25): "Adam", with no alt.
- Where the Hebrew text breaks off or spells a name two ways (4:8, 18), the main text gives a readable form and the other goes in an [alt: ...].

## Settled in Genesis 5
- sefer (5:1) "book". demut "likeness"; tselem "image" (1:26-27; 5:1, 3), keeping the prepositions as they stand: be- "in", ke- "according to".
- adam in 5:1b-2 (the collective, as in 1:26-27): "humankind" [alt: Adam]; in 5:1a and 5:3-5 it is the name "Adam".
- holid (hiphil of yalad; 5:3-32) "fathered", the same English as qal yalad of a father (4:18). Where it has no object (5:3): "fathered one", so that it stays distinct from "fathered a son" (5:28).
- Genealogy formula: "And X lived N years, and he fathered Y. And X lived N years after he fathered Y, and he fathered sons and daughters. And all the days of X were N years, and he died."
- Numbers are written out in words in normal English order ("a hundred and thirty years", "nine hundred and sixty-nine years"). ben N shanah "N years old".
- hithallekh et-ha'elohim (5:22, 24) "walked with God"; hithallekh on its own stays "walk about" (3:8). ha'elohim (with the article) "God", the same as Elohim.
- einennu (5:24) "he was no more". laqach "take". mut "die".
- nacham (piel; 5:29) "comfort"; with min, "bring comfort from".
- ma'aseh (5:29) "work" (it shares its English with melakhah "work" in 2:2-3).
- Names: Seth, Enosh, Kenan, Mahalalel, Jared, Enoch, Methuselah, Lamech, Noah, Shem, Ham, Japheth.

## Settled in 1 John 1
- Greek text: the main text follows the earliest manuscripts, as in the standard critical editions. Where many manuscripts read differently in a way that changes the meaning (1:4), the other reading goes in an [alt: ..., as other manuscripts read].
- arche "beginning" (ap' arches "from the beginning"). logos "word"; ho logos tes zoes (1:1) "the word of life" [alt: the Word of life].
- zoe "life"; he zoe he aionios "the eternal life" (aionios "eternal").
- akouo "hear"; horao "see"; theaomai "gaze at", kept distinct from horao. pselaphao "feel" (with the hands), kept distinct from "touch" (Gen 3:3).
- phaneroo "reveal"; passive "was revealed".
- martyreo "testify". apangello "announce"; anangello "declare"; angelia "message". Keep the two verbs distinct.
- koinonia "fellowship"; koinonian echein meta "have fellowship with".
- pater of God "the Father"; huios of Jesus "Son" ("his Son"), both capitalized as titles. Pronouns for God and for Jesus stay lowercase, as in Genesis. Iesous Christos "Jesus Christ".
- chara "joy"; pleroo of joy (1:4) "make full", so that "complete" stays free for another verb.
- phos "light". skotia (1:5) and skotos (1:6) both "darkness": two forms of one word, like hen / hinneh.
- peripateo "walk". pseudomai "lie"; pseustes "liar". aletheia "truth"; poiein ten aletheian (1:6) "do the truth".
- haima "blood". katharizo "cleanse". hamartia "sin" (the same English as chatta't); hamartano "sin" (verb).
- planao "lead astray", kept distinct from "deceive" (Gen 3:13).
- homologeo "confess". pistos "faithful". dikaios "righteous" [alt: just]; adikia "unrighteousness", so that the echo between the two survives. aphiemi of sins "forgive".
- hina "so that" (purpose in 1:3-4; result in 1:9).
- An explicit subject pronoun that carries emphasis: "we ourselves" (hemeis, 1:4), "he himself" (autos, 1:7).
- Sentence-opening kai is kept as "And", as with the Hebrew narrative chain. kai ... de (1:3) "And indeed".
- The opening sentence (1:1-3) breaks off and resumes in the Greek; the breaks are marked with dashes and the sentence is not smoothed out.

## Settled in 1 John 2
- Verse numbers: the sentence to the paidia ("I have written to you, young children") opens 2:14, as in the Greek editions and many English versions; the KJV counts it as the end of 2:13. The chapter's `numbering:` line says so once.
- Greek text, applying the 1 John 1 line: another reading is noted only where it changes what the sentence says (2:20, pantes "all of you know" / panta "you know all things"). A different form of address, a different tense, or an added or missing phrase that leaves the sense intact is not noted (2:7, 14, 23, 27).
- teknia "little children"; paidia "young children" (leaving "children" for tekna). pateres "fathers"; neaniskoi "young men". adelphos "brother". agapetoi (address) "Beloved".
- grapho "I am writing"; egrapsa (of this letter: 2:14, 21, 26) "I have written".
- parakletos "advocate". hilasmos "atonement" [alt: appeasement]. kosmos "world".
- ginosko and oida both "know" (English has no second plain verb for it); perfect egnoka "have come to know". en touto ginoskomen "by this we know".
- entole "commandment"; tereo "keep". opheilo "ought".
- ho with a participle "the one who"; hos an "whoever"; pas ho "everyone who"; ean tis "if anyone". houtos of a person (2:4-5) "this person". ekeinos of Jesus (2:6) "that one", kept distinct from autos "he".
- agape "love" (noun); agapao "love" (verb); miseo "hate". he agape tou theou / tou patros "the love of God / of the Father", leaving the genitive as open in English as it is in Greek.
- teleioo "make complete" (2:5 "has been made complete"); this is the verb that "complete" was kept free for.
- meno "remain" (never "abide"). kathos "just as"; hos "as".
- kainos "new"; palaios "old". palin where it marks a contrast (2:8) "then again".
- alethes and alethinos both "true" (two adjectives from one root, like skotia / skotos); alethos "truly". pseudos "a lie".
- paragomai "pass away". phaino "shine". heos arti "until now". skandalon "cause of stumbling". typhloo "blind" (verb). hypago "go".
- nikao "conquer". poneros "evil"; ho poneros "the evil one". ischyros "strong".
- epithymia "desire" (kept apart from "delight", "desirable" and "longing" in Genesis 3). sarx "flesh". alazoneia "arrogance". bios "possessions" [alt: life], kept distinct from zoe "life". thelema "will". eis ton aiona "forever".
- einai ek "be from" (2:16, 19, 21) and gennao (passive) ek "be born from" (2:29): the same English preposition for both, so that the link between them survives. exerchomai ek "go out from".
- eschate hora "the last hour". antichristos "antichrist", lowercase: without the article (2:18) "antichrist", with it (2:22) "the antichrist". ginomai of the antichrists (2:18) "arise". hothen "from which".
- chrisma "anointing". ho Christos as a title (2:22) "the Christ". The echo between anointing, Christ and antichrist is explained in one bracketed note (2:20). ho hagios (2:20) "the Holy One".
- arneomai "deny". echein ton patera "have the Father". epangelia "promise"; epangellomai "promise" (verb). didasko "teach". chreian echein hina (2:27) "have need for (anyone) to".
- hymeis standing first, outside its clause (2:24, 27): "As for you"; as an emphatic subject (2:20): "you yourselves".
- ean of an expected event (2:28) "when" [alt: if]; elsewhere "if". parresia "boldness". aischynomai apo (2:28) "be ashamed before". parousia "coming".
- dikaiosyne "righteousness"; poiein ten dikaiosynen "do righteousness", like "do the truth". The [alt: just] for dikaios is given at its first occurrence in a chapter (2:1), not at every one.
- hoti in 2:12-14 "because" [alt: that], the alt given once, at 2:12. An aorist is put in the English perfect where a past act explains a present state (2:11 "has blinded").

## Settled in 1 John 3
- Greek text, applying the 1 John 1 line: where the earliest manuscripts divide over a sentence-opening kai (3:13, 19) it is left out, and 3:7 reads teknia "Little children" (not paidia); none of these is noted, since the sense is intact.
- idete (3:1; the imperative of horao) "See". potapos "what kind of". homoios "like".
- dia touto "for this reason"; eis touto (3:8) "for this purpose"; charin tinos (3:12) "for what reason".
- hina after a noun whose content it gives (3:11 the message, 3:23 the commandment): "that we should"; elsewhere still "so that" (3:1, 5, 8).
- The aorist egno (3:1) "did not know", a simple past, kept apart from the perfect "have come to know".
- ean phanerothe (3:2) "when he is revealed" [alt: when it is revealed, or if he is revealed], as in 2:28.
- elpis "hope"; elpida echein epi (3:3) "have hope set on". hagnizo "purify"; hagnos "pure", kept distinct from katharizo "cleanse".
- poiein ten hamartian "do sin", like "do righteousness". anomia "lawlessness". airo (3:5) "take away".
- diabolos "the devil", lowercase. lyo (3:8) "undo" [alt: destroy]. ho huios tou theou "the Son of God".
- ergon "work" (ta erga "the works": 3:8, 12); in the pair with logos (3:18) "deed".
- sperma (3:9) "seed" [alt: offspring], the mirror image of zera in Gen 3:15. ou dynatai "cannot".
- phaneros (3:10) "revealed" (phanera estin "are revealed"), so that the echo with phaneroo survives.
- pas ho with a participle and a negated verb stays "everyone who ... does not" (3:6, 9, 10, as in 2:23); pas with a noun and a negative (3:15, as in 2:21) "no ...".
- ou kathos (3:12) "not as", with no alt: "not just as" would read in English as "not merely as". Kain "Cain". sphazo "slaughter", kept distinct from "kill" and "strike down" (Gen 4).
- thaumazo (3:13) "be surprised". metabaino ek ... eis "cross over from ... into". thanatos "death". anthropoktonos "murderer". zoe aionios without the article "eternal life".
- psyche (3:16) "life" [alt: soul], a different word from zoe "life"; the alt is given once in a chapter. tithenai ten psychen hyper "lay down one's life for".
- bios (3:17) "possessions", with no alt where "life" is not a possible reading. theoreo (3:17) "see", the same English as horao (no second plain verb fits here; "gaze at" stays for theaomai). chreian echon "in need".
- splanchna (3:17) "compassion" (kleio ta splanchna apo "shut off one's compassion from"), kept distinct from kardia "heart". glossa "tongue".
- kardia "heart", singular as in the Greek ("our heart"). emprosthen "before"; enopion "in the sight of". peitho (3:19) "reassure" [alt: persuade]. meizon "greater".
- kataginosko "condemn"; its echo with ginosko "know" is explained in one bracketed note (3:20).
- 3:19-20: the first hoti ean is read as ho ti ean "in whatever" and the second hoti as "because"; the other reading ("persuade our heart that, if ...") is the alt.
- parresian echein pros "have boldness toward". aiteo "ask"; lambano "receive". arestos "pleasing". allelous "one another".
- pisteuo with the dative (3:23) "believe" [alt: believe in], leaving "believe in" for pisteuo eis. onoma "name". didonai entolen "give a commandment".
- pneuma (3:24) "spirit", lowercase, as with ruach elohim in Genesis 1:2.
- Where a hoti clause depends on the verse before (3:11), the sentence runs on across the verse break as "because", rather than opening a new sentence with "For" (kept for gar).

## Settled in 1 John 4
- Greek text, applying the 1 John 1 line: the other reading is noted at 4:19 (agapomen with no object; many manuscripts add "him", some "God"). Not noted, since the sense is intact: 4:3, where many manuscripts repeat "Christ as having come in the flesh" from 4:2, and 4:20, where many read a question ("how can he love ...?").
- pneuma stays "spirit", lowercase, all through the chapter: to pneuma tou theou "the spirit of God" (as with ruach elohim), "the spirit of truth", "his spirit". to tou antichristou (4:3) "the spirit of the antichrist": the neuter article stands for pneuma, so the noun is repeated in English.
- dokimazo "test". pseudoprophetes "false prophet". laleo "speak". exerchomai eis "go out into".
- homologein Iesoun Christon en sarki eleluthota (4:2) "confess Jesus Christ as having come in the flesh" [alt: confess Jesus as the Christ who has come in the flesh]; a perfect participle "having come", "has come".
- An emphatic subject pronoun, continued: hymeis / autoi / hemeis (4:4-6) "You yourselves / They themselves / We ourselves"; kai hemeis in a main clause after "if" (4:11) "we ourselves also".
- ho en with a noun (4:4) "the one in". hos with the indicative (4:6) "whoever", the same English as hos an.
- akouo with the genitive of a person (4:5-6) stays "hear" [alt: listen to], the alt given once in a chapter.
- ek toutou (4:6) "from this", kept distinct from en touto "by this". en touto estin (4:10) "in this is"; en touto with any other verb stays "by this" (4:9, 13, 17), with a comma where "this" could be misread with the next noun (4:17).
- plane "error" [alt: leading astray]; the alt carries the echo with planao "lead astray". With plane, he aletheia loses its article in English ("the spirit of truth and the spirit of error").
- ouk egno (4:8) "did not know", as in 3:1, even with a present-tense subject; the perfect stays "have come to know" (4:16).
- en hemin "in us" all through the chapter (4:12, 13), with the other reading as an alt where it is likely: 4:9 [alt: among us]; 4:16 agapen echein en "have love in" [alt: for].
- monogenes "only" ("his only Son") [alt: only-born]. apostello "send": perfect "has sent" (4:9, 14), aorist "sent" (4:10). zao "live"; dia with the genitive "through".
- A second accusative naming a role (4:10, 14): "as" ("as the atonement", "as savior of the world"). soter "savior", lowercase.
- houtos (the adverb; 4:11) "in this way" [alt: so much]. popote "ever". protos "first".
- pisteuo with the accusative (4:16) "believe"; with the dative of a thing or spirit (4:1) "believe", with no alt.
- teleios "complete" (he teleia agape "complete love"), so that the echo with teleioo "make complete" survives; teteleiomene estin (4:12) "has been made complete", the same English as teteleiotai.
- meta with the genitive (4:17) "with". hemera tes kriseos "the day of judgment"; krisis "judgment". kathos ... kai (4:17) "just as ..., so", as in 2:18.
- phobos "fear"; phobeomai "fear" (verb; ho phoboumenos "the one who fears"). exo ballein "throw out", kept distinct from "drive out" (Gen 3:24). kolasis "punishment"; kolasin echei "has punishment", left as compressed as the Greek.
- agapomen (4:19), which can be a statement or an appeal: "we love" [alt: let us love]; in 4:7, without a pronoun and after "Beloved", "let us love".
- hina after entole (4:21) "that ... should", as in 3:23.

## Settled in 1 John 5
- Greek text, applying the 1 John 1 line: 5:7-8 follows the earliest manuscripts, and almost all the others with them: "there are three who testify, the spirit and the water and the blood". The longer reading (three who testify "in heaven, the Father, the Word and the Holy Spirit", and three "on earth"), found in a few late manuscripts and in the KJV, is not in the main text and has no note; whether to note it is for Obay to decide. The verse break falls where the Greek editions and most modern English versions put it.
- 5:18: the earliest manuscripts divide between auton ("the one who was born from God keeps him") and heauton ("keeps himself"), and the sense changes, so both are given: auton in the main text, as in the editions whose readings were followed at 1:7 and 3:7, and heauton as the alt.
- Not noted, since the sense is intact: 5:2 (many read "keep" for "do"), 5:5 (de, which the earliest have, is kept as "And"), 5:10 (en heauto / en auto, both "in himself"), 5:13 (many add "and so that you may believe in the name of the Son of God"), 5:21 (many add "Amen"). Also not noted: 5:6, where some add "and spirit" after "water and blood", and 5:10, where a few read "the Son" for "God".
- gennao active (5:1) "father", as with holid in Genesis 5: ho gennesas "the one who fathered", left without an object as in the Greek; its echo with the passive "be born" is explained in one bracketed note. Perfect passive "has been born" (5:1, 4, 18); the aorist ho gennetheis (5:18) "the one who was born", kept apart from the perfect.
- hotan (5:2) "when". poiein tas entolas (5:2) "do his commandments", kept distinct from tereo "keep" (5:3). barys (5:3) "burdensome".
- hina after "the love of God" (5:3) and after lego (5:16) "that ... should", as in 3:11, 23.
- pan to with a neuter participle (5:4) "everything that". nike "victory"; pistis "faith". Their echoes with nikao "conquer" and pisteuo "believe" are explained in one bracketed note (5:4). The aorist participle he nikesasa (5:4) "that conquered", a simple past, kept apart from the perfect "have conquered" (2:13-14; 4:4).
- de where it carries the thought forward (5:5, 20) "And"; where it contrasts it stays "but".
- hydor "water". erchomai dia (5:6) "come through"; en to hydati / en to haimati "in the water / in the blood" [alt: with], keeping en distinct from dia.
- to martyroun (5:6, neuter) "the one that testifies"; hoi martyrountes (5:7, masculine) "who testify".
- A hoti that opens a new sentence in the Greek editions (5:7) still runs on as "because", after a semicolon, as in 3:11.
- eis to hen einai (5:8) "be at one" [alt: be one].
- martyria "testimony"; he martyria hen memartyreken (5:10) "the testimony that God has testified", keeping the cognate object as in 2:25. anthropoi (5:9) "human beings".
- pisteuo eis "believe in" (5:10, 13); pisteuo with the dative of God (5:10) "believe", with no alt: the point is that what God has said is not believed.
- he zoe with the article (5:12) "the life", pointing back to "this life" (5:11).
- A dative participle set after its clause, in apposition to "you" or "him" (5:13, 16), stays at the end of the English sentence.
- kata with the accusative (5:14) "according to". ho ean (5:15) "whatever", as in 3:22. aitema "request" (noun; it shares its English with erotao). erotao (5:16) "make a request", kept distinct from aiteo "ask".
- hamartanein hamartian (5:16) "sin a sin", keeping the cognate object. pros thanaton "toward death", pros as in "boldness toward"; me and ou before it are both "not". tois hamartanousin me pros thanaton "those sinning not toward death": the participle is kept as "-ing" here, because "those who sin not" would read as old English for "do not sin".
- The subject of "he will give" (5:16) is left as open as in the Greek. ekeines (5:16) "that sin": the feminine pronoun stands for hamartia, so the noun is repeated, as in 4:3. hamartia without the article (5:16-17) "sin", with no article in English.
- haptomai "touch", the same English as naga in Gen 3:3. keimai en (5:19) "lie in" (of position). holos "whole". en to ponero (5:19) "in the evil one" [alt: in evil].
- heko (5:20) "has come", the same English as the perfect of erchomai (English has no second plain verb for it). dianoia "understanding". ho alethinos "the true one", lowercase like "the righteous one" (2:1) and "the evil one".
- houtos (5:20) "This", with no noun added, so that it points back as openly as in the Greek.
- phylasso (5:21) "guard", the same English as shamar in Gen 3:24, kept distinct from tereo "keep". eidolon "idol"; apo ton eidolon "from idols", without the article in English.

## Settled in John 1
- Greek text, applying the 1 John 1 line: the other reading is noted at 1:18 (monogenes theos in the earliest manuscripts; many read ho monogenes huios "the only Son"), 1:28 (Bethania; others read Bethabara), 1:34 (ho huios tou theou; a few of the earliest read ho eklektos "the chosen one") and 1:42 (Ioannou; many read Iona "Jonah"). Not noted, since the sense is intact: 1:16 (many read kai for the first hoti), 1:19 (pros auton "to him", over which the earliest divide, is kept), 1:27 (the doubtful ego is not marked; many add "who has come to be ahead of me"), 1:39 (many read "Come and see"), 1:41 (proton / protos, both "first"), 1:51 (many add "from now on").
- 1:3-4: ho gegonen is read with what follows, as the Greek editions punctuate it ("What has come to be in him was life"), and the verse break stays where they put it, in mid-sentence; the other division ("not even one thing came to be that has come to be. In him was life") is the alt.
- ho logos (1:1, 14) "the Word", capitalized as a title like "the Father" and "the Son"; logos elsewhere stays "word". pros ton theon (1:1-2) "with God", as in 1 Jn 1:2. 1:1 theos en ho logos "the Word was God", with no alt.
- houtos of a person, standing alone as the subject (1:2, 7, 41) "this one"; houtos estin / en (1:15, 30, 33, 34) "this is / was (the one)". ekeinos (1:8, 18, 33) "that one", as in 1 John.
- ginomai: "come to be" (1:3, 10, 17; perfect "has come to be", 1:3, 15, 30); with a predicate "become" (1:12, 14); of a man appearing (1:6) "arise", as in 1 Jn 2:18; of events (1:28) "take place". Never "was", which is kept for einai: the prologue sets the two verbs against each other.
- panta "all things". oude hen "not even one thing". choris "apart from". para with the genitive "from".
- anthropos: in the plural and of people in general (1:4, 9) "human being(s)", as in 1 Jn 5:9; of one particular man (1:6) "man", sharing its English with aner "man" (1:13 [alt: husband]; 1:30). ho huios tou anthropou (1:51) "the Son of Man", capitalized like "the Son of God" and kept as a fixed title.
- katalambano (1:5) "overcome" [alt: grasp]. photizo "give light to", so that the echo with phos survives.
- apostello and pempo both "send" (English has no second plain verb for it).
- eis martyrian (1:7) "for testimony". all' hina with no verb before it (1:8) "but came so that", the verb taken from 1:7, as "this was" is supplied in 1 Jn 2:19.
- 1:9: erchomenon is read with "light" ("was coming into the world"); the reading with "every human being" is the alt.
- ta idia (1:11) "what was his own"; hoi idioi "those who were his own"; ho idios (1:41) "his own". paralambano "take in", kept distinct from lambano "receive". hosoi "as many as".
- exousia "authority" [alt: the right]. haimata (1:13, a plural) "blood", as with the Hebrew plural in Gen 4:10. Nouns without the article in 1:13 stay without it ("the will of flesh", "the will of a man").
- skenoo (1:14) "pitch one's tent" [alt: live]. en hemin (1:14) "among us", which the tent requires. doxa "glory". charis "grace". pleres "full"; pleroma "fullness". With charis, he aletheia loses its article in English (1:17 "grace and truth"), as with plane in 1 Jn 4:6.
- monogenes with no noun (1:14) "the only Son" [alt: the only-born Son]: on its own the word means an only child. hos monogenous para patros "as of the only Son from the Father" [alt: as of an only son from a father]. monogenes theos (1:18) "the only Son, God" [alt: the only-born God]; "the only God" is left for monos theos (5:44; 17:3).
- krazo "cry out"; boao "shout"; phoneo and kaleo both "call". opiso mou "after me". emprosthen mou with ginomai (1:15, 30) "ahead of me"; protos mou "before me" (protos elsewhere stays "first").
- 1:14-17: verse 15 interrupts the sentence, and 1:16 picks up "full of grace and truth" with hoti. The interruption is marked with dashes, as in 1 Jn 1:1-3, and hoti (1:16, 17) runs on as "because".
- anti (1:16) "in place of" [alt: upon]. nomos "law", lowercase. Mouses "Moses"; Elias "Elijah"; Esaias "Isaiah": people of the Hebrew Bible keep their usual English names.
- kolpos (1:18) "lap" [alt: side], leaving "side" for pleura and "chest" for stethos. exegeomai "make known"; its object ("him") is supplied in English. The aorist exegesato "made known", a simple past beside the perfect "has seen".
- hoi Ioudaioi "the Jews" [alt: the Judeans], the alt given at 1:19. hiereus "priest"; Leuites "Levite"; Pharisaioi "Pharisees".
- erotao of a question (1:19, 21, 25) "question" (verb), kept distinct from aiteo "ask"; of a request it stays "make a request" (1 Jn 5:16). apokrinomai "answer"; apokrisis "answer" (noun). apekrithe kai eipen "answered and said", both verbs kept.
- The historical present in narrative (legei, heuriskei, blepei) is kept as an English present ("he says", "he finds", "he sees"), also where it stands beside a past tense (1:21, 43, 47).
- An emphatic subject pronoun, continued: ego / kago "I myself" / "And I myself" (1:20, 26, 30, 31, 33, 34); hymeis "you yourselves" (1:26). With einai in a question or a statement of who someone is (sy tis ei, sy ei: 1:19, 21, 25, 42, 49) the pronoun is ordinary Greek and is not marked.
- oun in narrative "So"; ti oun "What then?" / "Why then ...?". de that turns aside to give background (1:44) "Now", as in Genesis; otherwise "And" or "But", as in 1 Jn 5.
- kyrios "Lord"; where it stands for the divine name in a quotation from the Hebrew Bible (1:23) "the Lord", translated as the Greek has it. eremos "wilderness". euthyno "make straight". hodos "way".
- baptizo "baptize" [alt: immerse], the alt given once in a chapter. en hydati "in water" [alt: with water], as in 1 Jn 5:6. en pneumati hagio (1:33) "in holy spirit" [alt: in the holy spirit]: lowercase and without the article, as in the Greek, parallel to "in water". hagios "holy".
- mesos hymon "among you". axios "worthy". lyo of a sandal strap (1:27) "undo", the same English as in 1 Jn 3:8. peran "across".
- te epaurion "the next day". blepo "see" (it shares its English with horao and theoreo); emblepo "look at"; theaomai stays "gaze at". ide as an exclamation "Look", the same as hinneh; erchou kai ide (1:46) "Come and see".
- amnos "lamb": ho amnos tou theou "the lamb of God", lowercase like "savior". hyper with a verb of saying (1:30) "about", kept distinct from peri "concerning".
- ouranos "heaven". katabaino "come down"; anabaino "go up". peristera "dove". epi with the accusative (1:32, 33, 51) "on". A relative clause that repeats its pronoun (1:27, 33) gives it once in English.
- mathetes "disciple". akoloutheo "follow". zeteo "seek". heurisko "find". ago "bring". thelo "want". polis "city". apo and ek both "from". agathos "good". dolos "deceit". syke "fig tree"; hypo "under"; hypokato "underneath". basileus "king". angelos "angel". anoigo "open".
- rhabbi "Rabbi", Messias "Messiah" and Kephas "Cephas" are kept as the Greek keeps them, since the text itself goes on to translate them; didaskalos "Teacher". methermeneuo "translate"; hermeneuo "interpret". These asides by the writer stand in parentheses (1:38, 41, 42), and a short bracketed note gives the shared meaning of Messiah / Christ (1:41) and of Cephas / Peter (1:42).
- meno of lodging (1:38-39) "stay" [alt: remain], the alt given once; elsewhere it stays "remain" (1:32, 33). hora "hour", counted as the Greek counts it ("the tenth hour").
- pothen (1:48) "where ... from". pisteueis (1:50) "do you believe?" [alt: you believe]. meizo touton "greater things than these".
- amen amen "Amen, amen" [alt: Truly, truly]: the Greek keeps the Hebrew word untranslated, and "truly" is alethos (1:47).
- A plural "you" spoken to one person (1:51 hymin, opsesthe) "you all", so that the change of number is not lost.
- For Obay to decide (each is easy to reverse): "the Word" capitalized; "the only Son, God" at 1:18; "Amen, amen" or "Truly, truly"; the historical present kept as a present; "you all" for a plural "you" at 1:51; "baptize" or "immerse"; "grace" or "favor" for charis; "the Son of Man" or a rendering with "human being"; whether "[alt: the Judeans]" should be repeated in later chapters; "in holy spirit" with no article and no capital.

## Settled in John 2
- Greek text, applying the 1 John 1 line: the other reading is noted at 2:15 (phragellion "a whip"; the two earliest papyri and many others read hos phragellion "something like a whip"). Not noted, since the sense is intact: 2:4 (the opening kai, which the earliest papyri have, is kept), 2:10 (many add tote "then" before "the lesser"), 2:12 (the doubtful autou after "brothers" is not marked), 2:17 (kataphagetai "will devour"; a few late manuscripts and the KJV read katephagen "has devoured"), 2:22 (many add "to them"), 2:24 (auton / heauton, both "himself"). Also not noted: 2:3, where one early manuscript has a longer wording ("they had no wine, because the wine of the wedding was finished").
- gamos "wedding". meter "mother". oinos "wine". hystereo (2:3) "run short". oupo "not yet". heko of the hour (2:4) "has come", as in 1 Jn 5:20.
- kaleo of a guest (2:2) "invite" [alt: call]; elsewhere it stays "call". A singular verb with two subjects (2:2) is put in ordinary English ("Jesus and his disciples were also invited").
- ti emoi kai soi (2:4) "What do I have to do with you?": the set idiom, in a form that also fits where no "that" is in view (Mark 1:24; 5:7; Judg 11:12). The other reading ("What is that to me and to you?") is the alt. gynai as an address "woman", with nothing added.
- diakonos "servant" (to be kept distinct from doulos). ho ti an "whatever", as with ho ean.
- hydria "water jar"; lithinos "stone". keimai of things put in place (2:6) "be set" (keimai en "lie in" stays for 1 Jn 5:19). katharismos "cleansing", like katharizo "cleanse"; kata ton katharismon "according to the cleansing" [alt: for the cleansing]. choreo "hold"; ana "each". metretes "measure": the unit is translated, not converted and not explained.
- hoi Ioudaioi: until Obay decides, "[alt: the Judeans]" is given once in a chapter, at the first occurrence (2:6), like the other standing alts.
- gemizo "fill" (leaving "make full" for pleroo); heos ano "up to the top". antleo "draw (out)", its object ("some") supplied in English. phero "carry", kept distinct from ago "bring".
- architriklinos "the master of the banquet" [alt: the head steward], the alt given once in a chapter. geuomai "taste". nymphios "bridegroom".
- An aside by the writer inside a sentence (2:9) stands in parentheses, as in 1:38.
- pas anthropos (2:10) "every human being", as in 1:9; ho anthropos for people as a class (2:25) "a human being". An indefinite third-person plural (2:10 methysthosin) "people".
- kalos (of wine) "fine", kept distinct from agathos "good". elasson "lesser", the pair of meizon "greater". methysko (passive) "become drunk", not softened. tithemi of wine "set out". sy (2:10, 20) "you yourself", as an emphatic subject.
- semeion "sign" (the same English as ot in Genesis); poiein semeion "do a sign". arche ton semeion "the beginning of the signs", arche as always; the second accusative is "as", as in 1 Jn 4:10.
- katabaino of a journey (2:12) "go down", the pair of anabaino "go up"; of what descends from heaven it stays "come down". meta touto "after this". meno of lodging stays "stay" [alt: remain], the alt given once in a chapter. ou pollas hemeras "not many days", left as the Greek puts it.
- pascha "Passover"; heorte "festival". engys "near". Kana "Cana"; Kapharnaoum "Capernaum"; Hierosolyma "Jerusalem".
- hieron "temple" (the whole sacred enclosure: 2:14, 15); naos "sanctuary" [alt: temple] (the house itself: 2:19-21), the alt given once in a chapter. Keep the two distinct: the saying about the body uses the second word.
- poleo "sell". bous "cattle"; probaton "sheep". kermatistes "coin changer" and kerma "coins" (2:14-15), keeping their echo; kollybistes "money changer". trapeza "table". phragellion "whip"; schoinion "cord".
- ekballo "drive out", the same English as garash (Gen 3:24); exo ballein stays "throw out". ekcheo "pour out". anatrepo "overturn". enteuthen "from here".
- 2:15 pantas ... ta te probata kai tous boas: "them all ..., both the sheep and the cattle" [alt: along with the sheep and the cattle]: "all" may be the animals alone, or the people with the animals.
- oikos "house"; ho oikos tou patros mou "my Father's house"; oikos emporiou "a house of trade".
- mimneskomai "remember". gegrammenon estin "it is written". zelos "zeal"; ho zelos tou oikou sou (2:17) "zeal for your house", since the genitive can only be the object here. katesthio "devour", kept distinct from "eat".
- deiknymi "show". hoti after a question (2:18) "since".
- lyo of a building (2:19) "destroy", the listed alt of 1 Jn 3:8, because "undo" cannot be said of a building. egeiro "raise", of a building and of the dead alike, so that the double sense survives. egerthe ek nekron (2:22) "was raised from the dead" [alt: rose from the dead]. nekros "dead".
- oikodomeo "build"; 2:20 "was built in forty-six years" [alt: has been under construction for forty-six years]. soma "body". graphe "scripture", lowercase like "law".
- lego peri with no object (2:21) "speak concerning" (it shares its English with laleo); elsewhere lego stays "say" (2:22 "the word that Jesus had said").
- pisteuein heauton with the dative (2:24) "entrust oneself to"; its echo with pisteuo "believe" (2:23) is explained in one bracketed note, set at the end of the sentence (2:25). pantas (2:24, masculine) "everyone".
- For Obay to decide (each is easy to reverse): "What do I have to do with you, woman?" or "What is that to me and to you, woman?" as the main text at 2:4; "sanctuary" for naos beside "temple" for hieron, or "temple" for both; "fine" or "good" for kalos; "measures" left unconverted at 2:6; whether to note "something like a whip" at 2:15; "a human being" at 2:25, which loses the link to "a man" in 3:1.

## Settled in John 3
- Greek text, applying the 1 John 1 line: the other reading is noted at 3:13 (the earliest manuscripts end at "the Son of Man"; many add ho on en to ourano "who is in heaven") and 3:25 (meta Ioudaiou "with a Jew"; one of the earliest papyri and many others read meta Ioudaion "with Jews"). 3:15 reads en auto, as in the editions; the earliest manuscripts divide between en auto, ep' auto and eis auton, and the alt there covers them. Not noted, since the sense is intact: 3:2 (many read "to Jesus" for "to him"), 3:15 (many add "should not perish but" from 3:16), 3:16 and 3:17 (many read "his only Son", "his Son"), 3:31 (the second "is above all", over which the earliest divide, is kept, as in the editions), 3:32 (many open with kai), 3:34 (many add ho theos as the subject of "gives"; "he" is left as open as in the Greek, as at 1 Jn 5:16).
- anthropos stays as in John 1 and 2: of one particular man (3:1) "man"; for people as a class (3:4, 27) "a human being"; hoi anthropoi (3:19) "human beings". So the link between 2:25 and 3:1 is still not visible (see the John 2 line for Obay).
- archon "ruler". Nikodemos onoma auto "his name was Nicodemus", as in 1:6. nyktos "at night".
- ean me "unless"; ean me tis "unless someone ..., he". dynamai "can"; ou dynatai "cannot".
- anothen "from above" [alt: again], the alt given once in a chapter (3:3). The word has both senses; Nicodemus answers with deuteron "a second time", and at 3:31 it can only be "from above".
- basileia "kingdom"; he basileia tou theou "the kingdom of God". eiserchomai eis "enter" (the English verb needs no "into"). koilia "womb". geron on "when he is old".
- me opening a question that expects the answer no (3:4): a statement with a tag, "He cannot ..., can he?"
- gennao passive stays "be born" all through 3:3-8; to gegennemenon "what has been born"; ex hydatos kai pneumatos (3:5) "from water and spirit", without articles as in the Greek; ek tou pneumatos (3:6, 8) "from the spirit". dei "must".
- pneuma at its first occurrence in 3:8 "wind" [alt: spirit], with pneo "blow" and phone "sound" [alt: voice]; one bracketed note says that wind and spirit are the same word. phone elsewhere stays "voice" (3:29).
- houtos (the adverb) "in this way", as in 1 Jn 4:11: houtos estin (3:8) "it is this way with"; kathos ... houtos (3:14) "just as ..., in this way" (kathos ... kai stays "just as ..., so"); houtos ... hoste (3:16) "in this way [alt: so much], that".
- A plural "you" spoken to one person, continued (3:7, 11, 12): "you all", once in a sentence, and placed where it cannot be misread (3:12 "said ... to you all", not "told you all the ...").
- lambanein ten martyrian "receive the testimony". martyreo with an accusative (3:11, 32) and with a dative of the person (3:26) "testify to"; moi martyreite (3:28) "testify for me", since "testify to me" would say something else in English.
- ta epigeia / ta epourania "the earthly things / the heavenly things". An aorist beside a present (3:12 eipon ... ou pisteuete) is put in the English perfect, as in 1 Jn 2:11. ei me (3:13) "except". eis ton ouranon "into heaven".
- hypsoo "lift up" (its other sense, "exalt", is not noted). ophis "snake", the same English as nachash.
- 3:15 ho pisteuon en auto: en auto is read with "have" ("may have eternal life in him"), since "believe in" in John is always pisteuo eis; the reading with "believes" is the alt.
- hina after a past tense "so that ... might" (3:16, 17); after a present "so that ... may" (3:15, 20, 21), as in John 1 and 1 John.
- ton huion ton monogene (3:16) "the only Son" [alt: the only-born Son], the alt given once in a chapter; 3:18 "the only Son of God".
- apollymi (middle) "perish". sozo "save". krino "judge", never "condemn" (which is kept for kataginosko), so that the echo with krisis "judgment" survives: ou krinetai "is not judged", kekritai "has been judged". ede "already".
- mallon ... e (3:19) "rather than" [alt: more than].
- phaulos "worthless", kept distinct from poneros "evil" in the verse before, and leaving "bad" for kakos. prasso "practice", kept distinct from poieo "do". elencho (3:20) "expose".
- ergazomai "work" (verb): estin eirgasmena (3:21) "have been worked", keeping the echo with erga "works". hoti in 3:21 "that" [alt: because], on the pattern of 1 Jn 2:19.
- meta tauta "after these things". he Ioudaia ge "the Judean land"; ge "land" (3:22) or "earth" (3:31), by context. diatribo "spend time". Ainon "Aenon"; Saleim "Salim". hydata polla (a plural) "much water", as with haimata. paraginomai "arrive", kept distinct from erchomai "come". ballo "throw"; phylake "prison".
- zetesis "debate"; ginomai of a debate (3:25) "arise"; ek ton matheton "from (John's) disciples". pantes of people (3:26) "everyone", as in 2:24.
- A relative clause left hanging and picked up by houtos (3:26) is marked with a dash.
- autoi hymeis (3:28) "You yourselves". emprosthen ekeinou (3:28) "ahead of that one", as in 1:15.
- nymphe "bride". philos "friend". histemi (perfect) "stand". chairo "rejoice"; chara chairei "rejoices with joy", keeping the cognate dative. dia with the accusative "because of". he chara he eme "this joy of mine".
- auxano "grow"; elattoo (passive) "become less", keeping the echo with elasson "lesser" (2:10).
- epano panton (3:31) "above all", left as open as the Greek (all people or all things).
- A perfect and an aorist under one relative (3:32 ho heoraken kai ekousen) "what he has seen and heard". touto picking up the relative clause: "to this he testifies".
- sphragizo "set one's seal" (3:33 "set his seal to this: that"). rhema "word" (it shares its English with logos; in John it is always plural). ek metrou "by measure". cheir "hand"; didonai en te cheiri "give into his hand".
- apeitheo "disobey" [alt: refuse to believe], kept distinct from me pisteuo "not believe". orge "anger" (plain English for what is often put as "wrath"). menei ep' auton "remains on him", as in 1:32.
- Quotation marks: the Greek does not mark where a speech ends. Where nothing signals a change of speaker, the speech runs on: Jesus' words close at 3:21 and John's at 3:36. A new paragraph inside a speech opens with a quotation mark.
- For Obay to decide (each is easy to reverse): where the two speeches end (3:21 and 3:36 here; many readers end them at 3:15 and 3:30 and take what follows as the writer's own words); "born from above" with "born again" as the alt, or the other way round; "wind" as the main text at 3:8; "in this way [alt: so much]" at 3:16; "judge" rather than "condemn" in 3:17-18; "worthless" for phaulos; "anger" or "wrath" for orge; "disobeys" or "does not believe" at 3:36; whether "who is in heaven" at 3:13 should be noted.

## Settled in John 4
- Greek text, applying the 1 John 1 line: the other reading is noted at 4:1 (ho Iesous, as in the editions; the earliest manuscripts divide, and most manuscripts read ho kyrios "the Lord"). 4:35-36: ede "Already" is read with what follows, as the editions punctuate it, and the verse break stays where they put it; the other division ("already white for harvest") is the alt, as at 1:3-4. Not noted, since the sense is intact: 4:9 (a few omit "For Jews do not have dealings with Samaritans"), 4:11 (the doubtful he gyne is kept), 4:16 (many add "Jesus"), 4:42 (many add "the Christ" after "the savior of the world"), 4:43 (many add "and went away"), 4:51 (many read "your boy" for "his boy").
- Samareia "Samaria"; Samarites / Samaritis "Samaritan" / "Samaritan woman"; Sychar "Sychar"; Iakob "Jacob"; he Ioudaia on its own "Judea"; Galilaioi "Galileans".
- pege "spring" (4:6, 14); phrear "well" (4:11, 12). Never interchange them: the writer and Jesus say "spring", the woman says "well".
- kopiao "become weary" (4:6) and "labor" (4:38); kopos "labor". One bracketed note (4:38) says that the two are the same verb. hodoiporia "journey".
- houtos (the adverb) at 4:6 stays "in this way" [alt: just as he was]. plesion "close to" (engys stays "near"). chorion "plot of land". legomenos "called" (it shares its English with kaleo).
- dos moi pein "Give me a drink"; pino "drink"; phago "eat". trophai (4:8, a plural) "provisions"; brosis and broma both "food" (two forms of one word, like skotia / skotos). agorazo "buy".
- Ioudaios in the singular "a Jew" [alt: a Judean]: the standing alt is given at the first occurrence in the chapter, whatever the number (4:9). Ioudaioi without the article "Jews".
- sy carrying a participle (4:9 "you, being a Jew") is not marked further; the other emphatic subject pronouns are marked as before (4:10, 14, 20, 22, 32, 35, 38, 42, 44, 45, 53).
- synchraomai "have dealings with" [alt: use things in common with]. A sentence with gar that explains the story (4:8, 9) is the writer's: it stands outside the quotation marks, without parentheses, as at 3:24. An aside inside a sentence (4:2, 25) stands in parentheses.
- dorea "gift". hydor zon "living water" [alt: flowing water], the alt given once in a chapter: the phrase is also the ordinary one for spring water.
- kyrie as an address from someone who does not know who Jesus is (4:11, 15, 19, 49) "Sir" [alt: Lord], the alt given once in a chapter.
- antlema "something to draw with", keeping the echo with antleo "draw". bathys "deep". thremmata "livestock" (the same English as behemah).
- me, meti and me tis opening a question (4:12, 29, 33): a statement with a tag, as at 3:4.
- dipsao "be thirsty". ou me with eis ton aiona (4:14) "will never"; ou me on its own (4:48) "will certainly not". hallomai "leap up". eis zoen aionion (4:14, 36) "into eternal life".
- dierchomai dia (4:4) "pass through"; dierchomai enthade (4:15) "come all the way here". enthade "here". aphiemi of a place, a thing or a fever (4:3, 28, 52) "leave". aperchomai "go away". poreuomai "go on one's way", kept distinct from hypago "go".
- aner of a woman's man (4:16-18) "husband". kalos (the adverb) "well". 4:17: the woman's words come back in a different order, and the English keeps it ("A husband I do not have"). touto alethes eirekas (4:18) "this is a true thing you have said", keeping the adjective apart from alethos "truly".
- prophetes "prophet". oros "mountain". topos "place". proskyneo "worship"; proskynetes "worshiper". soteria "salvation", beside sozo "save" and soter "savior".
- A plural "you" spoken to one person, continued: hymeis (4:20, 22) "you yourselves" already shows the number; a plural verb alone (4:21, 48) "you all".
- erchetai hora "an hour is coming", without the article; kai nyn estin "and it is now". pneuma ho theos "God is spirit"; en pneumati kai aletheia "in spirit and truth", lowercase and without articles. kai gar "for indeed". toioutous ... tous proskynountas auton (4:23) "such people to be those who worship him".
- Messias (4:25) is kept, as at 1:41, with ho legomenos christos in parentheses and the same bracketed note.
- ego eimi with no predicate (4:26) "I am he" [alt: I am].
- epi touto "at this". mentoi "yet". hoi anthropoi of the people of a town (4:28) "the people"; anthropos of one man (4:29, 50) "man", so the link between 4:28 and 4:29 is not visible, as at 2:25-3:1. deute "Come". panta hosa and panta ha "all the things that".
- en to metaxy "in the meantime". erotao of a request stays "make a request": of a person "make a request of" (4:31, 40), with hina "make a request that ... should" (4:47). phero with a dative (4:33) stays "carry"; lego with a dative and an object (4:29, 39) stays "say to", as at 3:12.
- emon broma estin hina (4:34) "My food is that I should", as with hina after a noun in 1 Jn 3:11.
- therismos "harvest"; therizo "harvest" (verb), so that the echo survives; speiro "sow". tetramenos "four months". epairo "lift" (hypsoo stays "lift up"). chora in the plural "fields". leukos "white"; pros therismon "for harvest". misthos "wages". synago "gather". karpos "fruit". homou "together". allos ... allos "one ... another".
- logos stays "word" where it is a saying (4:37) and what a person has said (4:39, 41, 50); lalia (4:42) "speaking", keeping the echo with laleo "speak". eiserchomai eis with an abstract noun (4:38) "enter into".
- pollo pleious "many more". ouketi "no longer". ekeithen "from there". patris "homeland" [alt: hometown]. time "honor". dechomai "welcome", kept distinct from lambano "receive" and paralambano "take in".
- basilikos "royal official". astheneo "be sick". iaomai "heal". mello "be about to". apothnesko "die". teras "wonder". huios "son", paidion "young child" and pais "boy" (4:46-53) are kept distinct. doulos "slave". hypantao "meet". pynthanomai "inquire". kompsoteron echein "get better". echthes "yesterday". pyretos "fever". oikia "household" (oikos stays "house").
- katabaino of a journey: "go down" or "come down", by where the speaker stands (4:47, 49, 51).
- Reported speech after a past tense is put back one tense in English (4:1 "was making", 4:47 "had come", 4:51 "was living").
- For Obay to decide (each is easy to reverse): "Jesus" or "the Lord" as the main text at 4:1, where the earliest manuscripts divide; "spring" for pege beside "well" for phrear, which leaves no "Jacob's well" at 4:6; "Sir" or "Lord" for kyrie from the woman and the official; "I am he" or "I am" at 4:26; whether "living water" needs its alt; "harvest" or "reap" for therizo; "in this way" or "just as he was" at 4:6; "have dealings with" or "use things in common with" at 4:9; "homeland" or "hometown" at 4:44; "slaves" for douloi at 4:51; "provisions" at 4:8; and whether "carried" (4:33) and "said to me" (4:29, 39) should give way to "brought" and "told".

## Settled in John 5
- Greek text, applying the 1 John 1 line: 5:3b-4 (the sick "waiting for the movement of the water", and the angel who stirred it) is not in the earliest manuscripts and is not in the main text. Standard English numbering counts verse 4, so its number is kept: the words stand as an [alt: ..., as other manuscripts read] at the end of verse 3 and as the whole of verse 4, translated from the form most manuscripts have (with no "of the Lord" after "angel"), and the chapter's `numbering:` line says so once.
- Other readings noted: 5:1 (heorte "a festival"; other manuscripts, one of the earliest among them, read he heorte "the festival"), 5:2 (Bethzatha, as in the standard editions; many manuscripts read Bethesda, and the earliest papyri Bethsaida) and 5:44 (tou monou theou "the only God", as in the editions; the earliest papyri leave out "God"). Not noted, since the sense is intact: 5:3 (many add "great" to "multitude"), 5:5 (the doubtful kai in "thirty-eight" is not marked), 5:10 ("your mat"; many read "the mat"), 5:12 (many open with oun and add "your mat"), 5:15 (some read "said" for "declared"), 5:16 (many add "and were seeking to kill him" from 5:18), 5:17 (the doubtful Iesous is kept), 5:30 (many add "the Father" after "the one who sent me"), 5:36 (meizo / meizon, both "greater"). Also not noted: 5:32, where a few read "you know" for "I know".
- 5:2: epi te probatike is read with "gate" understood and kolymbethra as the subject ("by the Sheep Gate, a pool"); the other reading, with kolymbethra in the dative ("by the Sheep Pool, a place"), is the alt. kolymbethra "pool". epilegomenos "called", like legomenos. Hebraisti "in Hebrew", translated as the Greek has it. stoa "colonnade".
- katakeimai "lie" (5:3) / "lie down" (5:6), of the sick. plethos "multitude", kept distinct from ochlos "crowd". typhlos "blind"; cholos "lame"; xeros "withered". astheneia "sickness", beside astheneo "be sick". The three adjectives in 5:3 have no article and are set off with a dash, as kinds of "those who were sick".
- In the longer reading (5:3b-4): ekdechomai "wait for"; kinesis "movement"; kata kairon "from time to time"; embaino "step in"; tarache "stirring"; nosema "disease"; katechomai "be held"; the imperfects are "would go down", "would become".
- echein with a length of time (5:5, 6) "be ... (for)": "had been in his sickness for thirty-eight years"; in 5:6 the Greek leaves the condition unstated, and English supplies "that way". After a participle and a historical present (5:6) reported speech is not put back a tense ("has already been").
- hygies "healthy": hygies ginesthai "become healthy"; poiein hygie "make healthy". "Well" is left for the adverb kalos.
- therapeuo "cure", kept distinct from iaomai "heal" (5:10, 13).
- anthropon ouk echo hina (5:7) "I do not have a man to", as with chreian echein hina (2:25). ballo of a person into the pool (5:7) stays "throw" [alt: put]. tarasso of water "stir". en ho "while". pro emou "before me".
- egeire (5:8; the imperative, of getting up) "Rise", keeping the echo with egeiro "raise" (5:21). airo of a mat "take up", beside "take away" (1:29; 2:16); where it has no object (5:12), "it" is supplied. krabattos "mat".
- eutheos "immediately". sabbaton "Sabbath", capitalized like "Passover"; without the article "a Sabbath". exestin "it is permitted".
- ekneuo (5:13) "slip away". ochlos "crowd". A genitive absolute (5:13) "as there was", left as open as the Greek (time or cause). meketi "no longer", like ouketi. cheiron "worse". ginomai with a dative of the person (5:14) "happen to".
- dioko "persecute". apokteino "kill" (the same English as harag). mallon on its own (5:18) "all the more". isos "equal". legein with two accusatives (5:18) "call".
- apekrinato (5:17, 19; the middle form) "answered", the same English as apekrithe.
- lyo of the Sabbath (5:18) "undo", the listed rendering [alt: break]: the verb can mean doing away with the Sabbath or breaking it.
- aph' heautou / ap' emautou (5:19, 30) "from himself / from myself" [alt: on his own], the alt given once in a chapter. ou dynatai ... ouden "can do nothing". ean me ti (5:19) "unless it is something". ha an "whatever". homoios (the adverb) "likewise", beside homoios "like".
- phileo "hold dear" [alt: love], the alt given once in a chapter, kept distinct from agapao "love" (3:35).
- hosper "just as", the same English as kathos (English has no third plain word beside "as"); hosper ... houtos kai (5:21, 26) "just as ..., in this way ... also". zoopoieo "make alive", kept distinct from "give life"; its object ("them") is supplied in 5:21.
- oude gar (5:22) "For neither". he krisis pasa "all judgment". timao "honor" (verb), beside time "honor". krisin poiein (5:27) "do judgment", like "do the truth". exousia (5:27) "authority", with no alt.
- hoti after amen amen lego hymin (5:24, 25) is not translated, as at 3:11.
- An aorist participle before a future verb (5:25, 29) is put in the English perfect: "those who have heard", "those who have done".
- huios anthropou without the articles (5:27) "Son of Man" [alt: a son of man], with no article in English, like "king of Israel" (1:49).
- hoti after "Do not be surprised at this" (5:28) "because" [alt: that]. mnemeion "tomb". ekporeuomai "come out". anastasis "resurrection"; eis anastasin "into a resurrection", without the article as in the Greek. ta agatha / ta phaula (5:29) "good things / worthless things", without the article in English, as at 3:20.
- he krisis he eme, to thelema to emon (5:30) "my judgment", "my will": emos is "my", as at 4:34, leaving "his own" for idios (5:18, 43).
- allos estin ho martyron (5:32) "another is the one who testifies"; estin ho kategoron (5:45) "there is one who accuses".
- lychnos "lamp"; kaio "burn". agalliao "exult", kept distinct from chairo "rejoice". pros horan "for an hour", hora as always.
- ten martyrian meizo tou Ioannou (5:36) "the testimony that I have is greater than John's": the adjective is a predicate, and "John" stands for John's testimony. auta ta erga "the works themselves".
- eidos "form". oute ... oute "neither ..., nor".
- eraunate (5:39), which can be a statement or a command: "You search" [alt: Search]. eraunao "search". dokeo "think". ekeinai eisin hai martyrousai "those are the ones that testify".
- 5:44: a participle is followed by a finite verb inside the question, and the English keeps the shift ("receiving ..., and you do not seek").
- kategoreo "accuse"; pros ton patera (5:45) "to the Father". elpizo eis (perfect) "have hoped in". gramma "writing" (plural "writings"), keeping the echo with grapho "write".
- For Obay to decide (each is easy to reverse): how 5:3b-4 is shown (verse 4 as an alt-only line with a `numbering:` note, or the words in the main text, or no note at all); "Bethzatha" or the familiar "Bethesda" as the main text at 5:2; whether "the festival" (5:1) and "the only one" (5:44) need their notes; "holds dear" or "loves" for phileo; "healthy" or "well" for hygies; "undoing the Sabbath" or "breaking the Sabbath" as the main text at 5:18; "Rise, take up your mat" or "Get up, pick up your mat"; "throw" or "put" at 5:7; "from himself" or "on his own"; "be surprised" for thaumazo at 5:20 and 5:28, where "be amazed" would be stronger; "make alive" or "give life"; "do judgment" or "carry out judgment"; "Son of Man" or "a son of man" at 5:27; "You search" or "Search" at 5:39; "exult" for agalliao; "multitude" for plethos; "cure" beside "heal".
